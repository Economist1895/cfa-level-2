Analysis of Active Portfolio Management

In 30 seconds

  • Value added = portfolio return − benchmark return = Σ active weight × return. Active weights sum to zero.
  • Sharpe ratio (absolute) is unchanged by cash/leverage. Information ratio (relative) is unchanged by scaling active weights, but shrinks if you add cash.
  • SRP2​=SRB2​+IR2: the manager with the highest IR gives the highest Sharpe ratio. Optimal active risk = (IR/SRB) × σB.
  • Fundamental law: E(RA) = TC × IC × √BR × σA, i.e. skill × implementation × breadth × aggressiveness.
  • Limits: IC is uncertain and overestimated; breadth is overstated when decisions are correlated across assets or over time.

01Value added & benchmarks

  • Good benchmark: representative of the investable assets, replicable at low cost, weights verifiable ex ante and returns timely ex post.
  • With a float-adjusted market benchmark, active management is a zero-sum game before costs. Not zero-sum for narrower benchmarks.

02Sharpe ratio vs information ratio

Absolute reward per unit of total risk. Annualise: mean × 12, σ × √12. Unaffected by cash or leverage: SRC​=wP​σP​wP​(RP​−RF​)​=SRP​.

  • Two-fund separation: hold the max-Sharpe risky portfolio + cash; tune total risk with cash/leverage.
  • Tune active risk with the benchmark: e.g. 80% fund + 20% benchmark → σA × 0.8. To raise it, short the benchmark (or hold less of it).
  • Closet indexer: SR ≈ benchmark SR, tiny active risk, IR ≈ 0 or negative after fees. Detect with active share = ½ Σ|Δwi|.
  • Market-neutral long–short fund with a cash benchmark: SR = IR.
  • Ranking by active risk ≠ ranking by total risk.

03Optimal active risk

F5

Sharpe ratio of the best combination

SRP2​=SRB2​+IR2

Holds when active risk is set optimally. Highest IR ⇒ highest SR, so expected IR is the single best criterion for choosing among managers with the same benchmark. Squaring loses the sign, so don't use it with negative IRs.

F6

Optimal active risk (unconstrained)

σA∗​=SRB​IR​σB​

Multiply by β if the fund's beta ≠ 1. Total risk: σP2​=σB2​+σA2​. Weight on the active fund = σA∗​/σA,fund​; the benchmark gets the rest (negative ⇒ short). IR → 0 ⇒ σA* → 0 (just hold the benchmark).

F7

With constraints (TC < 1)

σA∗​=TCSRB​IR∗​σB​SRP2​=SRB2​+TC2(IR∗)2​

IR∗ = unconstrained IR. Constraints lower TC → lower optimal active risk and a smaller SR gain.

04The fundamental law

Correlation triangle

  • IC (signal quality) = corr(forecast μi, realised active return). Ex ante must be > 0; usually < 0.20.
  • TC (implementation) = corr(forecast μi, actual active weights). 1 if unconstrained; typically 0.2–0.9.
  • Value added = corr(active weights, realised active returns).
  • BR (breadth) = number of independent decisions per year.

F10

Fundamental law of active management

E(RA​)=TC⋅IC⋅BR​⋅σA​IR=TC⋅IC⋅BR​​

Basic (unconstrained) law: TC = 1, so IR∗=ICBR​. Elements: skill (IC), structure (TC), breadth (BR), aggressiveness (σA). The last three may be set by policy or regulation.

F11

Ex post: realised IC + noise

E(RA​∣ICR​)=TC⋅ICR​⋅BR​⋅σA​RA​=E(RA​∣ICR​)+Noise​

Share of active-return variance from forecasting = TC²; from constraint-induced noise = 1 − TC². Low-TC managers can lose with good forecasts or win with bad ones.

05Applying the law

  • Security selection vs sector/country timing: low IC × large BR can beat high IC × small BR (e.g. 0.05√100 = 0.50 > 0.15√9 = 0.45).
  • Constrained and more aggressive: constrained weights drift further from optimal → TC falls → IR falls. Active return rises less than proportionally.
  • Market timing (binary ±1 calls): IC = 2 × (% correct) − 1 (55% right → IC = 0.10). BR = calls per year (quarterly → 4).
  • Rebalancing more often raises BR only if the decisions are truly independent over time and IC and TC hold up (turnover limits lower TC).
  • Correlated assets (same sector or region, a duration call across all maturities) → BR < N. "Both correlated assets outperform" is a less ambitious forecast → lower effective IC.
  • Scores matter: a forecast that is effectively one decision (rates up → short long bonds, long short bonds) has little breadth.

06Practical limitations

  • Inherits mean–variance limits; ignores transaction costs and taxes.
  • Ex ante skill is hard to measure; managers overestimate IC; IC varies over time and by segment.
F12

Uncertain IC (strategy risk)

σA​=σIC​N​σRM​E(RA​)=σIC​IC​σA​​

Realised active risk exceeds the risk-model estimate σRM. Actual IRs come out well below the basic law's prediction.

F13

Effective breadth

BR=1+(N−1)ρN​

ρ = common correlation of active returns. ρ > 0 → BR < N. Near-arbitrage pairs (ρ strongly negative) → BR can far exceed N.

  • Time-series dependence: slow-moving signals (e.g. earnings yield) repeat the same call monthly, so 12 × N overstates BR.
  • Fixed income: bond returns are driven by a few factors (duration, credit), so many bonds ≠ many independent decisions.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Active return ≠ alpha: RA​=RP​−RB​; α=RP​−βRB​.
  • Allocation effect: Δw × benchmark return. Selection effect: portfolio weight × sleeve value added. Don't swap them.
  • Fund matching the policy weights → allocation value added = 0.
  • "Most consistent active return" / "best skill" → highest IR, not highest active return or SR.
  • Adding cash: SR unchanged, IR shrinks. Mixing with the benchmark: IR unchanged.
  • Optimal weight on the active fund = σA*/fund σA. If > 1, short the benchmark.
  • Constraints (sector caps, long-only, turnover) → TC ↓ → IR ↓ and optimal active risk ↓. More stocks or more frequent rebalancing → BR ↑.
  • Relaxing constraints raises TC, but if IC drops, IR can still fall.
  • Solve for breadth: BR=(IR/IC)2. Use risk-weighted correlations for IC and TC.
  • Claims like IC 0.05 × √(12 × 500) are overstated: correlated stocks, persistent signals, uncertain IC.
