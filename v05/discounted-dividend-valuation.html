Discounted Dividend Valuation

In 30 seconds

  • Value = PV of all future dividends. Use a DDM for dividend payers with a policy linked to profits, from a non-control view.
  • Gordon: V0​=D1​/(r−g). Use D₁, not D₀, and r > g. Very sensitive to r and g.
  • Multistage (two-stage, H-model, three-stage): high growth first, then Gordon for the terminal value.
  • PVGO = price − E₁/r. It is the part of value from future profitable growth.
  • Sustainable g = b × ROE; expand ROE with DuPont (PRAT).

01Which cash flow to discount?

ModelMost suitable when…
DDMPays dividends (there is a record) · policy has an understandable, consistent link to profitability · non-control perspective
FCFF / FCFENo dividends · dividends ≫ or ≪ FCFE · FCF aligns with profitability within the forecast horizon · control perspective (takeover candidate)
Residual incomeNo dividends (alternative to FCF) · FCF negative within the forecast horizon
  • DDM can value a non-payer in theory (stage-1 dividends = 0), but timing and size of the first dividend are hard to forecast, so FCF or RI is usually preferred.
  • Share repurchases don't break the DDM: adjust the per-share dividend growth rate for them. Buybacks are less predictable than dividends and are wealth-neutral at market prices.

02The dividend discount model

F1

Single holding period

V0​=1+rD1​+P1​​r=P0​D1​​+P0​P1​−P0​​​

Expected holding-period return = dividend yield + price appreciation (assumes V0​=P0​).

F2

Multiple periods & general DDM

V0​=t=1∑n​(1+r)tDt​​+(1+r)nPn​​V0​=t=1∑∞​(1+r)tDt​​​

Even a finite-horizon investor depends on all future dividends, because they set Pn​. Forecast either with stylised growth patterns or individually for 3–10 years plus a terminal value.

03Gordon growth model

F3

Constant growth

V0​=r−gD0​(1+g)​=r−gD1​​

Assumes dividends grow at g forever and r > g. Works with g = 0 or negative g (e.g. depleting mine: r−g=r+∣g∣).

F4

Perpetual preferred stock

V0​=rD​

Non-callable, fixed-rate. D = coupon rate × par. Rearranged, the capitalisation rate is r=D/P0​.

F5

Implied required return & implied growth

r=P0​D1​​+gg=P0​+D0​rP0​−D0​​​

Return = dividend yield + capital gains yield (g). Implied g above your forecast or the sustainable g → stock overvalued. Don't feed the implied r back into a DDM (circular).

💡
In the Gordon model, g is everything: dividends, earnings (constant payout) and value all grow at g. If price = value, the dividend yield and capital gains yield stay constant, and total return = r.
  • Best for: stable dividend payers growing at or below nominal GDP growth (real GDP growth + inflation); broad developed-market indexes; the final stage of multistage models.
  • Growth well above GDP → use a multistage model.

04PVGO & justified P/E

F6

Present value of growth opportunities

V0​=rE1​​+PVGOE1​P0​​=r1​+E1​PVGO​​
E1​/r
no-growth value (assets in place; all earnings paid out)
1/r
no-growth P/E; PVGO/E1​ is the growth component of the leading P/E

Growth adds value only if new investments earn more than r (ROE = r → NPV 0 → PVGO 0). PVGO also includes real options (timing, scale, abandonment). Negative PVGO → market expects value-destroying investment, or E₁ is too high.

F7

Justified P/E (Gordon)

Leading: E1​P0​​=r−g1−b​Trailing: E0​P0​​=r−g(1−b)(1+g)​​

1−b = payout ratio. Use the expected future payout, not the most recent one. Justified P/E vs actual P/E → under- or overvalued.

PhaseFeatures
GrowthSupernormal EPS growth, high margins, negative FCFE, low / zero payout
TransitionGrowth slows toward the economy's rate; capex falls; FCF turns positive; payout rises
MatureROE → r; growth, payout and ROE stable → Gordon
F8

Two-stage DDM

V0​=t=1∑n​(1+r)tD0​(1+gS​)t​+(1+r)nVn​​Vn​=r−gL​D0​(1+gS​)n(1+gL​)​​

Growth drops abruptly from gS​ to gL​. Vn​ = PV at time n of dividends from n + 1 on, so it uses Dn+1​. Alternatively Vn​ = P/E × En​, with En​=Dn​/payout.

F9

H-model

V0​=r−gL​D0​(1+gL​)+D0​H(gS​−gL​)​
H
half the length of the high-growth period (10-year fade → H = 5)

Growth declines linearly from gS​ to gL​ over 2H years. First term = Gordon value at gL​; second term = extra value from supernormal growth. An approximation. Uses D₀.

F10

H-model implied return

r=P0​D0​​[(1+gL​)+H(gS​−gL​)]+gL​

Reduces to Gordon when gS​=gL​. For two- and three-stage or spreadsheet models, find r as the IRR by trial and error.

  • Non-payer: if the first dividend is Dn+1​, then Vn​=Dn+1​/(r−g) and discount it back n years (not n + 1).
  • Three-stage, version 1: three distinct constant rates (e.g. 2 years at 27%, 10 years at 12%, then mature).
  • Three-stage, version 2: stage 2 growth declines linearly, so stages 2 + 3 form an H-model (e.g. patent expiry, then a gradual fade).
  • Spreadsheet models handle any dividend pattern and make sensitivity analysis easy.
  • Limitation: the terminal stage is often > 75% of value and very sensitive to r and gL​. A longer high-growth stage → higher value and a smaller terminal share.

06Sustainable growth & PRAT

F11

Sustainable growth rate

g=b×ROE

Growth funded by retained earnings only: no new equity, constant capital structure. Strictly, use the return on new investment and ROE on beginning equity.

F12

PRAT (DuPont) decomposition

g=​NINI−Div​×SalesNI​×TASales​×EquityTA​​

Profit margin × Retention × Asset turnover × financial leverage (TA/equity). P and A drive ROA; R and T are financial policy. For terminal g, use mature-phase forecasts (e.g. industry-median margin and turnover), not today's abnormal ROE.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Gordon numerator is D₁ = D₀(1 + g). The H-model uses D₀ in both terms.
  • Terminal value Vn​ is the PV of dividends after year n. Discount it n years, and add it to the year-n dividend, not instead of it.
  • H = half the high-growth period. A 4-year linear fade means H = 2.
  • H-model vs two-stage with the same gS​ and gL​: linear decline → lower dividends and a lower terminal value than an abrupt drop.
  • Fairly valued Gordon stock: dividend yield = r − g; price (capital gains yield) grows at g, not r.
  • Implied g (from price) > sustainable or forecast g → overvalued.
  • Largest PVGO ≠ largest growth share of P/E. Compare PVGO/E1​ for the P/E component.
  • Takeover candidate → FCF is appropriate (control perspective). Suspended dividends don't make the DDM unusable.
  • "When can Gordon be used?" Long-run growth above r → no. Earnings growth above GDP growth (e.g. 4.5% vs 2.75%) → no. Predictable buybacks → yes (adjust g).
  • Payout given as a range (e.g. 0–10%) → retention is 90–100%. Sustainable g = ROE × retention, not × payout.
