Equity Valuation

Equity Valuation: Applications and Processes

In 30 seconds

  • Active investing assumes price can differ from intrinsic value. Your estimate − price = true mispricing + your estimation error.
  • Pick the value definition that fits: going concern vs liquidation, fair market value vs investment value (one buyer's synergies).
  • 5 steps: understand the business → forecast → select model → convert forecasts to value → apply the conclusion.
  • Absolute models (DCF, asset-based) give intrinsic value. Relative models (multiples) only say cheap or dear versus a benchmark.
  • Model choice: fit the company's characteristics, the data available and the purpose / perspective.

01What is value?

Winning requires expectations that differ from consensus and are, on average, correct.

  • Intrinsic value: value given a hypothetically complete understanding of the asset. Relevant concept for public equities.
  • Rational efficient markets (Grossman–Stiglitz): investors only pay for information if they expect higher gross returns, so prices can't be perfectly efficient.
  • Catalyst: an event that makes the market re-evaluate the stock. Needed because price may not converge to value within your horizon.
ConceptMeaning
Going-concernFirm keeps operating for the foreseeable future. Default for healthy firms.
LiquidationFirm dissolved, assets sold individually. Relevant for distressed firms. Orderly liquidation > forced sale.
Fair market valueWilling, informed buyer and seller, neither compelled. Used for tax and buy–sell agreements.
Investment valueValue to a specific buyer, including its synergies and requirements.

Fair value (financial reporting) is related to, but not identical to, fair market value: arm's-length exchange between knowledgeable, willing parties.

02Applications & the valuation process

What valuation is used for

  • Stock selection (primary use): fairly priced, over- or underpriced vs value and vs comparables?
  • Inferring market expectations: fix every input except one fundamental, solve for the value that makes model value = price, then compare with your own view. Implied growth > your forecast → overvalued.
  • Evaluating corporate events (M&A, divestitures, spin-offs, going private) · fairness opinions · business strategies · communicating with shareholders · appraising private businesses · share-based payment.

Five-step process

  • 1. Understand the business: industry, competitive position, strategy, financial reports.
  • 2. Forecast performance: sales, earnings, dividends, financial position.
  • 3. Select the model.
  • 4. Convert forecasts to value: sensitivity analysis + situational adjustments.
  • 5. Apply the conclusion: recommendation, transaction opinion, strategic decision.

03Understanding the business

Three industry & competitive questions

  • How attractive is the industry (sustained profitability)?
  • What is the firm's competitive position and strategy? (cost leadership · differentiation · focus)
  • How well has it executed, and what are its prospects?

💡
Regression to the mean: don't extrapolate past results. High profits attract competitors; weak firms get restructured. IP and franchise agreements expire. Long-run growth converges toward the economy's growth.

Quality of earnings

  • Non-recurring gains (litigation wins, one-off tax credits, gains on non-operating asset sales) → reported earnings biased upward vs sustainable earnings.
  • Start by comparing net income with operating cash flow. The cash component is more persistent than accruals. High accruals → lower future ROA → lower earnings quality.
  • Red flags: early revenue (bill-and-hold, before installation), non-operating gains classed as operating, reserve manipulation, capitalising costs, long useful lives, high pension return or discount-rate assumptions, off-balance-sheet financing, bank overdraft increases shown as CFO.
  • Qualitative factors matter too: ownership structure, IP, licences, contingent liabilities, management integrity, accounting transparency.

  • "Low P/E vs peers" = relatively undervalued. If the whole peer group is overvalued, the stock may be too.
  • Two analysts can both be right: overvalued vs industry peers, undervalued vs the broad market.
  • Pairs trading (relative-value investing): buy the undervalued stock, short the overvalued one in the same industry, regardless of market direction.

Three model-selection criteria

  • Consistent with the company's characteristics: bank (marketable assets) → asset-based relative value works; service firm with intangibles → it doesn't.
  • Data availability and quality: no dividend history → not DDM; negative or volatile EPS → not P/E.
  • Purpose and perspective: a controlling buyer can redirect cash flows → use FCF, not dividends. Watch the report writer's biases.

Using more than one model adds insight.

05Sum-of-the-parts & situational adjustments

  • Sum-of-the-parts (breakup value, private market value): value each segment as an independent going concern, then adjust for corporate items (overhead, taxes, unallocated assets and liabilities).
  • Best when segments are in different industries with different growth / valuation characteristics. Also used to size value unlocked by a spin-off, split-off, tracking stock or carve-out.
  • Conglomerate discount: market discounts firms in multiple unrelated businesses. Explanations: inefficient internal capital markets; endogenous factors (weak firms diversify by acquisition); research measurement errors (the discount may not exist).
  • Breakup value > going-concern value → may prompt a divestiture or spin-off.

Converting forecasts to value

  • Sensitivity analysis: how value changes with growth, margins, discount rate, competitive response.
  • Control premium: controlling stake (usually > 50%) can redeploy assets and change capital structure → value ↑.
  • Lack of marketability discount: no public market (private shares).
  • Illiquidity discount: public shares with thin trading. Blockage factor: selling a block that is large vs trading volume (but not a controlling stake).

06Analyst role & research reports

  • Sell-side (brokerage): widely distributed reports. Buy-side (investment managers, trusts): report to a PM or committee. Also corporate analysts and independent vendors.
  • Analysts help clients (better decisions), capital markets (prices reflect value) and suppliers of capital (monitoring management).
  • Due diligence goes beyond public filings and conference calls: talk to management, employees, competitors; understand the industry and general economic conditions. Don't reveal insights by asking probing questions on public calls.

An effective report…

  • is timely, clear and incisive, objective and well researched, with key assumptions identified
  • separates fact from opinion; analysis, forecasts, valuation and recommendation are internally consistent
  • gives enough information for the reader to critique the valuation; states risk factors; discloses conflicts of interest
  • a target price needs its basis, time frame and the uncertainty of reaching it

Format: summary & investment conclusion → business summary → risks → valuation → historical / pro forma tables. Key Standards: I(B) independence, I(C) misrepresentation, V(A) diligence & reasonable basis, V(B) communication (fact vs opinion), V(C) record retention.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • The mispricing active managers try to capture is V − P (intrinsic − price), not VE − P or VE − V.
  • Distressed-securities fund → liquidation value. Healthy firm generating FCF → going-concern value.
  • Value to a buyer with synergies is investment value, not fair market value.
  • Many suppliers and many buyers are good for industry profitability. Low entry or switching costs are bad.
  • Firm not profitable, no dividend planned, likely takeover target → FCF model (not DDM, not P/E).
  • Mostly intangible assets + only occasional special dividends → FCFF, not asset-based or DDM.
  • Unrelated segments with different growth → sum-of-the-parts. Buying 70% of a listed firm → control premium (not marketability discount; the shares are listed).
  • Top-down means starting from macro / economic conditions. Extrapolating store-level sales per m² is bottom-up.
  • Discounting forecast FCF plus a terminal value is an absolute (PV) model.
