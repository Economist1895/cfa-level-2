Equity Valuation

Private Company Valuation

In 30 seconds

  • Same tools as public firms (DCF, multiples, assets), with three extra adjustments: numerator (normalize earnings), denominator (higher, built-up discount rate) and discounts/premiums (control, marketability).
  • Normalize for owner pay, personal expenses, non-operating assets, related-party deals and rent on owner-held property.
  • Cost of equity: expanded CAPM (β + size + company-specific) or build-up (implied β = 1, + industry premium).
  • DLOC from the control premium. DLOM from restricted stock, pre-IPO or put options. Combine them multiplicatively.
  • Market approach (GPCM, GTM, prior transactions) is the most used. EV multiples are preferred.

01Public vs private & why value

Factor typePrivate-company features
Company-specific (± for value)Early or late life-cycle stage, smaller size (higher r), owner = manager (fewer agency problems), limited disclosure, less access to debt
Stock-specific (usually −)Illiquidity (the main one), concentrated control (value can be shifted to controllers), share-sale restrictions
  • Risk/return range is wider than for public firms. Estimates diverge more by purpose and perspective.
  • Uses: transactions (VC, PE growth/buyout, debt raise, IPO, M&A, bankruptcy, share-based compensation), compliance (financial reporting e.g. impairment tests, tax), litigation (damages, shareholder disputes, divorce).
  • Standard of value matters: fair market value (reporting/tax) can differ from investment value to a buyer with synergies.
  • Statements may be audited, reviewed (limited assurance) or compiled (no opinion). The last two usually need adjustment.

F1

Base-year FCFF

FCFF​=EBITDA(1−t)+Dep⋅t−ΔLT assets−ΔWC​

Start from normalized EBITDA. FCFF suits a controlling buyer who sets financing and payouts. Equity = firm value − market value of debt (face value is OK if debt is small and operations stable).

Cash flow issues

  • Value depends on the interest being valued (small minority vs total equity) and the purpose.
  • Very uncertain futures (IPO / sale / bankruptcy, regulatory milestones) → scenario analysis: probability-weight terminal values (joint probabilities along the tree), then discount at WACC.
  • Management forecasts can be biased: overstated for goodwill-impairment tests, understated for option grants. Check that capital needs are included.

03Discount rates

  • Size premium: common in private valuations. Beware: tiny-cap public data may reflect distress that doesn't apply.
  • Debt access: less (and dearer) debt → more equity → higher WACC.
  • Acquisitions: use the target's risk and capital structure, not the buyer's lower cost of capital. Otherwise the buyer pays the seller for value the buyer brings.
  • Projection risk: less information or inexperienced forecasters → higher r (judgmental).
  • CAPM fit is weak: private owners are undiversified (they bear total risk, but beta prices only systematic risk), and comparables are hard to find.

Build-up has no beta (implied β = 1). It is used when there are no good public comparables. With β > 1 and IP = 0, build-up < expanded CAPM.

Unlever with the public comps' t and D/E. Relever with the private firm's t* and D/E*. A lower private D/E → a lower relevered beta than the comps'.

WACC=wd​rd​(1−t)+we​re​

For a sale, use optimal (achievable) weights, not the current ones. Too little debt → WACC above optimal (it tends to re as wd → 0). Large public peers' leverage may be more than a small private firm can carry.

F5

Discount for lack of control

DLOC=1−1+Control premium1​

Control lets you pick management, set payouts, buy or sell assets and raise finance. Supported where controllers take disproportionate returns. A DCF using control cash flows and the optimal capital structure is a control value. Otherwise it already reflects lack of control.

F6

DLOM via put option

DLOM≈Share priceATM put premium​

Strike = forward price S0​erT (at-the-money forward), with volatility from comparables. Pro: captures risk directly through volatility. Con: a put protects the price but doesn't give liquidity. Other sources: restricted stock blocks and pre-IPO sales.

F7

Total discount (multiplicative)

Total=1−(1−DLOC)(1−DLOM)

Apply in sequence: control → minority, then marketable → non-marketable. Never add the two.

  • DLOM is bigger with no liquidity event in sight, transfer restrictions, few buyers and no dividends. It is smaller near an IPO or sale.
  • Minority basis → DLOM typically applies too. Other discounts: key person, portfolio (mixed assets), non-voting shares.
  • If the base value already reflects one adjustment (e.g. already non-marketable), apply only what's missing.

05Valuation approaches

  • Income (absolute): DCF, capitalized cash flow, excess earnings. Market (relative): multiples. Asset-based (absolute): assets − liabilities at fair value. Used e.g. in bankruptcy to compare going-concern and liquidation value.
  • Multistage DCF is theoretically best when you can forecast. Terminal value: capitalized CF, excess earnings or a market multiple.
F8

Capitalized cash flow method (CCM)

Firm=WACC−gFCFF1​​Equity=re​−gFCFE1​​Firm=WACC−gEBIT1​(1−t)(1−RIR)​RIR=WACCg​​

Single-stage growing perpetuity. r − g = capitalization rate. Use when there are no projections and little market evidence (small, stable firms). Very sensitive to g. Use FCFF₁ = FCFF₀(1 + g). Assumes a constant capital structure.

F9

Excess earnings method (EEM)

RI=NInorm​−WC⋅rWC​−FA⋅rFA​RV=rRI​−gRI(1+g)​Firm=WC+FA+RV​

Required returns rise with risk: rWC < rFA < rRI. RV = value of all intangibles, i.e. residual income. Used for intangible asset valuation (financial reporting) and very small businesses. It is the least useful for whole businesses because it needs several subjective rates.

Market approach

MethodSource · issues
GPCM (guideline public co.)Trading multiples of similar public firms (match industry, size, leverage, life-cycle stage). Large data pool. Comparability and adjustments are subjective. Minority basis, so add a control premium if valuing control.
GTM (guideline transactions)Multiples from control acquisitions. Watch: synergies paid, contingent and stock consideration, few deals, stale dates.
Prior transactionPast deals in the subject company's own stock. Relevance fades as conditions change.
  • Market approach = most used, often seen as conceptually preferable. Key assumption: the comparables really are comparable.
  • Private valuations favour EV multiples (EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, EV/S) because capital structure may change. Equity = EV − debt.
  • Control premium sources: strategic deals > financial (synergies); sector "in play" means prices already hold some premium; stock-paid deals are less reliable.

Weight each segment's peer multiple by the firm's own share of the denominator (sales for EV/S, EBITDA for EV/EBITDA). A loss-making segment gets a negative EBITDA weight, so value it on sales instead. Then apply DLOC/DLOM to the public-equivalent value.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Owner paid via dividend instead of salary → add a market salary; normalized earnings go down.
  • Rent-free use of family property → add market rent (earnings ↓). Non-operating assets → remove opex and depreciation.
  • Owner/manager overlap means fewer agency problems. If owners don't manage, agency isn't a private-vs-public difference.
  • Same family control, one listed and one not → the gap is illiquidity.
  • DLOC ≠ control premium: 30% premium → DLOC = 1 − 1/1.3 = 23.1%, not 30%.
  • Total discount is multiplicative: 1 − (1 − 0.231)(1 − 0.20) = 38.5%, not 43–50%.
  • Selling a minority stake in a firm already valued on a non-marketable control basis → apply DLOC only. A financial buyer pays a financial (not synergistic) control premium.
  • Build-up: no β; add the industry premium. Expanded CAPM: β × ERP; no industry premium.
  • Pricing off the buyer's lower WACC transfers value to the seller. Use the target's (optimal) structure.
  • CCM numerator is next-year FCFF: FCFF₀ × (1 + g). DLOM put strike = forward price, not spot.
