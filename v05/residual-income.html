Residual Income Valuation

In 30 seconds

  • Residual income (RI) = net income minus a charge for the cost of equity. It is economic profit: accounting profit isn't enough, the firm must beat r.
  • Value = book value today + PV of future RI. ROE > r → worth more than book (P/B > 1).
  • Value is recognised earlier than in DDM/FCF, so the terminal value matters less.
  • Relies on clean surplus accounting. Watch for OCI, non-recurring items and intangibles.

Explicit sum runs to T − 1 only. Year T's RI sits inside the terminal term, and the discounting exponent is T − 1.

Continuing-RI assumptions (after the forecast horizon)

  • RI continues at a positive level forever (ω = 1; e.g. a perpetuity RIT​/r at time T)
  • RI = 0 from the terminal year (PT​=BT​)
  • RI declines to zero as ROE fades to r (0 < ω < 1)
  • RI declines to some mean level (e.g. ROE fades to the industry average)

05Strengths, weaknesses & when to use

Strengths

  • Terminal value is a smaller share of value
  • Uses readily available accounting data
  • Works with no dividends or negative near-term FCF
  • Works when cash flows are unpredictable
  • Focuses on economic profitability

Weaknesses

  • Accounting data can be manipulated
  • Data may need significant adjustments
  • Needs clean surplus to hold, or adjustments
  • Assumes interest expense reflects the cost of debt

No dividends, or unpredictable dividendsBig departures from clean surplus

06Accounting adjustments

Clean surplus violations (OCI)

  • Items that bypass the income statement: FVOCI / AFS unrealised gains and losses, FX translation adjustments, some pension adjustments, some hedging gains and losses, revaluation surplus (IFRS only), and own-credit changes on FV liabilities (IFRS only).
  • Effect: book value is correct (it includes AOCI), but net income and ROE are misstated.
  • Fix: if OCI isn't expected to net to zero over time, adjust NI toward comprehensive income (NI + OCI) when forecasting RI.
  • Compute historical ROE at the aggregate level (NI ÷ equity), not per share. Buybacks and issuance distort per-share ROE.

Balance sheet & income statement

  • Adjust BV to fair value / for off-balance-sheet items: inventory (LIFO), deferred taxes, operating leases, reserves and allowances, intangibles.
  • Acquired intangibles: amortisation lowers ROE and understates value, so add back the amortisation when forecasting ROE. Capitalising and then amortising an omitted asset has no net effect on RI value.
  • Non-recurring items (restructuring, unusual items, discontinued ops, accounting changes): exclude them from forecast earnings. No BV adjustment is needed. If "one-offs" recur every year, treat them as ordinary expenses.
  • Aggressive accounting (accelerating revenue, deferring expenses, capitalising costs) overstates both E and B. Cookie-jar reserves shift income into later periods.
  • International: works best with reliable earnings forecasts, few clean surplus violations, and no delayed recognition of value changes. Otherwise use FCFE.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • "Terminal value is a larger share of value in RI models" → false; it's smaller.
  • "RI can't be used without positive near-term FCF" → false; that's a strength of RI.
  • Forecast OCI affects book value per share, not EPS or DPS.
  • Persistence formula: sum RI to T − 1, then the terminal term uses RIT​ and (1+r)T−1
  • A higher ω gives a higher value. A very low ω (e.g. 0.10) suits extreme ROE.
  • Compare intrinsic value with market price to call a stock under- or overvalued, not with book value.
  • RI growth ≠ NI growth in general. They match only when ROE and payout are constant (g = b × ROE).
  • Single-stage: if V₀ = B₀, then ROE = r.
