Equity Valuation

Free Cash Flow Valuation

In 30 seconds

  • FCFF = cash for all capital providers → discount at WACC → firm value − debt = equity.
  • FCFE = cash for common shareholders → discount at r (cost of equity) → equity directly.
  • Build FCF from NI, CFO, EBIT or EBITDA. Each start point needs different add-backs.
  • Dividends, buybacks and share issues are uses of FCF and don't change it. Net borrowing changes FCFE only.
  • Use FCF when there are no dividends, dividends ≠ capacity to pay, or for a control perspective.

01Valuing with FCFF & FCFE

F1

FCFF approach

Firm=t=1∑∞​(1+WACC)tFCFFt​​Equity=Firm−MV of debt​

Divide equity by shares outstanding (consider dilution from employee options). Subtract preferred too if there is any.

F2

WACC

WACC=wd​rd​(1−t)+wp​rp​+we​r

Weights at market values (or target structure if known and different), not book. t = marginal tax rate. Preferred dividends are not tax-deductible, so there is no (1 − t) on rp​.

F3

FCFE approach

Equity=t=1∑∞​(1+r)tFCFEt​​
F4

Single-stage (constant growth)

Firm=WACC−gFCFF0​(1+g)​Equity=r−gFCFE0​(1+g)​​

FCFF and FCFE growth rates are often not the same. Value scales proportionally with the base-year FCF, so normalise it (strip non-recurring items).

Prefer…When
FCFEStable capital structure (simpler, more direct)
FCFFLevered firm with negative FCFE · changing capital structure (FCFF growth is cleaner; WACC less sensitive to leverage than r)

APV: unlevered firm value (FCFF at the unlevered cost of equity) + NPV of debt effects (tax shield − distress costs).

02Computing FCFF & FCFE

F5

From net income

FCFFFCFE​=NI+NCC+Int(1−t)−FCInv−WCInv=NI+NCC−FCInv−WCInv+Net borr.​

NI is to common, so add back preferred dividends for FCFF. FCInv = capex − proceeds from asset sales; include cash acquisitions.

F6

From CFO

FCFF=CFO+Int(1−t)−FCInvFCFE=CFO−FCInv+Net borr.​

CFO already includes NCC and WCInv. Add back interest only if interest paid is in CFO.

F7

From EBIT or EBITDA

FCFFFCFF​=EBIT(1−t)+Dep−FCInv−WCInv=EBITDA(1−t)+Dep⋅t−FCInv−WCInv​

EBIT: add back all of Dep. EBITDA: add back only the depreciation tax shield (Dep × t). Non-cash items below EBIT need no adjustment.

F8

FCFF ↔ FCFE

FCFE=​FCFF−Int(1−t)+Net borr.​

Net borrowing = debt issued − debt repaid (include notes payable). FCFE > FCFF is possible when borrowing is large.

Depreciation, amortisation, impairment of intangiblesAdd back
Restructuring charge (expense) / its reversal (income)Add back / subtract
Bond discount amortisation / premium amortisationAdd back / subtract

Deferred taxesAdd back, with care
  • Working capital for FCF excludes cash and short-term debt (notes payable, current portion of LTD). ↑ receivables / inventory = use of cash; ↑ payables / accruals = source.
  • Deferred taxes: don't add back DTL increases expected to reverse soon. Add back only if the firm can defer indefinitely (growing firm). Same logic in reverse for a DTA.
  • Share-based comp: non-cash, but option-exercise cash and some tax benefits go to financing. Exclude non-persistent flows; use diluted share counts.
  • Working-capital releases (e.g. falling inventory) are not a sustainable source of FCF growth, especially for a growing firm.
  • Big acquisitions / foreign subsidiaries → balance-sheet changes may not match the cash flow statement ("non-articulation"). Adjust CFO or forecast components.
💡
IFRS vs US GAAP: interest paid sits in CFO under US GAAP, but in CFO or financing under IFRS. Dividends paid are financing under US GAAP, CFO or financing under IFRS. Interest and dividends received are CFO under US GAAP, CFO or investing under IFRS. If interest paid is already in financing, don't add it back to CFO.

Dividends, share issues, buybacks00
  • Dividends and buybacks are uses of FCF. More leverage → bigger interest tax shield (FCFF unaffected, FCFE ↓ by after-tax interest).
  • Uses of FCFF = Δcash + Int(1 − t) − net borrowing + dividends + net buybacks. Uses of FCFE = Δcash + dividends + net buybacks. Uses = sources (a consistency check).
  • NI ≠ FCFE (ignores reinvestment and borrowing). EBITDA ≠ FCFF (pre-tax, ignores the depreciation tax shield, capex and WC). Equal only in special cases (capex = Dep, no ΔWC, no net borrowing).

04Forecasting FCF

F9

Sales-based FCFF

FCFF=​EBIT(1−t)−(FCInv−Dep)−WCInv​
FCInv − Dep
= (capex − Dep) ÷ ΔSales × forecast ΔSales
WCInv
= ΔWC ÷ ΔSales × forecast ΔSales

Replacement capex ≈ Dep (tied to the sales level); net capex funds growth (tied to Δsales).

F10

FCFE with a target debt ratio

FCFE​=NI−(1−DR)(FCInv−Dep)−(1−DR)WCInv​

DR = share of net new investment funded by debt, so net borrowing = DR × (FCInv − Dep + WCInv). Assumes Dep is the only NCC; if there are big other non-cash charges (e.g. ongoing restructuring), forecast each component instead.

05Multistage models & other issues

F11

Two-stage FCFF / FCFE

FirmTVn​EquityTVn​​=t=1∑n​(1+WACC)tFCFFt​​+(1+WACC)nTVn​​=WACC−gFCFFn+1​​=t=1∑n​(1+r)tFCFEt​​+(1+r)nTVn​​=r−gFCFEn+1​​​

Terminal value uses year n + 1 cash flow and is discounted n years. It can also come from a P/E × EPS. It is usually a big share of value.

  • Two-stage versions: constant growth in stage 1, or declining stage-1 growth (H-model-like). "Growth" may refer to sales, NI or FCF, so check which.
  • Three-stage: constant growth in each stage, or constant in stages 1 and 3 with declining growth in stage 2. Good approximations of lumpy cash flows.
  • Stage-2 (terminal) g: slightly below GDP for declining industries, slightly above for industries outgrowing the economy.
  • Real model (high or volatile inflation, international): single-stage FCFE model with real g and real r. Real r = country real return ± industry, size and leverage adjustments.
  • Sensitivity analysis: growth rates and r usually move value the most. Check the base-year FCF too.
  • ESG: quantifiable items (fines, capex) go into cash flows. Hard-to-quantify material risks → add a premium to the cost of equity.
F12

Non-operating assets

Firm=​Operating assets (FCFF)+Non-operating assets​

Excess cash and securities, land held for investment, non-current financial investments at market (not book) value, and a pension surplus (plan assets − liabilities). Then subtract debt (and preferred) to get equity.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Discount FCFF at WACC and FCFE at r. Mixing them is the classic error.
  • From EBITDA, add back Dep × t, not Dep. From EBIT, add back the full Dep.
  • Interest is added back after tax for FCFF. It changes FCFE (−after-tax amount), not FCFF.
  • Increases in notes payable / short-term debt are net borrowing, not working capital.
  • Gain on an asset sale: subtract from NI. Reversal of a restructuring charge: subtract. Loss or impairment: add back.
  • Dividends, buybacks and new share issues have zero effect on FCFF and FCFE.
  • Changing capital structure → prefer FCFF. Takeover target or dividends ≠ FCFE → FCF beats DDM.
  • Non-operating assets: use market value for investments; pension = surplus only; excess cash only.
  • Inventory reductions can't drive long-term FCF growth (they stop at zero).
  • Equity value = firm − debt (− preferred). Don't subtract book debt if market value is given.
