Market-Based Valuation: Price & EV Multiples

In 30 seconds

  • Method of comparables (law of one price) gives relative value. Forecasted fundamentals (a DCF restated as a multiple) gives a justified multiple.
  • Every justified multiple rises with growth and profitability and falls with the required return.
  • Clean the denominator: diluted, underlying (no one-offs), normalized for the cycle, same definition for every company.
  • EV multiples (EV/EBITDA, EV/S) neutralise leverage differences. Match numerator and denominator: firm value ↔ pre-interest flow.
  • Average peer multiples with the median or harmonic mean, not the arithmetic mean.

01P/E: getting the denominator right

  • Trailing P/E: price ÷ last 4 quarters' EPS (TTM). Forward (leading) P/E: price ÷ next 4 quarters / next 12 months / next fiscal year EPS.
  • Prefer forward when forecasts exist, and always after a major acquisition, divestiture or leverage change (trailing EPS no longer relevant). Prefer trailing when earnings aren't predictable.
  • Rising earnings → forward P/E < trailing P/E. So never mix definitions across companies.
  • Use diluted EPS when comparing companies (options, warrants, convertibles).
  • Underlying (core, persistent) EPS: strip items that won't recur (asset-sale gains, one-off legal items, impairments). Check the notes, not just the income statement lines. Restructuring charges that keep coming back are recurring. Don't trust company-defined "core EPS".
  • Look-ahead bias: in back-tests, use only EPS that had been reported at the time (lag the data).
F1

Normalized EPS (cyclicals)

Hist. avg: EPSfull cycle​Avg ROE: ROEfull cycle​×BVPScurrent​​

EPS the firm could earn now under mid-cycle conditions. Average ROE is preferred because current BVPS captures the firm's current size. The historical average ignores growth. Molodovsky effect: cyclicals show high P/Es at the trough and low P/Es at the peak.

Zero, negative or tiny EPS

  • Negative P/E = NM (not meaningful). It would rank as "cheapest" but is actually the most expensive per unit of earnings.
  • Fixes: normalized EPS, forward EPS (if positive), or rank by the earnings yield E/P, highest to lowest = cheapest to dearest. Inverse ratios are also less skewed by outliers.
  • Other inverse ratios: B/P, S/P, CF/P, D/P (used far more than P/D because P/D is undefined with no dividend).

02Justified multiples from fundamentals

Each comes from the Gordon growth model, P0​=D1​/(r−g), divided by a fundamental. Or take any DCF value and divide it by EPS, BVPS, sales or CF.

E/S = net profit margin. Margin works twice: directly and through g = b × PM × asset turnover × leverage. Identity: P/S = P/E × net margin.

Predicted P/E (cross-sectional regression)

  • Regress peers' P/Es on payout, growth and risk (beta), then plug in the stock's values. Predicted < actual → overvalued.
  • Limits: valid only for that sample and period; coefficients unstable over time; multicollinearity makes individual coefficients hard to read.

03Using P/E with comparables

  • Steps: pick the multiple → pick comps and compute a benchmark (median/mean) → apply it, adjusted for fundamentals → explain any gap using fundamentals.
  • Benchmarks: peer group (narrowest GICS sub-industry / ICB subsector), industry or sector, market index, the stock's own history.
  • Higher growth → a premium P/E is justified. Higher risk (operating or financial) → a discount.
  • Market-cap-weighted index P/Es are dominated by mega-caps. For mid-caps, use the index median.
  • Own history: business-mix, leverage, interest-rate or inflation changes break the comparison.
💡
Comparables give relative value only. If the whole peer group is overpriced, a "cheap" stock can still be above intrinsic value.
F6

PEG ratio

PEG=g (in % points)P/E​

Lower = more attractive; < 1 is often read as cheap, but compare with the sector PEG. Flaws: assumes P/E is linear in growth; ignores risk; ignores the duration of growth.

Fed: market overvalued if forward E/P < T-bond yield. Ignores growth, the equity risk premium and inflation effects. Non-linear and explodes at low rates. Yardeni adds growth: higher CBY → lower P/E, higher LTEG → higher P/E.

F8

Inflation & cost pass-through

E1​P0​​=ρ+(1−λ)I1​
ρ
real required return (r − I)
λ
share of cost inflation passed through to earnings

Assumes no real growth and full payout. λ = 1 → P/E = 1/ρ (inflation-neutral). λ = 0 → 1/r. Below full pass-through, higher inflation or lower λ → lower P/E. Explains why cross-country P/Es differ.

F9

Terminal value from a multiple

Vn​=trailing P/E×En​Vn​=forward P/E×En+1​​

Benchmark from comps (median/average industry P/E, own past P/E) or from fundamentals (Gordon). Comps are grounded in market data, but any mispricing carries into the terminal value. Gordon is very sensitive to inputs.

04P/B, P/S, P/CF & dividend yield

F10

Book value per share

BVPS=Common shares outstandingTotal equity−Preferred​

Subtract all claims senior to common: preferred stock plus preferred dividends in arrears. Adjustments: tangible BV (remove goodwill and intangibles), fair-value adjustments to assets and liabilities, off-balance-sheet items, and inventory method (LIFO vs FIFO) for comparability.

05Enterprise value multiples

F11

Enterprise value

EV​=MVequity​+MVpreferred​+Minority interest+MVdebt​−Cash & ST investments​

Net price an acquirer pays: it buys out debt and equity but gets the cash. Use market values of debt (book if close). TIC (total invested capital) = same but does not subtract cash. EV/EBITDA and TIC/EBITDA rankings differ only if cash relative to EBITDA differs.

EV/EBITDA: why

  • Compares firms with different leverage (pre-interest flow)
  • Removes D&A differences, so suits capital-intensive firms
  • EBITDA often positive when EPS isn't
  • Control perspective; most widely used EV multiple

Drawbacks

  • Overstates CFO when working capital grows
  • Ignores revenue-recognition effects on cash
  • Ignores capex: FCFF is better unless D&A ≈ capex

  • All add back interest: they're flows to all capital providers. Industry variants: EV/EBITDAR (airlines: adds back rent), EV/subscribers, EV/reserves.
  • EV/S beats P/S for levered firms (not all sales belong to equity). Compare EV/S with operating margin, not net margin.
  • Lower EV/EBITDA than peers → relatively undervalued, all else equal.

International comparisons

  • Accounting, cultural and macro differences → wide P/E gaps across countries.
  • Least affected by accounting differences: P/CFO, P/FCFE. Most affected: P/E, P/B, EBITDA-based multiples.

06Momentum indicators & averaging

F12

Earnings surprise & SUE

UEt​=EPSt​−E(EPSt​)SUEt​=σ[EPSt​−E(EPSt​)]EPSt​−E(EPSt​)​​

SUE scales by the std dev of past surprises. Scaled earnings surprise scales by the std dev of analysts' current forecasts. Smaller denominator → the same surprise matters more. Rationale: positive surprises may bring persistent alpha.

  • Relative strength: either the stock's own past return (price momentum), or stock ÷ index (above 1 = outperforming). Based on persistence or reversal patterns. A technical indicator suited to growth/momentum styles, not value.
  • Momentum may hint at when price will converge to intrinsic value.
F13

Harmonic means

XH​=∑i=1n​1/Xi​n​XWH​=∑i=1n​wi​/Xi​1​​

Weighted harmonic mean (value weights) = the true portfolio/index P/E (Σ cap ÷ Σ earnings), so index vendors use it. Harmonic ≤ arithmetic. It dampens large outliers but amplifies small ones (near zero). The median dampens both.

Screens

  • A "max P/E" screen must also require positive EPS, or negative-P/E stocks slip through.
  • Limits: vendor data definitions out of your control, no qualitative factors, value-only screens ignore growth, risk and financial strength; results can be very concentrated (few names, one sector); back-tests risk look-ahead bias.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • A recent acquisition or unusual items in trailing EPS → use forward P/E (and forward EPS in the PEG).
  • Underlying EPS: remove only truly one-off items (legal gain/charge, integration cost). Recurring depreciation or repeated "restructuring" stays in.
  • Normalized EPS by average ROE uses current BVPS (common equity, net of preferred), not average BVPS.
  • Negative EPS → rank by E/P; the higher (less negative) E/P is cheaper. Forward P/E doesn't help if next year is also a loss.
  • Trailing justified P/E and P/S carry (1 + g). Forward ones don't. Payout = DPS ÷ EPS of the same year.
  • PEG divides by growth in percent (18.7 ÷ 12.4, not ÷ 0.124). Lowest PEG = most attractive.
  • EV: subtract cash and short-term investments; add preferred and minority interest. Divide by total EBITDA, not per share.
  • P/EBITDA vs EV/EBITDA disagree when leverage differs. Trust EV/EBITDA.
  • SUE denominator = σ of past surprises, not σ of analyst forecasts (that's the scaled surprise).
  • Fed model: E/P > T-bond yield → market undervalued. Yardeni adds the missing growth term.
  • Harmonic mean reduces large outliers only. Stock P/E above the peer harmonic mean → relatively overvalued.
