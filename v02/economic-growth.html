Economic Growth

In 30 seconds

  • Sustainable growth = growth of potential GDP. Long run, earnings growth ≤ potential GDP growth.
  • Growth accounting: output growth = TFP growth + weighted capital and labour growth. TFP is the residual.
  • Capital deepening = move along the production function (diminishing returns). TFP = shifts the whole curve up.
  • Neoclassical: saving changes the level, not the long-run growth rate. Endogenous: higher saving/R&D → permanently higher growth.
  • Convergence: neoclassical predicts it (conditional), endogenous doesn't. Evidence is mixed → club convergence.

01Factors favouring & limiting growth

  • Saving & investment: low income → low saving → low investment → slow growth (vicious cycle). Foreign investment (FDI or portfolio) breaks it.
  • Financial markets: screen and monitor borrowers, create instruments that encourage saving and risk-taking, ease credit constraints. But lax credit / leverage doesn't add long-run growth.
  • Political stability, rule of law, property rights: the first priority in many poor countries.
  • Education & health: developing countries (imitators) → primary/secondary education; developed (innovators) → post-secondary.
  • Tax & regulation: low start-up costs encourage entry and productivity.
  • Free trade & capital flows: world savings fund investment; access to technology and bigger markets.
⚠️
Trap: a new tax on foreign investors hurts growth (blocks capital inflows). Cutting taxes in a very poor economy with a big informal sector is unlikely to help much. High import tariffs delay convergence.

Long run: Δ(P/E) ≈ 0 and the profit/GDP share can't trend forever → equity appreciation ≈ nominal GDP growth. High GDP growth ≠ high equity returns if dilution is big (e.g. privatisations).

Fixed income

  • Actual growth above potential → inflation pressure → higher yields, lower bond prices. Below → output gap, easing, bonds rally.
  • Faster potential growth → higher real interest rates and real asset returns (needed to induce saving).
  • Higher potential growth → better sovereign credit quality; lower forecast growth → higher credit risk.
  • Fiscal stance judged by the structural (cyclically adjusted) deficit at potential GDP.

03Production function & growth accounting

F2

Cobb–Douglas production function

Y=AKαL1−αy=Y/L=Akα,k=K/L​
A
TFP: scales output for any input mix
α
capital's share of income = output elasticity to capital (1−α = labour share)

Constant returns to scale overall; diminishing marginal returns to each input. MPK=αY/K = rental price of capital. α near 0 → returns to capital diminish fast.

F3

Growth accounting (Solow)

YΔY​=AΔA​+αKΔK​+(1−α)LΔL​

TFP growth is the residual (output growth minus weighted input growth), so it is sensitive to how inputs are measured. Use trend K, L and estimated TFP to get potential GDP growth.

F4

Labour productivity method

gpotential​=​glabour force​+glabour productivity​​

Simpler: no capital stock or TFP estimate. Weakness: lumps capital deepening and TFP together, hard to forecast long term. Labour input often = growth in total hours worked.

F5

Capital deepening contribution

glab. prod.​deepening​=gTFP​+deepening=glab. prod.​−gTFP​​

Per worker: Δy/y=ΔA/A+αΔk/k. The bigger the gap between labour-productivity and TFP growth, the more growth came from deepening.

💡
Capital deepening (k rises because capital grows faster than labour) = a move along the curve: big gains at low k (developing), tiny at high k (developed). TFP = upward shift; also raises MPK, so more investment pays. Developed countries grow mainly via technology.

04Inputs: resources, labour, capital, technology

Expanded production function: Y=AF(N,L,H,KIT​,KNT​,KP​): natural resources, labour quantity, human capital, ICT capital, non-ICT capital, public capital.

Natural resources

  • Renewable vs non-renewable. Owning resources is not necessary for wealth (access via trade is enough).
  • Resource curse: (1) institutions fail to develop; (2) Dutch disease: resource exports push the currency up → manufacturing becomes uncompetitive.
  • Depletion fears are probably overstated: technology cuts resource use per unit and creates substitutes.

Labour supply

  • Driven by population growth, participation rate, net migration and average hours (trend down in developed countries; cyclical).
  • Population growth raises GDP growth, not per capita GDP growth. Higher participation (e.g. more women working) can raise per capita growth.
  • Age mix: large share under 15 = demographic dividend; ageing (over 65) = burden. Immigration can offset low birth rates.
  • Lower unemployment = a move to a higher level, not a permanently higher growth rate.
  • Human capital: education and training; positive externalities (spillovers).

Physical capital & technology

  • Investment/GDP and growth are highly correlated: deepening lasts a long time from a low base; composition matters.
  • ICT capital → network externalities, can lift TFP and potential growth. Non-ICT capital → mostly deepening.
  • Technology is the most important driver of per capita growth in developed countries (R&D, patents, management methods).
  • Public infrastructure complements private capital and raises productivity.
  • A permanent rise in labour productivity growth raises the ceiling on earnings growth and equity returns.

05Theories of growth

Classical (Malthus): per capita income gains are temporary; population rises until income falls back to subsistence. Failed: richer countries had slower population growth, and technology outran diminishing returns.

Neoclassical (Solow)

Saving per worker just covers: equipping new workers (n), replacing depreciation (δ) and deepening at the steady-state rate.

yΔy​kΔk​​=1−αθ​+αs(KY​−Ψ)=1−αθ​+s(KY​−Ψ)​

Y/K above Ψ (capital scarce) → growth above steady state. A rise in s lowers Ψ → growth jumps, then decays back as Y/K falls.

Increase in…Steady-state k and y (level)
Saving rate sHigher

Only a change in TFP growth changes the long-run growth rate of output per worker.

Neoclassical implications

  • Capital accumulation affects the level of output, not the long-run growth rate.
  • Growth is fastest when capital is first accumulated; it slows as k rises.
  • Without TFP gains, labour productivity growth eventually stalls.
  • Poor countries (high MPK) should grow faster → convergence.
  • Criticism: TFP is exogenous (unexplained; Solow found it drove > 80% of US per capita growth). Augmented Solow adds inputs (human capital, ICT) to shrink the residual but keeps diminishing returns.

Endogenous growth (Romer)

Broad capital (human, knowledge, R&D) → no diminishing returns in aggregate. Higher s → permanently higher growth. No steady state.

  • R&D creates spillovers: social return > private return → firms under-invest (market failure) → case for government subsidies to R&D/knowledge, not to all investment.
  • Constant returns to capital for the economy, but diminishing for each firm (no single-firm monopoly).

06Convergence & open economies

TypeMeaning
AbsoluteAll poor countries catch up in per capita level, whatever their traits. Neoclassical predicts equal growth rates, not this.
ConditionalCatch-up only among countries with the same saving rate, population growth and production function.
ClubRich and middle-income "club" members converge; outsiders fall behind until they make institutional reforms.
  • Convergence channels: capital deepening (high MPK) and imitating technology.
  • Non-convergence trap: no reforms; old policies (e.g. import substitution) kept too long.
  • Evidence mixed: some fast catch-up, some poor countries diverge.

Open economy

  • Benefits: investment not limited by domestic saving; comparative advantage; scale; imported technology; competition.
  • Neoclassical: capital flows to capital-poor countries (they run trade deficits), speeding convergence. Growth above steady state is temporary.
  • Endogenous: trade permanently raises growth via selection (weak firms exit), scale (bigger markets, helps small countries) and backwardness (knowledge spillovers to laggards) effects.
  • Outward-oriented (export-led) policies have beaten inward-oriented (import substitution).

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Long-run stock market driver = GDP growth; Δ(P/E) and Δ(profits/GDP) → 0. A profit share well above average will fall.
  • Steady state: θ/(1−α)+n. Divide by the labour share. Potential growth above it = still converging (capital deepening).
  • Capital deepening = labour productivity growth minus TFP growth.
  • Same capital per worker but higher output per worker → difference is TFP.
  • For a developed, high-k country the biggest lever is technology, not more saving or freer trade.
  • Population growth does not explain higher per capita growth.
  • Resource-rich, good institutions, slow growth → Dutch disease (strong currency hurts manufacturing). Strong manufacturing exports are not the cause.
  • High potential growth → high real interest rates and real asset returns.
  • Same saving, population growth and production function → conditional convergence; "only with institutional reforms" → club.
  • "Open trade speeds convergence but no permanent growth gain" = neoclassical. A patent initiative raises TFP; new factories are pure deepening.
