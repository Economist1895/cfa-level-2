Hedge Fund Strategies

In 30 seconds

  • Hedge funds: light regulation, flexible mandates, shorting, leverage, lock-ups/gates, high fees. Classified by instruments, trading philosophy (systematic vs discretionary) and risks taken.
  • Six groups: equity (L/S, short-biased, EMN), event-driven (merger arb, distressed), relative value (FI arb, convertible arb), opportunistic (global macro, managed futures), specialist (volatility, reinsurance/life settlements), multi-manager (FoF, multi-strategy).
  • Key shapes: merger arb and FI carry ≈ short put (left tail). Managed futures and long volatility ≈ right-tail / convex.
  • Conditional factor model adds crisis-dummy betas (equity, credit, USD, VIX) to expose hidden stress risks.
  • A 20% hedge fund sleeve in a 60/40 portfolio usually lowers SD and drawdown and raises Sharpe and Sortino.

01Characteristics & classification

  • Features: few legal constraints (accredited investors), flexible mandate, wide universe (distressed, royalties, insurance), aggressive/concentrated styles, liberal leverage (prime broker or derivatives), lock-ups, gates, mgmt fee ≈ 1%+ and incentive fee 10–20%.
  • Liquid alts (mutual fund/UCITS/ETF versions): daily liquidity, lower fees, leverage limits → underperform LP hedge funds (lost illiquidity premium). Mostly L/S equity and managed futures.
  • Single-manager vs multi-manager (multi-strategy fund or fund-of-funds).
  • Some strategies are return enhancers (expensive embedded beta in L/S, event-driven); others are true diversifiers (macro, managed futures, long vol, at a cost in normal times).

03Event-driven strategies

  • Hard catalyst (event announced) is less risky than soft catalyst (anticipated event).

Merger arbitrage

  • Cash deal: buy the target. Stock-for-stock: buy target, short acquirer in the offer ratio. Acquirers pay in stock when they think their shares are overvalued.
  • 70–90% of US deals close. If a deal fails, prices revert → losses of 20–40%.
  • Profile = selling insurance ≈ long bond + short put. High Sharpe, left-tail risk; deals fail more in stress → hidden market beta. Liquid, moderate–high leverage.
  • Wider spreads (higher risk): cross-border (two regulators), vertical integration (antitrust).
  • Other tools: target's convertibles (bond floor if the deal fails), CDS when the acquirer's credit is stronger.

Distressed securities

  • Bought from forced sellers (rating limits) at discounts. Liquidation: estimate recovery by priority (senior secured → junior secured → unsecured → convertible → preferred → common). Reorganisation: value the new claims; debt may become equity (fulcrum security).
  • Long-biased, illiquid, mark-to-model (smoothed), binary outcomes. Long lock-ups and gates. Leverage low–moderate (≈1.2–1.7× NAV). Lumpy, cyclical; best early in a recovery.
  • Capital structure arbitrage: long undervalued debt, short equity (or long puts) → can profit across outcomes.

04Relative value strategies

Fixed-income arbitrage

  • Buy cheap, short rich; usually duration-neutral (only hedges small parallel shifts). Credit bets → L/S credit (more volatile).
  • Yield curve trades (calendar spreads: steepeners/flatteners). Carry trades: long higher-yielding, short lower-yielding, e.g. long off-the-run vs short on-the-run Treasuries.
  • Payoff ≈ short put: carry + convergence, but spread widening + high leverage → margin calls, forced sales. Leverage via repo with haircuts; availability falls with complexity.
  • US Treasuries: most liquid, fewest mispricings. Liquidity falls through other sovereigns → mortgages → corporates.
  • Crisis "arbitrage" (e.g. TIPS + inflation swap vs Treasuries) still needs repo access, credit lines and the borrow, which shrink in stress.

Convertible bond arbitrage

  • Convertible = straight bond + equity call. Small, illiquid, unrated issues → embedded option is cheap (implied vol < historical vol).
  • Trade: long convertible, short stock (delta hedge), then gamma-trade. Hedge or keep credit and rate risk (credit-oriented managers keep credit).
  • Typical book: 300% long, 200% short (short sized by delta). High leverage.
  • Best: high issuance, moderate volatility, good liquidity. Worst: credit stress, illiquidity, short squeezes / lost borrow.

05Opportunistic: global macro & managed futures

  • Top-down, across asset classes. Split by analysis (fundamental vs technical), implementation (discretionary: behavioural biases; systematic: struggles with new regimes, herding/slippage) and instruments.
Global macroManaged futures
StyleMostly discretionary, anticipatory, often early/contrarian; directional or thematicMostly systematic trend/momentum; futures & options on futures
Liquidity · leverageHigh · high (margin/equity 15–25%)High · high (margin 0.1–10%; 85–90% of capital in T-bills)

EnemyLow-volatility, mean-reverting, trendless markets (e.g. QE era, central bank intervention)
  • Time-series momentum (TSM): long assets with positive past returns, short those with negative → can be net long or short, more volatile, needs strong trends.
  • Cross-sectional momentum (CSM): long the best relative performers, short the worst within an asset class → roughly net zero exposure.
  • Position sizing by volatility and correlation; exits by price target, momentum reversal, time, or trailing stop. Avoid overfitting; signals decay as they spread.

06Specialist & multi-manager strategies

Volatility trading

  • Relative value vol (same option cheaper in one time zone/market), smile and skew trades with spreads, straddles, calendars. Equity vol is ~80% negatively correlated with equity returns.
  • Long vol: buy options and delta hedge; longer-dated = more vega. OTC options, VIX futures, volatility swaps (realised vol) or variance swaps (vol²) → counterparty risk, ISDA, margin.
  • Long vol is convex (right-skewed payoff, small premium), a crisis hedge, but bleeds from time decay in calm markets. Short vol earns steady premium.

Reinsurance & life settlements

  • Life settlement: buy policies from a broker, pay the premiums, collect the death benefit. Look for low surrender value, low ongoing premiums, and shorter life expectancy than actuarial tables imply.
  • Catastrophe reinsurance / cat bonds: risks uncorrelated with markets; seasonal issuance.

  • Stepwise build: list factors → pairwise correlations (both regimes) → for pairs with ρ > 0.6 keep the one giving the higher adjusted R² → repeat.
  • Desired exposures. Normal: long equity, long credit (+), short USD (−), short vol (−). Crisis: short equity, short credit, long USD (+, safe haven), long vol (+).
  • Findings: EMN ≈ 0.1 equity beta; L/S equity significant equity beta; most managers cut equity exposure in crises. Analysis reveals hidden stress exposures.
  • Databases are full of defunct funds (~80%) → high attrition. Rho (serial correlation) flags smoothed, illiquid returns.

HWM = high-water mark (prior peak). Sortino penalises only downside → better for strategies with big negative events. Smoothed (illiquid) returns flatter Sharpe.

  • 48/32/20 test (20% hedge fund sleeve in 60/40): nearly all strategies cut SD and raise Sharpe/Sortino.
  • Lowest SD: systematic futures, EMN, macro FoFs. Smallest max drawdown: global macro, systematic futures, merger arb, EMN. FoFs and multi-manager add little risk-adjusted benefit.
  • Variance limit: required σ ≤ σcurrent × √(limit %), e.g. "variance ≤ 90% of current".

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • Merger arb isn't beta-free: deals fail in stress → left tail, ≈ short put. The arb shorts the acquirer in stock deals only.
  • Failed deal: revalue both legs at pre-announcement prices; the long target and short acquirer both lose.
  • Short-biased = lower returns and higher volatility than L/S equity; the benefit is negative correlation.
  • EMN uses high leverage (beta hedged away); distressed and short sellers use low leverage. Convert arb ≈ 300/200.
  • Beta-neutral pair: short = long × βlong/βshort, not equal dollar amounts.
  • Convertible arb: long the convertible, short the stock. Net profit subtracts borrow cost and dividend, and adds coupon per share.
  • FI carry: long off-the-run (cheap, illiquid), short on-the-run. Duration-neutral ≠ curve-neutral.
  • Right-tail skew in stress = managed futures (and long vol). Global macro diversifies but with mixed outcomes.
  • CSM ≈ net zero exposure; TSM can be net long/short and is more volatile.
  • Netting risk falls on the FoF investor; multi-strategy GPs usually absorb it. Multi-strategy risk = leverage + concentrated operational risk.
