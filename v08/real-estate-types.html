Overview of Types of Real Estate Investment

In 30 seconds

  • Economic use drives value. NOI = EGI − operating expenses − maintenance allowance. It is before financing and tax.
  • Debt view: LTV and DSC. Equity view: equity dividend rate (pre-tax cash yield) and after-tax cash flow.
  • Real estate cycle: recovery → expansion → oversupply → recession. NOI and DSC peak in oversupply. LTV peaks in recovery.
  • Three valuation approaches: income (direct cap NOI/(r − g), or DCF), cost (replacement cost − depreciation), sales comparison.
  • Appraisal-based indexes lag and smooth → understate volatility and correlation. Fix by unsmoothing or using transaction-based indexes.

01Features, NOI & return measures

  • Risk spectrum (low → high): senior debt → core (income-producing, bond-like) → core-plus → value-add → opportunistic (development, equity-like).
  • Property classes A–D: Class A = new, best amenities, highest rents but least appreciation potential. Lower classes are cheaper with more upside (needs capex).

Loss to lease = market rents − current lease rents. Positive → in-place leases below market (tight supply). Negative → leases above market (excess supply, rents falling).

Debt service = interest + principal. Lenders cap LTV and set a minimum DSC. Max loan = the lower of the two limits. For interest-only debt: loan = (NOI / min DSC) / rate.

DebtEquity
PrivateMortgages, construction loans, mezzanineDirect (sole, JV, LP), private funds/REITs
PublicMBS/CMBS, covered bonds, mortgage REITsREOCs, REITs, funds/ETFs
  • Buying a property directly = private equity. Lending on it = private debt.
  • GICS: REITs vs real estate management & development companies. Mortgages sit in financials/fixed income.
  • REOC = taxable, few restrictions. REIT = must mainly own rental property or mortgages and distribute (nearly) all income → no corporate tax.
  • Cyclicality: health care = defensive. Office, industrial = cyclical.
  • Covered bonds: dual recourse (issuer + segregated pool), substitutable collateral → lower risk. US/Japan mortgages carry prepayment risk.
  • Property-specific risks: management, obsolescence, tech change, environmental, zoning, and over-reliance on pricing models.

03Value drivers, cycle & portfolio role

  • Drivers: GDP, jobs, wages, household formation, demographics; heavy debt use → very sensitive to rates and credit; local factors (employers, infrastructure, zoning, taxes).
  • Construction lags → new supply arrives after conditions change.
PhaseWhat happens
RecoveryTrough, tight credit, little construction, low occupancy. NOI, DSC low; LTV high
ExpansionEasier credit, occupancy and rents rise, prices up, new building starts
OversupplyPipeline completes → glut, occupancy falls. NOI, DSC peak; LTV low. Replacement cost > market price
RecessionOccupancy falls faster, construction at lows, prices decline

Portfolio characteristics

  • Current income: leases with step-ups (pre-set, not contingent), indexed rents (CPI), overage rent (% of sales above a threshold). Rollover risk when leases expire before the holding period ends.
  • Capital appreciation, measurable precisely only on sale.
  • Inflation hedge: rents and prices tend to rise with inflation.
  • Diversification: low correlation for private property, but REITs and MBS correlate more with stocks/bonds in the short to medium term.
  • Tax: accelerated tax depreciation; REIT pass-through avoids double taxation.

05Due diligence & valuation

  • Due diligence: market review, current lease review (rents vs market, expiries, tenant credit), future lease outlook (re-leasing costs, free rent, rent caps), financial review (underinvested maintenance? inflated occupancy?), documentation (liens, title, zoning; feeds the purchase, not NOI), inspection.
F8

Direct capitalisation

V=Cap rateNOI1​​Cap rate=r−g​

Cap rate = NOI / value (≈ a bond's current yield, or inverse of EV/EBITDA). Higher r (rates, risk, oversupply) → higher cap rate → lower value. Faster growth → lower cap rate → higher value. Use when NOI is stable; otherwise normalise NOI or use DCF.

Going-in cap = NOI1 / V0. It can be below the terminal cap if near-term NOI is temporarily depressed.

IncomeDirect cap or DCF. Large commercial / investment property (few comparables)

06Real estate indexes

F10

Holding period return (appraisal index)

HPR=V0​NOI−Capex+(V1​−V0​)​

V from appraisals. ≈ single-period IRR. The income part does not equal cash distributions to fund or REIT investors.

  • Appraisal-based: values lag transactions and properties aren't all appraised each quarter → smoothed. Lag rising markets (understate gains) and falling markets (understate losses).
  • Effect: volatility and correlation understated → Sharpe overstated → over-allocation to real estate.
  • Transaction-based: repeat sales (same property sold twice; needs repeat transactions) or hedonic (regression controlling for size, age, quality, location; no repeat sales needed). They lead appraisal indexes but are noisier.
  • Public: REIT indexes (float-adjusted cap-weighted). Fixed income: MBS indexes carry prepayment (contraction/extension) risk; covered bonds simplest.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Direct cap: divide by r − g, not r + g and not r alone.
  • Terminal value uses year n+1 NOI ÷ terminal cap rate, not year-n NOI and not the going-in cap.
  • Max loan: compute both the LTV limit and the DSC limit and take the smaller.
  • DSC uses total debt service (interest + principal). Taxes deduct only interest + depreciation; depreciation excludes land.
  • GPRI − gross rental income = loss to lease, not vacancy or concessions (those come next).
  • Cycle metrics: NOI and DSC peak in oversupply and bottom in recovery. LTV is the reverse.
  • Cost approach overstates value in oversupply. Replacement cost includes developer's profit.
  • Appraisal indexes understate volatility and correlation; they understate returns in rising markets and overstate them in falling ones.
  • Hedonic ≠ repeat sales: only repeat-sales indexes need the same property sold more than once.
  • Documentation review doesn't feed the NOI forecast. Industrial property is the least convertible; multi-family has the lowest structural risk.
