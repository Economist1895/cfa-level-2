Model Misspecification

In 30 seconds

  • Good specification: economic reasoning, parsimony, out-of-sample performance, right functional form, assumptions satisfied.
  • Conditional heteroskedasticity → SEs too small → t inflated → too many Type I errors. Detect with Breusch–Pagan (nR²); fix with robust (White) SEs.
  • Serial correlation → same SE problem; coefficients also inconsistent if a lagged Y is a regressor. Detect with Breusch–Godfrey (or DW); fix with Newey–West / HAC SEs.
  • Multicollinearity → SEs too large → t deflated → Type II errors. Detect with VIF (>5 look, >10 serious).

Omitted variable bias

  • Omitted X2​ uncorrelated with X1​: slope on X1​ still correct; intercept biased; residuals not i.i.d.
  • Omitted X2​ correlated with X1​: error correlated with X1​ → intercept, slope and SEs all biased and inconsistent. No valid tests.
  • Nonlinearity: if the relation is linear in proportional changes, take the natural log.
  • Pooling: scatter shows separate clusters. Use the subsample most like the forecast period.

02Heteroskedasticity

  • Unconditional: error variance not related to the X's. Violates an assumption but causes no major inference problems.
  • Conditional: error variance related to the X's (e.g. fans out as X grows). Problematic.
  • Effects: coefficients unaffected (still consistent); SEs underestimated → t inflated → false significance (Type I). MSE is biased → F-test unreliable.
F1

Breusch–Pagan test

χBP2​=nRresid2​,df=k
Rresid2​
R² from regressing the squared residuals on the original X's (not the original R²)
k
number of independent variables

H0​: no conditional heteroskedasticity. One-tailed (right) χ² test. Reject if nR2 > critical value.

💡
Fix: robust standard errors (also called White-corrected or heteroskedasticity-consistent). Coefficients don't change; SEs get larger and t-stats smaller.

03Serial correlation

  • Errors correlated across observations. Common in time series and panel data.
  • Positive (most common): residual sign persists. F inflated (MSE too low); SEs underestimated → t inflated → Type I.
  • Under EMH, financial returns shouldn't show it. If they do, there's a predictable pattern to exploit.

F2

Breusch–Godfrey test

ε^t​=a0​+∑aj​Xjt​+ρ1​ε^t−1​+⋯+ρp​ε^t−p​+ut​​

Regress residuals on the original X's plus p lagged residuals. H0​: ρ1​=⋯=ρp​=0 (no serial correlation up to lag p). F-distributed, df p and n−p−k−1 (or χ²).

  • Durbin–Watson tests only first-order serial correlation. BG handles any order p, so it's more robust.
  • Fix: Newey–West / HAC / serial-correlation-consistent SEs. They also correct conditional heteroskedasticity. Coefficients unchanged; serial correlation isn't removed, just accounted for.

04Multicollinearity

  • Two or more X's highly correlated, or an approximate linear combination among X's. (An exact one means the model can't be estimated.)
  • Coefficients still consistent but imprecise; can't separate individual effects.
  • SEs inflated → t deflated → low power → Type II errors.
  • Classic symptom: high R² and significant F, but few or no significant t-stats.
  • Pairwise correlations are only adequate with exactly two X's. With more, multicollinearity can exist despite low pairwise correlations.
F3

Variance inflation factor

VIFj​=1−Rj2​1​
Rj2​
R² from regressing Xj​ on the other k−1 X's

Minimum 1 (Rj2​=0, no correlation). > 5 → investigate; > 10 → serious, correct it.

Fixes: drop one or more X's · use a different proxy · increase the sample size. If the goal is prediction only, multicollinearity may not matter.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • BP statistic = n × R² of the residual regression (e.g. 96 × 0.065 = 6.25), not n × the original R². Compare it with the χ² critical value (df = k).
  • Serial correlation + lagged Y as a regressor → coefficients invalid and SEs deflated. Without a lagged Y, coefficients stay valid.
  • "Deflated" (underestimated) SEs mean inflated t-stats. Don't mix up the direction.
  • Multicollinearity does the opposite: SEs inflated, t too small.
  • VIF flags: choose the variables with VIF > 10. VIF ≈ 4 is not a serious problem.
  • All three multicollinearity fixes (drop, proxy, larger sample) are valid.
  • Unconditional heteroskedasticity is not a serious problem. Only conditional heteroskedasticity is.
  • Robust SEs change standard errors only, never the coefficients.
  • Newey–West SEs fix both serial correlation and conditional heteroskedasticity.
