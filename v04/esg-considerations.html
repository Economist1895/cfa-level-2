ESG Considerations in Investment Analysis

In 30 seconds

  • Ownership × voting power decides the conflict: dispersed/dispersed → principal–agent; concentrated voting → principal–principal.
  • Judge the board: independence, CEO duality, committees, skills, tenure (>10 yrs = long), diversity. Plus pay and voting rights.
  • ESG data is voluntary and inconsistent → comparability problems. Materiality depends on industry and horizon.
  • Equity: ESG finds opportunities and downside. Fixed income: mainly downside (bond is capped at par).
  • Integration = adjust forecasts (revenue, costs, capex, impairments) then valuation (discount rate, multiples; credit spread for bonds).

01Ownership structures & conflicts

  • Dispersed: many holders, none can control. Concentrated: a holder or group (family, company, state) can control. Hybrid in between. Concentrated is the global norm.
  • Control ≠ ownership: a controller can be a minority holder via dual-class shares, pyramid (vertical) structures or cross-holdings (horizontal).

Diagnose from the vignette: % owned by the largest holder and whether votes are one-share-one-vote. A family with 55% and equal votes = concentrated/concentrated → principal–principal.

How ownership shapes governance

  • Independent directors: more common in dispersed markets (they police managers). Rules: minimum number (1–3) or ratio (20–50%+).
  • Board structure: one-tier (exec + non-exec) is most common; two-tier = supervisory board over management board (mandatory in some countries; may include labour/bank reps).
  • Special voting arrangements to protect minorities (e.g. separate minority approval of independent directors).
  • Codes: "comply or explain"; some go further. Stewardship codes push investors to engage.

  • Board evaluation: self or external review; covers duties, leadership, structure, board–management interaction.
  • Remuneration: transparency, KPI disclosure, link to strategy, CEO-to-worker pay ratio. Say-on-pay = shareholder vote; clawback = recover pay after restatement or misconduct.
  • Voting: straight voting (one share, one vote) vs dual-class (founders keep control → minority conflict).
  • Also look at ethics, bribery, whistleblower and related-party policies.
  • Good governance → lower cost of capital, better credit access. Analysts express governance views via the risk premium or credit spread.

04Identifying ESG risks & opportunities

  • Main challenge: getting relevant, comparable, decision-useful data. Disclosure is mostly voluntary and inconsistently reported; reports may not be assured.
  • Materiality: ESG issues expected to affect operations, financial performance and valuation. Firms may label positive items "material" and under-report negative ones.
  • Horizon matters (esp. for bonds of different maturities). Issues can jump from long-term to short-term (new regulation, spill).
  • Governance factors are fairly uniform across firms; E and S are industry-specific (energy/steel → E; banks → S such as data privacy).

  • Stranded assets: obsolete or uneconomic due to regulation or demand shifts → impairments; hit long-dated bonds harder than short-dated.

05ESG integration & green bonds

EquityCredit
GoalOpportunities + downsideMainly downside
StatementsRevenue, costs, margins, capex; balance-sheet impairments
ValuationDiscount rate, multiples, terminal value, scenariosCredit spread, CDS, relative value, duration
  • Process: identify material factors → assess vs history and peers → adjust forecasts → adjust valuation. Check whether the risk is already priced in spreads.
  • Recurring vs one-off issues need different forecast treatment.
  • E: resource intensity (e.g. water per unit output) → COGS and margin. S: recalls, warning letters, fines, labour turnover, wage laws → revenue ↓, costs ↑, litigation provisions. G: weak board vs peers → higher risk premium / spread.

Green bonds

  • Proceeds earmarked for environmental projects; same rating and recourse as the issuer's conventional bonds. Analysis ≈ conventional.
  • Issuer labels the bond; Green Bond Principles are voluntary (ICMA). Extra monitoring/reporting cost; benefit: broader investor base, possible new-issue premium.
  • May trade at a premium (tighter spread) from demand. Risks: greenwashing (proceeds not really green) and liquidity (buy-and-hold holders).

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Majority family stake + equal votes → principal–principal, not principal–agent. Voting caps = concentrated ownership with dispersed votes.
  • Family control lowers principal–agent risk; it isn't automatically the governance red flag.
  • 1 independent of 15 directors is below the 20–50% norm → the governance risk. Separate CEO/chair = no duality (a positive).
  • "Material ESG info is best obtained from companies" → false: company data are inconsistent and voluntary.
  • "Horizon has little effect on materiality" → false.
  • Weakness by source: proprietary → inconsistent reporting; vendors → subjective scores; SASB etc. → promote uniform standards (not a weakness).
  • Bond analysis focuses on downside risk and adjusts the spread, not the equity discount rate.
  • Coal-reliant producer facing emissions rules + falling customer demand → E risk, stranded assets, higher discount rate, lower revenue, higher costs → value ↓.
  • Higher labour costs from wage laws + weak board → spread widens.
