Corporate Restructuring

In 30 seconds

  • Three families: investment (size ↑: equity stake, JV, acquisition), divestment (size ↓: sale, spin-off), restructuring (same scope: cost, balance sheet, reorganisation). LBO mixes all three.
  • Activity is pro-cyclical (confidence, cheap financing, overvalued stock), plus industry shocks.
  • Evaluate: initial (what, why, material?, when) → preliminary valuation (comps, transactions, premium paid) → pro forma modelling → update thesis.
  • Material = size (> 10% of acquirer's EV) or fit (signals a strategy change).
  • Pro forma: EPS, net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, WACC (weights and component costs both move; watch the IG rating line).

01Life cycle, motives & types

  • Life cycle: start-up → growth → maturity (peak FCF, 20%+ debt) → decline. Managers act to change that destiny.

InvestmentSynergies, growth, capabilities/resources (vertical integration), undervalued target
DivestmentFocus, valuation (conglomerate discount), liquidity, regulation
RestructuringRaise returns on capital (opportunistic), or forced by financial distress
  • Cost synergies = economies of scale (one HQ, utilisation, route density). Revenue synergies = economies of scope (cross-selling, pricing power).
  • Top-down drivers: high security prices (CEO confidence, lower financing cost, overvalued stock as currency) and industry shocks (regulation, technology, growth shifts). Deals in weak economies have created more value on average.

02Restructuring actions & LBOs

  • Opportunistic: change the business model (e.g. franchising: asset-light royalty income, store-level risk shifts to franchisees).
  • Cost restructuring: lift margins to history/peers; outsourcing (third party, scale) and offshoring (own operations moved abroad). Often prompted by activists or an unwanted bid.
  • Balance sheet restructuring:
    • Sale-leaseback: cash now, keep use. Lease cost usually > the depreciation it replaces. Quick liquidity; unlocks non-core asset value.
    • Dividend recap: borrow to pay dividends/buy back → cheaper capital mix, lower WACC, higher leverage. Suits stable cash flows.
  • Reorganisation: court-supervised; operations and management continue; negotiate asset sales, debt-for-equity, refinancing. Can be pre-packaged. ≠ liquidation (court sells assets, pays creditors by priority).
  • LBO: PE fund buys with heavy debt, restructures, exits by sale or IPO. Returns driven by purchase price, leverage, FCF used to repay debt, exit price. More leveraged after exit than before.

03Initial evaluation & preliminary valuation

  • Four questions: what, why, is it material, when. Be sceptical: management always frames it positively.
  • Size: deal value (cash + stock + debt assumed) ÷ acquirer's pre-deal EV; for cost programmes, cuts as % of revenue/opex. Most deals are small; for large caps most are immaterial.
  • Fit: even a small deal in a new industry can signal a strategy change or a problem in the core business.

  • Then: financing → synergies → required divestitures → goodwill and fair-value step-ups.
  • Debt-funded equity stake in a loss-maker: equity-method losses + interest → EPS ↓; debt ↑ but investee income isn't in EBITDA → debt/EBITDA ↑.
  • JV buy-out to control: switch from equity method to consolidation + non-controlling interest.
  • Stock-funded deal: share count ↑ → EPS can fall even if earnings rise. Selling a segment for acquirer stock leaves the seller with an equity investment.
  • Sale + buyback: lost segment earnings usually dilute EPS; buyback offsets partly.
F3

Cost cut to reach a target margin

Cut=Opex−Sales(1−Target margin)

Phase in evenly over the programme; one-time restructuring costs can make next year's EBIT fall. Risks: cutting the spend that drives growth; political/regulatory backlash from layoffs.

Credit ratios & cap rate

EBITDADebt​,Coverage=InterestEBIT​Cap rate=Property valueNOI​​

Sale-leaseback: rent ↑ opex, depreciation ↓, deferred gain amortised; repaying debt cuts interest → ratios improve → possible upgrade. Lower cap rate = higher price = better for the seller.

Pro forma WACC

  • Weights: debt-funded cash deal → wd​ ↑. Selling a unit to repay debt → we​ ↑. Measure with market values (shares × price).
  • Costs move with profitability, volatility, leverage (debt/EBITDA), collateral, rates. More leverage + lower margins → higher WACC.
  • Falling from BBB to BB (IG → speculative) adds several hundred bp to WACC, so IG issuers size deals to keep the rating.

  • Judge a bid against (1) comparable transactions and (2) the segment's implied value inside the current EV (current EV − other segments' value).
  • Separating a low-margin segment lifts margin but can cut total EBIT; also check stranded corporate costs and effects on remaining segments.
  • Strategic reviews get priced in on announcement → value each scenario × likelihood.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Selling stores and leasing them back = balance sheet restructuring, not cost restructuring or reorganisation.
  • Sale-leaseback that repays debt → cost of capital falls (not rises); real estate is non-core for a retailer.
  • SOTP: multiply each segment by its own peer multiple; using an average multiple is the distractor.
  • Conglomerate discount = SOTP − current EV (not the reverse).
  • Pro forma operating income: include both that year's synergies and one-time costs.
  • Material if deal value > 10% of the acquirer's pre-announcement EV, even with no strategy change.
  • Pre-deal debt = post-deal debt − debt assumed − new debt; equity at market (shares × price).
  • Only acquisitions give control; JVs need bigger investment and more equal governance than equity stakes.
  • Comparable company analysis gives a trading price: add a premium for a takeover value. Its strength is market-derived values, its weakness market mispricing.
