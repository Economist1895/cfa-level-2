Cost of Capital: Advanced Topics

In 30 seconds

  • WACC uses target weights and the marginal tax rate; there's no single right method for each cost.
  • Cost drivers: top-down (capital availability, rates/spreads/ERP, legal system, tax) and bottom-up (volatility, assets, leverage, security features).
  • Cost of debt: YTM if traded; else matrix pricing or synthetic rating; bank loans, leases (implicit rate / IBR); add a country premium abroad.
  • ERP: historical (index, period, mean, rf proxy; survivorship bias) or forward-looking (survey, DDM, Grinold–Kroner).
  • Cost of equity: DDM, bond yield + RP, CAPM / Fama–French; private firms add SP, IP, SCRP; international adds a CRP.

01WACC & what drives it

F1

WACC

WACC=wd​rd​(1−t)+wp​rp​+we​re​

Weights: long-term target structure (may differ from current). t = marginal rate, not effective. If the interest-deduction cap is already reached, no tax adjustment on new debt.

Top-down (external)

  • More capital availability / developed markets → lower spreads and ERP. Thin bond markets → banks, shadow banking.
  • Market conditions: higher rf, inflation, FX volatility → higher cost. Spreads and ERP widen in recessions, tighten in expansions.
  • Common law (stronger investor rights) → lower cost than civil law. Regulation matters (banks, utilities).
  • Higher marginal tax rate → bigger debt tax shield.

Bottom-up (company)

Lowers costRaises cost
Stable, recurring revenue; predictable earningsCustomer concentration; high operating & financial leverage; ESG risk
Tangible, fungible, liquid assetsIntangibles, specialised illiquid PP&E
High profitability, cash flow, interest coverageHigh debt/EBITDA, D/E
Put, convertible debt; cumulative preferred; secured debtCallable debt; inferior voting/cash-flow share class
  • Collateralised debt lowers rd​ but may raise re​ (creditors' prior claim).
  • Put/convertible: cheaper at issue, but later cost (refinance at higher rates if put; dilution if converted). Callable: dearer at issue, cheaper later if rates fall.

02Estimating the cost of debt

Debt typeMethod
Traded, straightYTM on longest bond (or a more liquid shorter one)
Non-traded, ratedMatrix pricing: YTMs of similar rating/maturity bonds, interpolate
Non-traded, unratedSynthetic rating from IC, D/E → rating's YTM or rf + spread
Bank debtRate on recent new loans (if conditions and risk unchanged). Amortising < bullet cost
LeasesRate implicit in lease; else IBR; else non-traded method
  • Illiquid debt's quoted YTM includes a liquidity premium: unreliable. Matrix-priced yield from liquid bonds → actual cost slightly higher.
  • Synthetic rating ratios can conflict (IC says B, D/E says BBB) → look at trends and use judgment.
  • Issuer rating can differ from issue ratings (collateral, seniority).
F2

Rate implicit in a lease

FVasset​+Lessor direct costs=t=1∑N​(1+r)tPMT​+(1+r)NRV​​

Need: payments, residual value, asset fair value, lessor direct costs, term. Secured → usually cheaper than unsecured borrowing. Tax-adjust if lease interest is deductible.

  • International: debt cost in the currency of the cash flows; add a country risk premium from a country risk rating (economic, political, FX, market development). CRP = median rate for rating − benchmark country rate.

03The equity risk premium

Historical (ex post): 4 decisions

ChoiceGuidance
IndexBroad, cap-weighted
PeriodLong → lower standard error, but may be unrepresentative. Short → noisy
MeanArithmetic: single-period, consistent with CAPM, higher. Geometric: terminal wealth, less outlier-sensitive, generally preferred
rf proxyBill: truly riskless, but duration mismatch. Long bond YTM: matches equity's long life (industry practice), reinvestment risk
  • Normal upward curve: bill-based ERP is larger than bond-based. Arithmetic > geometric. Bill + arithmetic = the highest estimate.
  • Limitations: ERP non-stationary; survivorship bias (inflates; e.g. back-filled index history). One-off disasters in the sample bias it down.
  • Assumes stationary returns and efficient markets.

Forward-looking (ex ante)

F3

DDM-based ERP

ERP=V0​D1​​+g−rf​

Assumes earnings, dividends and price grow at g → constant P/E; adjust if multiples will expand/contract. Can add index buybacks to the yield.

F4

Grinold–Kroner

ERP=​DY+Δ(P/E)+i+g−ΔS−rf​​
DY
expected dividend yield
Δ(P/E)
expected repricing (often 0)
i
expected (long-term) inflation ≈ nominal − real (TIPS) yield
g
real EPS growth ≈ real GDP growth
ΔS
% change in shares (dilution); often 0

Best where listed equity is a large share of the economy. Use long-term inflation and the long bond yield for a long-horizon ERP.

  • Limitations: surveys → sampling, response, recency and confirmation bias (and track recent returns); DDM → constant P/E; macro models → model error, forecasting bias. Ex ante is less hit by non-stationarity.

04Cost of equity: public companies

F5

DDM & bond yield plus risk premium

re​=P0​D1​​+gP0​=t=1∑n​(1+re​)tDt​​+(1+re​)nPn​​re​=rd​+RP​

DDM needs a traded, stable dividend payer. Preferred: rp​=Dp​/Pp​ (g = 0). BYPRP: rd​ = YTM on long-term debt; RP (historical equity vs corporate bond return) is arbitrary; needs traded debt.

F6

CAPM & Fama–French

CAPM: re​FF3: re​FF5: re​​=rf​+βERP=rf​+β1​ERP+β2​SMB+β3​HML=FF3+β4​RMW+β5​CMA​
SMB
small minus big (size)
HML
high minus low book-to-market (value)
RMW
robust minus weak profitability
CMA
conservative minus aggressive investment

Beta from the market model: regress excess stock returns on excess index returns. Choices: index, period, rf proxy (bill rate gives a lower re​ in a steep normal curve). Negative factor beta (e.g. RMW) subtracts.

💡
Sanity check: common equity is junior to preferred, so re​ must exceed rp​ (and rd​). A CAPM estimate below the preferred yield isn't credible.

05Private & international adjustments

F7

Expanded CAPM vs build-up

re​=rf​+βERP+SP+IP+SCRPre​=rf​+ERP+SP+IP+SCRP​
SP
size premium (beta-adjusted; inversely related to size)
IP
industry risk premium
SCRP
specific-company risk: key person, customer/geographic concentration, governance, leverage, earnings volatility

Expanded CAPM: beta from public peers (unlever, relever). Build-up: implicit β = 1 (rf + ERP = average large-cap); use when no good comparables. Illiquidity → a valuation discount (DLOM), not an extra premium in re​.

F8

Unlever / relever beta

βA​βsubject​​=1+(1−tpeer​)D/Epeer​βpeer​​=βA​[1+(1−ts​)D/Es​]​

Use each firm's own tax rate and D/E (D/E = debt ÷ (assets − debt) from book data if no market values).

  • Historical SP may be overstated for a healthy small firm (small-cap sample includes fallen, distressed firms).
F9

Country risk premium

ERP=ERPdev​+λ×CRPCRP=Sov. spread×σbond​σequity​​​

λ = exposure to the local country (e.g. share of sales). Sovereign spread: local bond in developed-market currency − developed government yield; relative to the home country if home isn't the benchmark. Damodaran's σ-ratio needs local equity and bond return history.

F10

International CAPM

E(re​)=rf​​+βG​[E(rgm​)−rf​]+βC​[E(rc​)−rf​]​

rgm​ global index; rc​ wealth-weighted currency index. Low βG​ = less globally connected. Global CAPM (one global factor) can give a tiny or negative beta for EM stocks (low correlation). Developed-only operations → GCAPM/ICAPM fine; EM operations → CRP methods.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Back-filled index history (built from today's survivors) biases historical ERP up. A temporary war/crisis in the sample biases it down.
  • Inverted curve + short-rate-based ERP and rf → long-term re​ biased up.
  • Grinold–Kroner: use long-term inflation (not current) and the 10-yr yield (not the short rate). Include Δ(P/E) if given.
  • Average-risk stock: re​ ≥ long bond yield + ERP (β = 1).
  • Callable debt raises cost at issue; put and convertible features lower it. Cumulative preferred is cheaper than non-cumulative.
  • To cut new-debt cost: issue secured, putable or convertible debt.
  • Matrix pricing: average YTMs per maturity, then linearly interpolate to the target maturity; add a bit for illiquidity.
  • CRP with exposure: (local − home spread) × σE/σB × λ. Don't forget λ.
  • Build-up has no beta; expanded CAPM does. Neither adds an illiquidity premium.
  • Recurring (subscription) revenue and lower leverage than peers → lower SCRP; founder dependence and customer concentration → higher.
