Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases

In 30 seconds

  • Only cash dividends (and buybacks) move cash to shareholders. Stock dividends and splits just cut the pie into more slices.
  • Theories: MM irrelevance (perfect markets, homemade dividends), bird in the hand (dividends lower re​), tax argument (prefer gains if dividends taxed more).
  • Imperfections that make policy matter: signalling, agency costs, taxes, flotation costs, covenants.
  • Buyback ≡ cash dividend of equal amount for shareholder wealth, if tax and information content are the same.
  • Debt-funded buyback raises EPS only if earnings yield > after-tax cost of debt. Buyback above BVPS lowers BVPS.

01Forms of dividends & their effects

FormEffect
Regular cashImplied commitment; increases signal confidence. Cash ↓, equity ↓ → current & cash ratios ↓, D/E and D/A ↑
Special / extraOne-off; used by cyclicals to pay out only in good years without raising the regular rate
LiquidatingReturn of capital: wind-up, sale of a business, or payout > retained earnings
Stock dividendRE → contributed capital; total equity unchanged. No ratio effects
Split / reverse splitNo entries in equity accounts; per-share figures scale; P/E, yield, payout, market value unchanged
  • Ex-dividend date: first day the share trades without the dividend; price drops by ≈ the dividend.
  • Stock dividend: no wealth change, same proportionate ownership, total cost basis unchanged (basis per share falls), generally not taxable.
  • Claimed benefits of stock dividends: favour long-term holders, more float → liquidity, keep price in a "trading range".
  • 2-for-1 split ≡ 100% stock dividend economically; only the accounting differs.
  • Reverse split: raises a low price into a marketable range (institutional minimums, listing rules); common after distress. No wealth effect.

02Does dividend policy matter?

ViewArgument
MM: irrelevantNo taxes, no transaction costs, symmetric info, fixed investment policy. Investors build homemade dividends by selling shares. Dividends ≡ buybacks.
Bird in the handDividends less risky than future gains → lower re​ → higher price. MM: paying out doesn't change the risk of future cash flows.
Tax argumentIf dividends taxed above gains, taxable investors prefer low payout + buybacks (extreme: zero payout).
  • MM says dividend policy is irrelevant, not that dividends are irrelevant to value.
  • MM does not assume investors prefer dividends; that's the bird-in-the-hand view.
  • Homemade dividend breaks down with transaction costs, capital gains tax, volatile prices; new issues cost 4–10% in flotation costs.
  • Clientele with equal (e.g. zero) tax on dividends and gains: price falls by the full dividend ex-date.

03Signalling & agency costs

Signalling (asymmetric information)

  • Cash talks: dividends are a costly-to-mimic signal. A weak firm can't sustain a raised dividend long-term (borrowing only works short-term).
  • Initiation / increase → positive; associated with higher future earnings growth. Cut / omission → negative.
  • Exceptions: a cut can lift the price if the market sees it as prudent (cash preservation). An initiation can read as "growth is over" for a tech firm.
  • Better governance, disclosure and investor protection → weaker signalling role → fewer payers.

Agency costs

Managers vs shareholders: ↓

  • Paying out FCFE stops over-investment in negative-NPV empire building
  • Jensen's free cash flow hypothesis

Shareholders vs bondholders: ↑

  • Cash cushion falls, default risk rises, wealth shifts to equity
  • Fix: covenants capping distributions, min. coverage, max debt/tangible net worth
💡
Bigger price reaction to dividend news where the overinvestment risk is greater (lots of cash, few good projects).

04Factors in practice & tax systems

  • Investment opportunities: many good projects → low payout (internal equity is cheapest). Tech < utilities.
  • Earnings volatility: managers hate cuts → cautious increases when earnings are volatile.
  • Financial flexibility: cash buffer for downturns → cut/omit, or use buybacks (no commitment).
  • Taxes, flotation costs (avoid paying out then reissuing; worse for small firms), legal/contractual limits: impairment-of-capital rules, indenture covenants, preferred dividends (incl. cumulative arrears) first, mandated payouts in some countries.

Effective tax rate on a dividend

Double:CTR+(1−CTR)ITRSplit-rate:CTRD​+(1−CTRD​)ITRImputation:ITR​
CTR
corporate tax rate
CTRD
lower corporate rate on earnings distributed
ITR

Equivalently 1−(1−CTR)(1−ITR). Imputation (franking credit): corporate tax is credited to the holder, so the dividend is taxed once at the holder's rate; low-bracket holders get a refund. Assumes 100% payout.

  • Lower dividend tax doesn't guarantee a preference for dividends: gains tax is deferred until sale, and some jurisdictions step up basis at death.
  • Tax-exempt holders (pensions, endowments) are indifferent between dividends and gains.

Price > BVPS → BVPS falls; price < BVPS → BVPS rises; price = BVPS → unchanged. Equity falls by the cash paid at market price.

At market price, P is unchanged (≡ dividend). Above market (greenmail), remaining holders lose; total wealth is conserved.

  • Buyback with cash: assets ↓, equity ↓ → leverage ↑. Debt-funded → leverage ↑ more.
  • Authorisation ≠ commitment (unlike a declared dividend). Cash isn't distributed pro rata: only sellers receive it.

07Dividend vs buyback & dividend safety

💡
Equivalence: a buyback at market price = a cash dividend of the same amount for shareholder wealth, if taxes and information content are equal. With no taxes, they're identical.

Reasons to buy back

  • Tax: gains taxed lower than dividends; holders can also defer by not selling.
  • Signal that shares are undervalued (but could signal few positive-NPV projects).
  • Flexibility: no expectation of continuation; optional for holders. Can replace a special dividend in boom years.
  • Offset dilution from employee options; raise leverage toward target.
  • "Raise EPS" is a weak reason: higher leverage raises re​; value depends on FCF, not share count.
F6

Dividend safety ratios

Div. coverage=DividendsNI​FCFE coverage=Div.+BuybacksFCFE​​

Payout = 1 ÷ coverage. Mature firms: payout 40–60% (coverage ≈ 1.7–2.5×). Coverage ≈ 1.0 → dividend in jeopardy. FCFE coverage ≈ 1: returning all available cash; < 1: unsustainable (drawing down cash or borrowing).

  • Warning signs: high payout vs peers, coverage near 1, very high yield vs own history (market pricing a cut), borrowing or cutting capex to fund payouts, above-average leverage, a past record of cuts.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • Stock dividend: not taxable, total cost basis unchanged, proportionate ownership unchanged, current ratio unchanged. Cash dividend lowers the current ratio.
  • Stock dividend = RE → contributed capital. A split touches no equity account.
  • BVPS after buyback: subtract cash from book equity and use the reduced share count (common wrong answers use one but not the other).
  • Given P/E, convert: P/E 32 → E/P = 3.1% < 5% after-tax debt cost → EPS falls.
  • Paying dividends increases shareholder–bondholder conflict but decreases manager–shareholder conflict.
  • Effective tax (double): 1−(1−0.40)(1−0.30)=58%, not 40% + 30%.
  • Lintner: AF for a 5-year adjustment is 0.2; add ΔD to the previous dividend.
  • Only open-market buybacks let management time purchases to price dips.
  • Equivalence needs same cash, taxes and information, not just the same amount.
  • Stable policy: dividends grow with sustainable earnings, not a temporary spike; a constant-payout firm's dividend tracks each year's EPS.
