Standard II: Integrity of Capital Markets

In 30 seconds

  • II(A): if you hold material nonpublic information (MNPI), don't trade on it and don't cause others to trade on it, in any instrument (stocks, bonds, options, funds, ETFs).
  • Material = would move the price or matter to a reasonable investor. Nonpublic = not yet disseminated to the market at large.
  • Mosaic theory: public + nonmaterial nonpublic pieces → a material conclusion is fine. Document the research.
  • II(B): no information-based or transaction-based manipulation. Intent to mislead is the test.
  • Key firm controls: firewalls, watch / restricted / rumour lists, pre-clearance and surveillance.

01Material Nonpublic Information

II(A)

Material Nonpublic Information

If you possess MNPI that could affect an investment's value, you must not act or cause others to act on it.

Material?

  • Price impact likely, or a reasonable investor would want it before deciding. Judge by specificity, difference from public info, nature and reliability.
  • Examples: earnings, M&A, contracts won or lost, drug trial results, management or auditor changes, dividends, splits, buybacks, defaults, rating changes, large pending orders, government economic data.
  • Reliability of the source matters: an insider's fact → material; a competitor's speculation, rumours, whisper numbers → usually not.
  • Ambiguous price effect → less material. Time can make it immaterial.
  • A respected analyst's rating change can be material. Clients may get it first (they paid for it), but early access by a non-client who then trades → violation.

Nonpublic?

  • Public once disseminated to the market in general (press release, filing) and people can reasonably have received it; no need to wait for the slowest channel.
  • A disclosure to a room of analysts, a conference call or a small forum is not public dissemination.
  • Closed social media groups: confirm the info is also publicly available. Public blogs and company websites are public.
  • Inside info given for due diligence (M&A, underwriting, lending, ratings) may be used for that purpose only, never for trading.
💡
Mosaic theory: conclusions drawn from public info + nonmaterial nonpublic info (e.g. supplier or designer opinions) may be acted on, even if they'd be MNPI had the company said them directly. Save and document the research to show how you got there.
  • Expert networks: paying industry experts is fine, but you're responsible for not asking for or acting on confidential info (e.g. interim trial results). Signed NDAs/attestations don't protect you.
  • Losing money on the trade, or believing the tipper wouldn't break the law, is no defence.

02Handling MNPI: compliance practices

  • Public dissemination: make reasonable efforts, usually by urging the company to release it. Not required, and not if it would breach a duty of confidentiality or loyalty. If not possible: tell only compliance, and don't trade or change recommendations.
  • Don't induce insiders to disclose MNPI privately.
  • Urge companies to issue press releases before analyst meetings, to script calls, and to release promptly anything disclosed first in a meeting.
  • Firewalls (the main control): control of interdepartmental communications through a clearance area in compliance or legal; watch, restricted and rumour lists; documented procedures and enforcement; heightened review or restriction of proprietary trading while holding MNPI.
  • No staff overlap between investment banking/corporate finance and sales/research (or lending and trust/research). One side of the wall at a time.
  • An analyst who helps the bankers on a deal stays on the banking side until the info is public. Cross-wall sharing goes through compliance ("looking over the wall").
  • Restricted list when the firm has or may have MNPI. A permanent ban on covering banking clients is not needed: coverage can resume once the info is public.
  • Personal trading limits: monitoring, periodic reports, restricted and watch lists.
⚠️
Best policy to stop MNPI leaks between banking and research = firewall, not "monitor the exchanges" (no exchange should happen) and not a permanent ban. Holding MNPI on a merger? Put it on the restricted list; waiting a day or sharing with other PMs is wrong.

03Market Manipulation

II(B)

Market Manipulation

No practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with the intent to mislead market participants.

TypeExamples
Information-basedFalse rumours; pump and dump; speculative, sensationalised reports timed for a company's quiet period; unrealistic estimates spread to force guidance.
Transaction-basedTrades that fake activity or price moves (e.g. trading between your own funds to raise volume and price); a dominant position in an asset to move a related derivative, or vice versa.
  • Warning signs: very short-lived orders; orders above market; no change in beneficial ownership (wash trades); orders cancelled before execution to move the bid or offer; both sides on different platforms; coordination in a thin market.
  • Legitimate strategies that exploit inefficiencies are allowed, even if they look aggressive in illiquid markets. Intent decides.
  • A good motive (e.g. helping fund holders exit an illiquid stock) does not excuse manipulation.
  • Routine announcements (confirming guidance, merger talks) aren't manipulation. A personal order far above normal volume to lift the price is.
  • Fine: an estimate framed as a range with clear assumptions.
  • Practices: order-cancellation controls, cross-platform surveillance, pre-trade controls, algo controls, trade exception reports.

04Exam traps

⚠️
  • Both the tipper (passing MNPI to a broker or friend) and the tippee who trades violate II(A).
  • MNPI about one company → don't trade the sector ETF or fund that holds it either.
  • Takeover rumour from an unreliable source → not material → may trade.
  • Info revealed at a small group meeting is still nonpublic.
  • Mosaic = public + nonmaterial nonpublic; "material public + material nonpublic" is not mosaic.
  • Public dissemination: "make reasonable efforts", not "must achieve".
  • Overheard conversation from a reliable source with clear price impact → MNPI; don't act or recommend.
  • Taking a former employer's model is a IV(A) and I(C) problem, not II(A).
  • Information to a non-client stranger: no III(C) duty, but II(A) and III(E) still bite.
  • II(B) needs intent to mislead; a legitimate arbitrage strategy is fine.
