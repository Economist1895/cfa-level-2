Ethical and Professional Standards

Standard I: Professionalism

In 30 seconds

  • I(A): follow the stricter of applicable law and the Code; dissociate from others' violations.
  • I(B): no gifts or pressure that could compromise your own or another's independence. Avoid beats disclose.
  • I(C): no knowing misrepresentation, which includes omissions, guarantees and plagiarism.
  • I(D): dishonesty, fraud or deceit only matter when tied to professional conduct.
  • I(E): have and maintain role-specific competence; a bad outcome ≠ incompetence.

01Knowledge of the Law

I(A)

Knowledge of the Law

Know and comply with all applicable laws, rules and regulations (incl. the Code); if they conflict, follow the more strict. Don't knowingly participate or assist in violations, and dissociate from them.

  • More strict = more restrictive, or demands more action to protect clients. Law stricter → follow the law; otherwise (incl. no law) → follow the Code.
  • Conduct that is legal can still violate the Code. The law is the minimum.
  • Need only know laws that directly govern your role; you need not be a compliance expert (a PM need not know audit rules; a CFO must).
  • Which law applies depends on the facts: e.g. a home law that defers to the locality of business or the client's home country.
  • Building products: know the law where they are created and sold.
  • Legal advice doesn't absolve you: you still must comply.

Dissociation steps

  • Talk to the wrongdoer → escalate to a supervisor or compliance → if unresolved, dissociate: remove your name from reports, ask for a different assignment, refuse or drop the client; in extreme cases resign.
  • Document the violation. Inaction plus continued association can count as participation.
  • Reporting to regulators is not required unless the law requires it (it may be prudent). Reporting to CFA Institute is encouraged, not required.
💡
Compliance practices: stay informed (compliance, CE), keep current reference copies of rules, seek advice when in doubt, document violations when dissociating.

02Independence and Objectivity

I(B)

Independence and Objectivity

Use reasonable care and judgment to stay independent and objective. Don't offer, solicit or accept any gift, benefit or consideration that could reasonably be expected to compromise your own or another's independence.

  • Modest gifts and customary business entertainment are OK if not meant to influence or reward. Limit to token items even when the firm has no policy.
  • Client gifts are less of a threat (seen as extra compensation): disclose to employer before accepting, or afterwards if prior notice isn't possible. (Client gifts that compete with the employer → IV(B).)
  • Travel: best practice is commercial transport paid by you or your firm. Issuer-arranged travel only when commercial options don't exist (e.g. a remote site tour), and modest.
  • Investment banking pressure: may agree to cover a company, never promise a favourable rating. Fix: put the company on a restricted list and give only factual information; firewalls.
  • If a boss's constraints compromise you: decline coverage, or reach your own independent conclusion.
  • Issuer-paid research: allowed with a flat fee set in advance and full disclosure of the arrangement. No pay tied to conclusions, investor inflows or warrants.
  • Manager selection: hirers must not solicit gifts, including charity or political donations; managers must not offer them ("pay-to-play").
  • Credit ratings: watch for issuer-pays conflicts; may need to validate ratings independently.
  • Performance analysts: don't drop poorly performing accounts from composites, or change benchmarks, under internal pressure.
⚠️
"Best" response to a conflict = avoid it (pay your own way) > disclose it. Moving the coverage to a new analyst does not fix banking pressure. A private gift from a relative sent to the office is no conflict.

03Misrepresentation

I(C)

Misrepresentation

Don't knowingly make any misrepresentation relating to analysis, recommendations, actions or other professional activities.

  • Knowingly = you knew or should have known. Communications must be accurate, timely, complete, plain language; verbal, written and online.
  • Covers qualifications, firm services, performance records, investment characteristics. Overselling ("all the services you need") or exaggerating AI/ESG capability is a violation.
  • Guarantees: can't promise a return or no loss on a risky investment, or imply past returns will repeat. OK when the guarantee is built into the product (e.g. insured deposits).
  • Opinion stated as fact violates I(C) (and V(B)); add "in my opinion…".
  • Omissions: knowingly dropping negative scenarios or model inputs; cherry-picking accounts. Model outputs aren't facts.
  • Benchmarks: not required, but if a non-comparable reference index is used (e.g. cash-plus), disclose why. Don't switch pricing providers just to get a higher value.
  • Third-party material: once you use it, its errors are yours. Out-of-date fact sheets → misrepresentation.
  • Unintentional error: not a violation, but stop distribution and correct it. Repeatedly using wrong material you should have noticed → violation.

Plagiarism

  • Copying others' work, even slightly reworded, unnamed "leading analysts" quotes, forecasts without their caveats, charts with no source, proprietary models without permission.
  • Even standard definitions copied verbatim need citing. Testing or tweaking someone else's model still requires credit for the idea.
  • Outsourced research: may distribute it, but don't imply you wrote it.
  • Research by an analyst who has left the firm is firm property: the firm may reuse it unattributed, but you can't reissue it under your own name only.
  • Practices: keep copies of sources, attribute quotations and summaries (except recognised data services).

04Misconduct

I(D)

Misconduct

No professional conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, and no act that reflects adversely on professional reputation, integrity or competence.

  • Professional activity = work, academia or market participation, or anything using the CFA charter, membership or candidacy: society volunteering, CFA events, investment writing and blogs, dealings with regulators.
  • Violation: drunk at work; fraud at work; fraud as a charity board member appointed because of CFA status; harassment at an industry event; a retiree using fake statistics while citing the charter.
  • Not a violation: civil disobedience for personal beliefs; dishonesty unrelated to the profession; personal bankruptcy (unless it involved fraud).
  • Abuse of the PC Program to settle personal disputes is policed. Practice: firm code of ethics that every employee signs.

05Competence

I(E)

Competence

Act with and maintain the competence necessary for your professional responsibilities.

  • Competence = knowledge, skills and abilities for the specific role; facts and circumstances decide.
  • A bad outcome (a losing trade, a fraud nobody spotted) does not prove incompetence.
  • Education ≠ competence: passing a licence exam alone isn't enough; an expert auditor may not be competent on GIPS.
  • New role or product → get the new skills first (promotion to supervisor, crypto, ESG). Hiring a specialist to fill a gap is fine.
  • No mandated CE programme. Options: CE, certifications, conferences, employer training, self-study, expert groups.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • No law or a lax law: the Code applies, so MNPI is still off-limits.
  • Relying on counsel's opinion doesn't excuse a violation. Using a colleague's inflated numbers = assisting.
  • Banking asks for an upgrade: refuse. Reassigning the analyst doesn't fix it; the firm should stop issuing ratings or use a restricted list.
  • Paid speaker travel can be OK, but extras (free passes for colleagues, golf) are inducements.
  • Issuer-paid report with a bonus for investors attracted → I(B) violation.
  • Average client return driven by one client's trade made against your advice → misleading (I(C)).
  • "This fund will earn 10%" = opinion as fact; a factual description of a product's terms is fine.
  • Unknowing error is not a violation, but you must correct it once you discover it.
  • Conduct outside work violates I(D) only when it touches your professional role or CFA status.
  • Personal trades at better prices than clients get, hidden from the firm → III(A) + I(D); the supervisor who didn't enforce the rules → IV(C).
