Standard III: Duties to Clients

In 30 seconds

  • III(A): client first, then employer, then self. Identify the real client (plan beneficiaries, not the sponsor). Best execution; soft dollars only for research that helps the client; vote proxies.
  • III(B): treat clients fairly, not identically. Simultaneous dissemination; pro-rata allocation; you come last in hot issues.
  • III(C): advisers must know the client (IPS), update it at least annually and judge suitability on the total portfolio. Fund managers just follow the mandate.
  • III(D): performance must be fair, accurate and complete; GIPS is the best way.
  • III(E): keep current, former and prospective client info confidential, except for illegal activity, legal requirement or client consent.

01Loyalty, Prudence, and Care

III(A)

Loyalty, Prudence, and Care

Duty of loyalty to clients; act with reasonable care and prudent judgment; act for the client's benefit and put client interests before the employer's or your own.

  • Prudence = the care, skill and diligence of a reasonable person in a like role; follow the client's parameters and balance risk and return.
  • A minimum benchmark for everyone; it does not make every member a legal fiduciary, but legal fiduciary duties still apply if stricter.
  • Custody (any direct or indirect access to client funds) → heightened duty. Governing documents (trust deed, IMA) set your powers.
  • Execution-only: duty = best execution within the client's instructions. Limited-product advice: tell clients upfront it's proprietary products only.

Who is the client?

  • Pension or trust: the beneficiaries/participants, not the sponsor, management or shareholders. Takeover vote → judge long-term value for them.
  • Fund to a mandate or index: duty = follow the mandate. The adviser of a fund investor owes that investor loyalty; the fund manager doesn't.
  • Company executives owe duties to public shareholders.

Brokerage & proxies

  • Brokerage is the client's asset. Seek best price and best execution unless the client directs otherwise.
  • Soft dollars: only for research that directly helps investment decisions for the client. Not for operating costs, office expenses or education (e.g. CFA fees). Never pay more commission without a matching client benefit.
  • A broker with the lowest cost and best execution is fine even if its bundled research doesn't help that account (no extra client money spent).
  • Directed brokerage (client names the broker): no loyalty breach, but confirm it benefits the beneficiaries and disclose that best execution may suffer.
  • Don't pick a broker because it gives you personal favours. No churning to hit commission targets.
  • Proxies have economic value: vote in an informed way (non-routine issues especially); cost–benefit may justify not voting some. Disclose the proxy policy.
💡
Practices: quarterly itemised statements, disclose where assets are held, segregate client assets; when unsure, disclose in writing and get client approval; diversify; regular reviews; disclose all forms of manager compensation.

02Fair Dealing

III(B)

Fair Dealing

Deal fairly and objectively with all clients when giving analysis or recommendations, taking investment action or doing other professional work.

  • Fairly ≠ equally: premium service levels are OK if they don't disadvantage others, are disclosed and are available to all (not offered selectively).
  • Recommendations: all interested clients get a fair chance to act. Material changes go to all current clients, especially those who acted on the old view.
  • Client orders against a recently changed recommendation: first make sure they've received the change; if they still want it, execute.
  • IPOs / new issues: allocate to all suitable accounts; if oversubscribed, pro-rata in round lots, and you and your family get none. Family accounts managed like other fee-paying clients must be treated like them.
  • Allocate by client, not by PM; a minimum-lot rule that breaks strict pro-rata is fine.
  • Disclosing an unfair allocation method doesn't cure it; client consent can't override fairness.

Compliance practices

  • Disseminate on suitability and known interest, not favoured status; simultaneously; follow up individually only afterwards.
  • Limit who knows about a pending recommendation; shorten the gap between decision and release (publish a summary first); no pre-release trading or talk.
  • Keep a list of clients and holdings for notifying changes.
  • Trade allocation: time-stamp orders, FIFO, same price and commission for all accounts in a block, partial fills pro-rata, allocate before execution; disclose the procedures.
  • Supervisors run systematic account reviews; disclose service levels.
⚠️
Institutional clients before individuals, a lunch preview for a few clients, or priority for discretionary or flagship accounts → violations. Sending the report to all first, then discussing it with premium clients → fine.

03Suitability

III(C)

Suitability

In an advisory relationship: inquire into experience, risk/return objectives and constraints before acting, and update regularly; ensure suitability vs the client's written objectives; judge in the total portfolio context. Managing to a mandate: stay consistent with it.

  • IPS (best practice, written): client identification; return objectives and risk tolerance; constraints (liquidity, cash flows, investable funds, horizon, tax, legal, unique needs incl. ESG, proxy voting); benchmarks.
  • Review the IPS at least annually and before material changes; sooner after life events (an inheritance, health, dependants).
  • Risky or leveraged assets can suit a client within the whole portfolio. You judge only on what the client discloses.
  • Unsolicited unsuitable trade: hold off, discuss and educate, get a written acknowledgment that it's against your advice, update the IPS if material; if the client refuses repeatedly, reconsider the relationship.
  • Fund managers aren't responsible for whether the fund suits each investor; sell-side analysts and execution-only staff may have no suitability duty.
  • Suitability test: look beyond return to diversification impact, risk vs tolerance and fit with strategy.
⚠️
Violations: copying one client's strategy to another; buying illiquid lock-up assets for a liquid-only IPS; high-beta bets to chase a new bonus scheme; a zero-dividend stock in an income fund. Adding ESG criteria within IPS objectives is fine.

04Performance Presentation

III(D)

Performance Presentation

When communicating performance, make reasonable efforts to ensure it is fair, accurate and complete.

  • Covers past and expected performance, any account, composite or fund, and analysts touting their recommendation record (verify the numbers first).
  • Brief presentation: say it's limited and provide detail on request.
  • GIPS compliance is the best route. Without GIPS: tailor to audience sophistication; use composites, not one representative account; include terminated accounts; label simulated results, prior-firm records, and gross/net/after-tax; keep the supporting records.
  • Claiming GIPS → meet all requirements (e.g. asset-weighted composites).
  • Team results must be disclosed as team results (or show the part you managed). Using a prior employer's record needs disclosure.
  • Don't deviate from documented policies (cash flows, attribution) to flatter results; don't mix return types; don't extrapolate one good year ("steady 25%").

05Preservation of Confidentiality

III(E)

Preservation of Confidentiality

Keep information about current, former and prospective clients confidential unless it concerns the client's illegal activities, disclosure is required by law, or the client permits it.

  • Applies to info received because of the special relationship and relevant to that work.
  • Law can require disclosure, or forbid it even for illegal activity: follow the law and ask compliance or legal counsel first.
  • Good intentions (helping a charity) don't excuse disclosure. Telling a group about a charity's fundraising, without client data, is fine.
  • Vulnerable investors: get a secondary contact at account opening; limited, legally permitted disclosures about diminished capacity are OK; document everything. Sudden odd decisions alone don't mean lost capacity, so don't act on relatives' instructions.
  • Must cooperate with CFA Institute PC investigations (not a breach).
  • Practices: share only with colleagues working for the client; follow electronic security policies (no need to be an IT expert); confidentiality training incl. non-investment staff; safe communication channels.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Directed brokerage is not a loyalty breach; paying firm operating expenses or CFA fees with soft dollars is.
  • Voting the way management asked is fine if you reached it independently.
  • Company pension fund: loyalty is owed to participants and beneficiaries, not management or shareholders.
  • Guidance on the IPS comes from III(A) and III(C), not III(B).
  • IPO allocations by PM rather than pro-rata by client → III(B) violation; over-allocating to a favoured client too.
  • A suitability inquiry must precede any recommendation.
  • Known-unsuitable client request: pause, discuss, get a written acknowledgment, then update the IPS; don't just execute silently.
  • Showing team results as your own → III(D), even with the old employer's permission.
  • Disclosing a former client's data, even to benefit that client, needs permission.
  • Suspected illegal activity: check with compliance or counsel whether the law requires reporting.
