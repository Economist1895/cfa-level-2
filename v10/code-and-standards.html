Ethical and Professional Standards

Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct

In 30 seconds

  • The Code = 6 broad principles. The Standards = 7 groups (I–VII) of specific, enforceable rules.
  • They bind individual members and candidates, not firms. Firms may adopt them, but CFA Institute does not verify a firm's claim.
  • 2023 changes: new I(E) Competence; V(B) now requires disclosing the nature of services and costs; VI(A) renamed Avoid or Disclose Conflicts.
  • Enforcement: Professional Conduct (PC) staff investigate; Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC) panels hear contested cases.
  • Legal ≠ ethical: obeying the law is the floor, not the standard.

01The Code of Ethics (6 principles)

Members and candidates must:

  • Act with integrity, competence, diligence and respect, and ethically, towards the public, clients, prospects, employers, employees, colleagues and other market participants.
  • Place the integrity of the profession and clients' interests above their own.
  • Use reasonable care and independent professional judgment in analysis, recommendations, actions and other professional activities.
  • Practise, and encourage others to practise, professionally and ethically, so as to reflect credit on themselves and the profession.
  • Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society.
  • Maintain and improve their competence and strive to improve that of other professionals.
⚠️
Wording traps: the capital-markets principle ends "for the ultimate benefit of society"; a version without it is incomplete. Clients come before self, not "balanced with" self.

02The Standards at a glance

StandardCore rule
I(A) Knowledge of the LawKnow and obey all applicable law and the Code; follow the stricter; don't knowingly assist and dissociate from violations.
I(B) Independence & ObjectivityDon't offer, solicit or accept any benefit that could reasonably compromise your own or another's independence.
I(C) MisrepresentationNo knowing misrepresentation (incl. plagiarism, omissions).
I(D) MisconductNo dishonesty, fraud or deceit; nothing reflecting adversely on reputation, integrity or competence.
I(E) CompetenceAct with and maintain the competence your role needs.
II(A) MNPIDon't act or cause others to act on material nonpublic information.
II(B) Market ManipulationNo practices that distort prices or inflate volume with intent to mislead.
III(A) Loyalty, Prudence & CareClients before employer before self; reasonable care, prudent judgment.
III(B) Fair DealingTreat all clients fairly and objectively (not necessarily equally).
III(C) SuitabilityAdvisory: inquire before acting, update regularly, judge vs the total portfolio. Mandate: stay within the mandate.
III(D) Performance PresentationReasonable efforts for fair, accurate and complete performance.
III(E) ConfidentialityKeep current, former and prospective client info confidential unless illegal activity, required by law or client permits.
IV(A) Loyalty (employer)Act for employer's benefit; don't deprive it of your skills, leak confidential info or harm it.
IV(B) Additional Comp.No competing benefits without written consent from all parties.
IV(C) SupervisorsReasonable efforts to ensure supervisees comply with law and the Code.
V(A) Diligence & Reasonable BasisDiligence, independence, thoroughness; reasonable and adequate basis.
V(B) CommunicationDisclose services & costs, process (and material changes, promptly), risks & limitations, key factors; fact vs opinion.
V(C) Record RetentionKeep records supporting analysis, recommendations, actions and client communications.
VI(A) Avoid or Disclose ConflictsAvoid, or make full, fair, prominent, plain-language disclosure.
VI(B) Priority of TransactionsClient & employer trades before any in which you are beneficial owner.
VI(C) Referral FeesDisclose to employer, clients, prospects any benefit received from or paid to others for referrals.
VII(A) Conduct in CFA ProgramsDon't compromise the reputation of CFA Institute or the integrity, validity or security of its programs.
VII(B) Reference to CFADon't misrepresent or exaggerate membership, the designation or candidacy.
💡
Know which standard owns each duty: gifts → I(B) (not IV); suitability → III(C), not V; performance → III(D), not V; fact vs opinion and risk disclosure → V(B); reasonable basis → V(A); price distortion → II(B), not II(A).

032023 changes

I(E)

Competence (new)

Act with and maintain the competence needed for your professional responsibilities.

  • Makes explicit the Code's "act with … competence" and "maintain and improve competence".
  • Competence is role-specific; judged on the facts and circumstances, and the bar rises as the role expands.
  • No required CE programme or specific professional-development path.
V(B)

Communication (revised)

New first bullet: disclose the nature of the services provided and information about their costs to the client.

  • Fills a gap: process disclosure was already required, but its financial impact on the client was not.
  • Lets clients decide whether to engage the member and firm.
VI(A)

Avoid or Disclose Conflicts (renamed)

Formerly "Disclosure of Conflicts". Now avoid conflicts or disclose them fully and fairly; disclosures must be prominent, in plain language and effective.

  • Avoidance is preferred whenever reasonably possible; when avoidance isn't reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is still required.

04Professional Conduct Program

  • Applies to members and candidates for the CFA and CIPM designations. Board of Governors oversees; PC staff + DRC enforce under the Bylaws and Rules of Procedure.
  • Inquiry sources: (1) self-disclosure on the annual Professional Conduct Statement (litigation, criminal investigation, written complaint); (2) written complaints; (3) media, regulatory notices or other public sources; (4) exam monitoring (test centres, score analysis, social media).
  • Investigation: may request a written explanation, interview the member, complainants and third parties, and collect documents.
  • Outcomes: close with no sanction, a cautionary letter, or proceed to discipline. Member may accept or reject the charges and proposed sanction.
  • Rejected → referred to a DRC hearing panel. Panels also review accepted sanctions for reasonableness.
  • Sanctions: public censure, suspension or revocation of membership and right to use the CFA designation; candidates may be suspended or barred from the program.
⚠️
Not every case goes to a DRC panel: only contested ones get a hearing. Inquiries can start from media reports.

05Adoption & why ethics matters

  • Firms or non-members may claim compliance once their code meets the principles, using the statement: "[Name] claims compliance with the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct. This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute."
  • The Asset Manager Code is for firms, in six areas: loyalty to clients, investment process, trading, compliance, performance, disclosure.
  • Trust drives capital markets: low trust → less investment or a higher risk premium → less innovation and growth.
  • Law vs ethics: law = what is required; ethics = what is morally right. Regulation alone is insufficient (gaps, under-resourced enforcement); firms need a culture of integrity, led by senior management.
  • Ethics are universal and override local customs. Consider market sustainability: individually harmless acts can add up to a crisis.
  • The Standards evolve over time to stay relevant; they are not unchanged since the 1960s.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • "Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets" is incomplete: it must end "for the ultimate benefit of society".
  • Accepting gifts that compromise independence → Standard I(B), not Standard IV.
  • IV(B) consent must be written and from all parties, not the employer only.
  • Causing others to act on MNPI violates II(A); distorting prices is II(B).
  • Under a mandate, stay consistent with the mandate; you need not judge whether the mandate suits all investors.
  • Suitability inquiry comes before acting, not after; clients come before employer, not equal.
  • "Reasonable and adequate basis" belongs to V(A), never to confidentiality.
  • Risks and limitations, prompt disclosure of material process changes, and fact vs opinion → V(B).
  • Supervisors' duty goes beyond detection and prevention: it covers broader compliance.
  • Standard VII covers CFA programs and references to CFA only, not loyalty or supervision.
