Ethical and Professional Standards

Standard V: Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions

In 30 seconds

  • V(A): be diligent, independent and thorough, and have a reasonable and adequate basis. Check third-party research, understand and stress-test your models, vet external managers fully.
  • V(B): tell clients the services and costs, the process (and material changes), the risks and limitations and the key factors; separate fact from opinion.
  • V(C): keep records that support research and actions. They belong to the firm. Default: 7 years if no rule says otherwise.
  • A good process doesn't guarantee a good outcome: losses alone aren't violations.

01Diligence and Reasonable Basis

V(A)

Diligence and Reasonable Basis

Exercise diligence, independence and thoroughness in analysis, recommendations and actions, and have a reasonable and adequate basis supported by appropriate research.

  • The level of diligence depends on the philosophy, your role and the firm's resources, and on the product.
  • Inputs to weigh: macro conditions, company history, industry and cycle, a fund's fees and manager history, model limitations, securitised asset quality, peer-group fit.
  • Short cuts (e.g. pricing new issues only by issuer size) → insufficient diligence. Update reports when material new information arrives.
  • An overheard opinion is not a reasonable basis unless it prompts your own research.

Secondary & third-party research

  • Secondary = by others in your firm; third-party = from outside. Make reasonable efforts to confirm it's sound: assumptions, rigour, timeliness, objectivity and independence.
  • Suspect bias or unreliability → don't rely on it. Blogs and social media need more scrutiny.
  • You may rely in good faith on your firm's adequate vendor due diligence.

Quant models

  • No need to be a technical expert, but understand the assumptions and limitations and how output is used; test output before use.
  • Model builders need more diligence than users and must test fully before release, even if they don't trade.
  • Use a broad range of scenarios including negative events and data outside the historical sample.
  • Adding new factors without testing their effect → no reasonable basis.

External advisers & group research

  • Review sub-advisers as diligently as securities: code of ethics, compliance and controls, quality of returns data, process and style adherence. Not on fees alone, nor performance alone. Criteria: Asset Manager Code, GIPS, Model RFP.
  • Choosing a service provider needs an assessment of its methods, not just price or a demo.
  • Group report: if you disagree but the consensus has a reasonable basis and is objective, you need not dissociate.
💡
Practices: written due-diligence policy; measurable criteria for research quality and recommendation accuracy; minimum scenario testing for models; criteria for outside data providers and sub-advisers, with review frequency.

02Communication with Clients

V(B)

Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients

Disclose (1) the nature of services and their costs; (2) the basic format and principles of the investment process, and promptly any material changes; (3) significant risks and limitations; (4) use judgment to include the important factors; (5) distinguish fact from opinion.

Services & costs (2023)

  • What you will and won't do, and the costs the client pays, including affiliate and third-party costs (e.g. compensation your firm receives from a fund).
  • No exact dollar amounts needed (asset-based fees can't be known in advance); no need to disclose the firm's own costs.
  • Ongoing: update affected clients promptly when services or fees change (incl. fee calculation methodology).
  • Can't skip it because a client is sophisticated, but you may tailor it. Best practice: in writing at the start.

Process, risks & factors

  • Describe the basic process (not every detail); disclose use of external managers.
  • Material changes → tell existing clients, not just prospects: a new valuation model, a switch to quant selection, a style or mandate change, moving to external managers.
  • Risks: market, complex instruments, leverage (always significant), counterparty, country, sector, credit. Limitations: liquidity and capacity; disclose a capacity limit even if it's not yet reached.
  • Only risks known at the time; an unknown risk may be a V(A) diligence gap, not V(B).
  • VaR or model outputs: explain their input sensitivity and limits.
  • Reports can focus on some areas if the scope limits are stated; give quant references and disclose methodology changes; abbreviated lists should say more detail is available.
  • Fact vs opinion: forecasts, dividend changes and price targets are opinions; separate fact from statistical conjecture.
⚠️
Firm-written disclosures don't excuse you: if they're inadequate, alert the firm, supplement them, or document objections and dissociate. V(B) duties fall on client-facing members only.

03Record Retention

V(C)

Record Retention

Develop and maintain appropriate records supporting your analysis, recommendations, actions and investment communications with clients and prospects.

  • Covers decisions to trade and reviews that led to no change.
  • Examples: meeting notes, company releases, model inputs and outputs, risk analyses, external-manager selection criteria, client meeting notes, third-party research used.
  • Format doesn't matter: emails, texts, blog and social media posts count.
  • Records are firm property, even if made after hours or on a personal computer. On leaving: no copies without consent; delete files kept at home unless permitted.
  • Reusing old research at a new firm → recreate supporting records from sources not taken from the old firm.
  • Follow regulatory and firm periods; if none, at least 7 years. Firm bears primary responsibility, but archive your own notes.

04Exam traps

⚠️
  • Sound mosaic analysis meets V(A); trading after getting MNPI breaks II(A), not V(A).
  • Disagreeing with a team report with a reasonable basis → no need to dissociate.
  • Changing a rating on an overheard remark → V(A) violation (no basis).
  • "Price will rise because the dividend will increase" when it depends on uncertain legislation → opinion as fact (V(B)).
  • Dropping a non-critical quant appendix from a fundamental report isn't a violation; switching the basis to quant is a process change to disclose.
  • Undisclosed fees your firm earns from a recommended fund → V(B), even for sophisticated clients.
  • Telling only prospects (not existing clients) about a process change → violation.
  • Picking a sub-adviser on fees alone → V(A).
  • Taking work files home after leaving, without permission → V(C) (and IV(A)).
