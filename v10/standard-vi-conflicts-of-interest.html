Standard VI: Conflicts of Interest

In 30 seconds

  • VI(A): avoid conflicts where reasonably possible; otherwise make full, fair, prominent, plain-language disclosure to clients, prospects and the employer.
  • VI(B): clients first, employer second, you last in any security you beneficially own. No front-running.
  • VI(C): disclose any referral benefit received or paid, including internal ones, before the client signs up: its nature and estimated value.
  • Classic conflicts: issuer relationships (underwriting, market-making, directorships), stock ownership, pay structures, family holdings.

01Avoid or Disclose Conflicts

VI(A)

Avoid or Disclose Conflicts

Avoid, or fully and fairly disclose, all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity or interfere with duties to clients, prospects and employer. Disclosures must be prominent, in plain language and effective.

  • Hierarchy: avoid (incl. the appearance) → if not reasonable, disclose clearly and mitigate, and say how.
  • You decide how often and how to disclose. Update when a conflict changes materially (e.g. annual → quarterly bonuses). Err on the side of caution.
  • Compensation is a common source: short-term incentives that ignore long-term value. Changes that clash with client IPSs must be disclosed.
  • Third-party payments for selling a product (e.g. fund fee rebates) must be disclosed.

To employers

  • Report promptly, with enough detail to assess: stock you own and cover, outside board seats, financial pressures, personal relationships (e.g. with a manager you hire or fire).
  • Follow employer bans on personal trading or board service even where no real conflict exists.

To clients

  • Always avoid or disclose: directorships or consultancies, investment banking/underwriting and financial relationships, market-making, material beneficial ownership.
  • Beneficial ownership = direct or indirect pecuniary interest, or power to vote or dispose (a spouse's shares count).
  • Disclose your firm's known conflicts too; take reasonable steps to find them. Also non-standard fees and sub-advisory deals.
  • Cross-departmental: pressure from sales, banking or issuers; broker-sponsored VC partnerships promoted after IPO.
  • Board service: 3 conflicts: duties to shareholders vs clients; pay in company shares or options; access to MNPI. Wall the director off from investment decisions.
⚠️
Best practice for an analyst with a conflict (spouse owns the stock, firm owns it, firm's officers sit on the board) is to not cover it or reassign. If you do write it, disclose every conflict in the report; not expressing an opinion doesn't remove the duty. Using the company's products, or a remote relative's link, isn't a conflict.

02Priority of Transactions

VI(B)

Priority of Transactions

Client and employer transactions must take priority over transactions in which you are the beneficial owner.

  • Order: clients → employer (firm account) → personal. Trade only after clients and employer have had an adequate opportunity to act.
  • Personal trading is fine if (1) clients aren't disadvantaged, (2) you don't profit from client trades and (3) you follow regulations. Co-investing with clients can align interests.
  • Selling against your recommendation for personal reasons (e.g. needing cash) can be justified if clients aren't disadvantaged. No need to match personal and client trades.
  • Covers accounts of immediate family and any account where you have a direct or indirect interest. Don't pass on or profit from knowledge of pending client trades.
  • Family as clients: fee-paying family accounts are treated like any other client, neither favoured nor disadvantaged.
  • The firm's own account trading options before a report is released → violation (clients first).

Compliance practices

  • IPOs: avoid, or pre-clear even with no apparent conflict. Private placements: strict limits (they can look like favours).
  • Blackout periods to prevent front-running.
  • Reporting: holdings at hire and periodically, duplicate confirms and statements to the firm, pre-clearance of trades.
  • Disclose personal trading policies to investors on request; boilerplate isn't enough.

03Referral Fees

VI(C)

Referral Fees

Disclose to employer, clients and prospects, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration or benefit received from or paid to others for recommending products or services.

  • Lets them judge partiality and the full cost of the service.
  • Timing: before any formal agreement for services.
  • Content: nature (flat or %, one-off or ongoing, performance-based, research, non-cash) plus the estimated dollar value. Cash, soft dollars or in kind.
  • Internal referral pay between departments counts too.
  • Fees paid to a consultant after a recommended firm wins business must be disclosed.
  • Practice: approved programmes; report to the employer at least quarterly.

04Exam traps

⚠️
  • Post-2023: "disclose" alone isn't the full answer; avoiding is preferred whenever reasonable.
  • Firm's own holding in the covered stock → disclose (or don't cover).
  • Underwriter relationships must be disclosed in research reports.
  • Extra pay from an issuer for selling its stock → disclose to clients (VI(A)), and get employer consent (IV(B)).
  • Buying for your spouse before your fund → VI(B) violation; also tell the employer.
  • Leaving parents' fee-paying accounts out of a good trade → VI(B) violation.
  • Personal trades ahead of clients plus missing trade reports → VI(B), and the supervisor IV(C).
  • VI(C) applies to referrals within the firm and to fees paid by you, not just received.
