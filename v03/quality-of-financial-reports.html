Financial Statement Analysis

Evaluating Quality of Financial Reports

In 30 seconds

  • Two questions: is reporting GAAP-compliant and decision-useful? Are results (earnings) high quality, i.e. sustainable and returning ≥ cost of capital?
  • High reporting quality is necessary to judge earnings quality but doesn't guarantee good earnings.
  • Earnings with big accruals are less persistent and mean-revert faster; cash earnings persist.
  • Screens: Beneish M-score (manipulation; higher = worse, cutoff −1.78) and Altman Z-score (bankruptcy; higher = safer, < 1.81 distress).
  • BS quality = completeness, unbiased measurement, clear presentation. Audit opinions are rarely a timely risk signal; auditor changes, NT filings and CFO exits are.

01Conceptual framework

  • Reporting quality: the information is relevant and faithfully represents reality. Earnings (results) quality: the underlying performance is sustainable and adequate.
  • Low earnings quality can sit with high reporting quality (e.g. honest reporting of a one-off gain). Poor results do motivate misreporting.

Quality spectrum (best → worst)

  • 1. GAAP, decision-useful, sustainable & adequate returns
  • 2. GAAP, decision-useful, but low earnings quality (not sustainable / inadequate)
  • 3. GAAP, but biased choices
  • 4. GAAP, but earnings management (real or accounting)
  • 5. Non-compliant accounting (can't assess earnings)
  • 6. Fictitious transactions or omissions (fabrication)
  • Aggressive = boosts current performance/position (and may hurt later periods). Conservative = lowers current (and may flatter later periods). Both are bias.
  • Smoothing = under-reporting in good times, over-reporting in bad times → GAAP-compliant earnings management.
  • Bias can also be in presentation (burying bad news, highlighting good).
  • New management, or a firm in trouble, may bias income down now ("big bath") to flatter later periods.

02Potential problems & warning signs

Amounts & timing (A − L = E)

  • Aggressive/fictitious revenue → income, equity and receivables overstated (and inventory understated).
  • Delayed or omitted expenses → income and equity overstated; assets overstated (low bad-debt → AR; low depreciation → PP&E) or liabilities understated (accrued interest, tax).
  • OCF can be pumped by stretching payables, speeding up collections, delaying inventory purchases, maintenance or R&D.

Classification

  • BS: moving AR to long-term or notes receivable, selling it or parking it in a controlled entity → lower AR, flattering DSO and turnover. Inventory moved to "other assets" breaks the turnover time series.
  • IS: non-operating gains called operating, ordinary expenses called non-recurring, gains through NI but losses through OCI.
  • CF: asset sales put in CFO; capitalising costs moves the outflow to CFI.

M&A

  • Acquiring a firm with healthy cash flow hides the acquirer's weak CFO (the acquired CFO lands in consolidated CFO; the price is in CFI, or nowhere if paid in stock). No "with vs without" disclosure is required.
  • Stock-paying acquirers and targets may inflate earnings pre-deal. Misreporters acquire more often, to hurt comparability.
  • Under-valuing amortisable intangibles → more goodwill (not amortised) → earnings overstated until impaired.

GAAP but not economic reality

  • Impairments & restructuring: if recurring, normalise (spread over past and current periods). If truly one-off, exclude.
  • Sudden jumps in allowances/reserves or big loss accruals → prior earnings were probably overstated. Revisions to asset lives raise the question of whether they should have come earlier.
  • Unrecognised assets: R&D (US GAAP expenses; IFRS capitalises development only if all 6 criteria are met) and order backlog.
  • OCI items the analyst may pull into income: FVOCI equity gains/losses, PP&E revaluation (IFRS only), CTA, pension remeasurements, cash flow hedges.

03Evaluation steps & the Beneish model

  • 1. Understand the company & industry and the accounting norms of peers.
  • 2. Learn about management: incentives, compensation, insider sales, related parties.
  • 3. Identify significant accounting areas (judgement-heavy).
  • 4. Compare: this year vs last year's report, policies vs peers, ratios vs peers.
  • 5. Check warning signs: falling AR turnover (fictitious/premature revenue, thin allowance), falling inventory turnover (obsolescence), NI > CFO (aggressive accruals).
  • 6. Multi-segment firms: watch for sales/costs shifted into the "hot" segment (strong segment, flat total).
  • 7. Use quantitative tools.

04Earnings quality indicators

  • Recurring earnings: strip out discontinued ops and one-offs (asset sales, litigation, tax settlements). More one-offs = lower quality.
  • Classification shifting (total NI unchanged): ordinary costs moved into "special items" or discontinued ops → inflated core earnings. Scrutinise income-decreasing special items, especially when they help beat forecasts.
  • Non-GAAP / pro forma earnings must be reconciled to reported; check that the excluded items really are non-recurring.
  • Core pre-tax earnings = reported pre-tax + non-recurring expenses (leave recurring non-operating costs in).

Earnings persistence

Higher β1​ = more persistent. Empirically β1​>β2​: the cash component persists more than accruals.

  • Non-discretionary accruals come from normal business; discretionary accruals come from unusual choices. Model normal accruals (regress on credit-sales growth, depreciable assets); the residual = abnormal accruals → focus here.
  • Simple screen: total accruals scaled by average assets or NOI across firms.
  • Positive NI with negative CFO is a strong red flag. But CFO > NI is no all-clear: capitalising operating costs moves outflows to CFI, inflating CFO; some frauds were built to generate CFO.
  • Mean reversion: extreme earnings and returns (high or low) revert toward normal through competition and restructuring. A big accrual component (especially outlier accruals) → faster reversion. Don't extrapolate extremes; forecast normalised earnings.
  • Benchmarks: consistently just meeting or narrowly beating consensus is a possible sign of management (debated).
  • External confirmations (enforcement actions, restatements) come too late to be very useful.

Revenue & expense red-flag checks

  • Revenue: shipping terms, return rights, rebates, multiple deliverables (shifting value to the earlier element; deferred revenue on BS?). Channel stuffing (incentives to over-order, often with returns), bill-and-hold (needs a buyer request, business purpose and transfer of risk).
  • Trend and peer-compare DSO and AR/revenue; tie revenue to non-financial data (stores, capacity, headcount); watch related-party sales.
  • Costs: check capitalisation and depreciation policies vs peers. Red flags: non-current assets building up while margins hold in a weak industry; falling asset turnover with steady revenue; falling depreciation/asset base; capex/gross PP&E rising.
  • Related parties: tunnelling (wealth moved out to manager-owned entities) vs propping (resources moved in to keep the firm alive).

06Cash flow & balance sheet quality

High-quality OCF (established firm)

  • Positive, from sustainable sources, enough to cover capex, dividends and debt repayments, with low volatility vs peers. (Start-ups are expected to have negative CFO and CFI.)
  • Low quality = honestly reported bad performance or misreporting.
  • Timing: selling receivables or stretching payables boosts CFO → DSO falls, days payable rises. Check activity ratios and the notes.
  • Classification shifting into CFO changes the mix, not the total. Trading securities → CFO; non-trading → CFI, and management decides which is which. Compare each year's restated prior year to the original.

Balance sheet quality

  • Results quality = optimal leverage, adequate liquidity, good asset allocation. Reporting quality = the three below.
  • Completeness: off-BS obligations (take-or-pay purchase contracts → constructively capitalise at PV). Equity-method / unconsolidated JVs hide liabilities and overstate return on sales (profit share counted without the sales) → adjusting lowers the margin. Many ~50%-owned unconsolidated subs = warning.
  • Unbiased measurement: impairments (inventory obsolescence, PP&E), goodwill with MV equity < BV equity, DTA valuation allowance swings, Level 3 investments, pension discount rate.
  • Clear presentation: aggregation choices; read the notes (e.g. LIFO inventory under inflation is unlikely to be overstated).

07Sources of information about risk

  • Financial statements: leverage and coverage (financial risk), volatile CFO and falling margins (operating risk), Z and M models.
  • Audit opinion: historical and rarely timely. A negative, going-concern or ICFR-weakness opinion is a warning, but often too late. Auditor changes (shopping), an undersized auditor or doubts about independence are better signals.
  • Notes: contingencies (amount, timing, uncertainty), pensions, financial instrument risks (rate, FX sensitivities).
  • Management commentary / MD&A: IFRS practice statement (non-binding) has 5 elements (nature of business; objectives & strategy; resources, risks & relationships; results & prospects; performance measures). US MD&A covers liquidity, capital resources, results, off-BS and contractual arrangements; market risk goes in Item 7A. Watch for generic boilerplate risks.
  • Event disclosures (8-K; ad hoc EU releases): NT late-filing notices (highly likely to signal reporting problems), sudden CFO or auditor resignation, legal disputes, M&A.
  • Financial press: can uncover issues first. Always confirm with filings and consider the source's credibility and bias.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • Shortening asset lives is conservative, not a warning sign. Falling discounts/returns are fine; rising ones are the warning.
  • Rising inventory or AR turnover is not a red flag; falling turnover is. NI > CFO is.
  • Operating income below CFO is not the warning; AP up while AR & inventory fall is (CFO pumped).
  • Revenue growing faster than receivables is fine; receivables outgrowing revenue is the flag. Flat margins are fine; rising margins are the flag.
  • Persistence: βCF​>βAccruals​. Higher cash share → ROE reverts more slowly.
  • Core pre-tax = reported + non-recurring expenses only (e.g. 5.4 + 1.2 + 0.5 = 7.1).
  • Misstatement NI impact is after tax: (50 revenue + 100 capitalised cost) × (1 − 25%) = 112.5.
  • M-score: the highest (least negative) is the likeliest manipulator, above −1.78. Change in M is irrelevant to the ranking.
  • Inflated sales (channel stuffing) → NI and AR overstated, inventory understated.
  • Obsolete inventory not written down = unbiased measurement failure (not completeness). Smoothing within GAAP = GAAP-compliant with earnings management.
  • Understated intangibles + no goodwill impairment → earnings overstated. Accrual follow-up → discretionary accruals. AR shifted to long-term → DSO falls (false comfort).
