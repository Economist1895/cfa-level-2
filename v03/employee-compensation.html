Employee Compensation: Post-Employment and Share-Based

In 30 seconds

  • Core principle: expense the fair value of compensation in the period the employee provides service (≈ as it vests).
  • Share-based: grant-date FV, expensed over the vesting period, offset to equity. Never remeasured. Non-cash but a real cost (dilution).
  • DB pension: balance sheet shows funded status (plan assets − obligation). IFRS P&L = service cost + net interest; remeasurements → OCI.
  • Valuation: deduct SBC and pension service cost from FCF; treat an underfunded plan as debt; ignore an overfunded one.

  • Other types: long-term benefits (> 12 months, e.g. sabbatical), termination benefits (often in "restructuring charges").
  • Expense is reported by function (R&D, SG&A…), not as a separate line. Manufacturing labour is capitalised to inventory → COGS on sale.
  • Cash-settled share awards → accounted like short-term benefits (liability).

Share-based: pros

  • Aligns employees with shareholders
  • Retention (multi-year vesting)
  • No cash outlay → preserves liquidity

Share-based: cons

  • Employee has little control over share price
  • Shares → too little risk-taking; options → too much (upside-only payoff)
  • Dilution; implicit cash cost (buybacks)

04Shares outstanding & diluted EPS

F4

Treasury stock method for awards

Dilutive shares=N−PˉProceeds​Proceeds=​N×K(0 for RSUs)+avg. unrecognised SBC​​
N
unvested RSUs / options outstanding
Pˉ
average share price for the period
Avg. unrec.
(beginning + ending unrecognised SBC expense) ÷ 2, at grant-date FV

Negative result → anti-dilutive, excluded. Only awards likely to vest are included (unmet performance conditions excluded).

  • Basic shares rise only when awards settle (RSUs vest, options exercised). Forfeited RSUs don't reduce basic shares.
  • Options: in the money → dilutive; ATM / OTM → anti-dilutive.
  • RSUs are dilutive unless the price has fallen well below the grant price.
  • Lower average price → more assumed buybacks → fewer dilutive shares. Higher price → more dilution.
  • Net loss → all awards anti-dilutive, so basic = diluted.
  • For valuation, add back anti-dilutive securities (from the notes), especially for loss-makers and after big price drops. Conservative option: basic + gross awards.

05Modelling & valuing share-based comp

  • Forecast SBC as % of revenue, as a separate line (it falls as % of revenue as firms mature). Strip it out of functional cost lines first.
  • Integrate: expense offset to equity (or the BS won't balance), add back in indirect CFO.
  • Basic shares (end) = beginning + RSUs vested / options exercised + other issuance − buybacks. New RSU shares ≈ SBC expense ÷ share price.
  • Diluted shares: assume a historical % of outstanding awards is dilutive.
  • DCF: existing awards → use the diluted (plus anti-dilutive) share count. Future awards → deduct SBC from FCF (pragmatic), or raise the share count / apply a dilution factor.
  • Multiples: GAAP and non-GAAP (SBC-excluded) multiples aren't comparable. Be consistent across peers.
  • SBC-heavy firms have flattered FCF (higher FCF margin, lower P/FCF) purely from paying in shares, not from being cheaper.

06Post-employment benefits

  • DC: employer pays an agreed contribution. Employee bears investment & actuarial risk. Expense = contribution; CFO outflow; no further obligation.
  • DB: defined future benefit (e.g. % of final salary × years). Employer bears investment & actuarial risk. Anything not explicitly DC (incl. OPEB, often unfunded) uses DB accounting.
  • Closed plan: no new entrants. Frozen: no further accrual for current members.
F5

Funded status (on the balance sheet)

Funded status=FVplan assets​−PBO
PBO
PV of benefits earned to date, discounted at the high-quality corporate bond yield

Negative → net pension liability; positive → net pension asset. Net presentation is allowed, but separate plans can't be netted against each other. Benefits paid cut assets and PBO equally → no effect on funded status.

F7

US GAAP periodic pension cost

P&L=​Current service cost+r×PBObeg​−E(R)×Plan assetsbeg​+amort. of past service cost+amort. of net actuarial loss​
  • Past service cost → OCI, then amortised over remaining service lives (IFRS: P&L immediately).
  • Actuarial G/L and actual − expected return → P&L immediately, or (usually) OCI then amortised.
  • Corridor: amortise only the excess of cumulative unrecognised G/L over 10% of the greater of PBO or plan assets (beginning), over avg. remaining working lives.

07Pension modelling & valuation

  • Model service cost, net interest, remeasurements and contributions. Skip detail if the net liability is small (< ~5% of market cap), especially if closed/frozen.
  • Underfunded status → treat as debt in the EV → equity bridge (deduct the net liability, not the gross PBO).
  • Overfunded → ignored: plan assets can't be withdrawn for shareholders.
  • Service cost: keep expensed in FCF (don't add back). Net interest: exclude from the DCF; it's discount unwinding already captured by deducting the liability.
  • Large DB sponsors are hurt by falling rates (PBO ↑), partly offset if assets are long-duration bonds.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • SBC expense uses grant-date FV. A later share price change doesn't touch past grants' expense.
  • Option exercise: CFF inflow = strike × number. No income statement effect.
  • Windfall: IFRS → equity; US GAAP → income tax expense. Don't mix them up.
  • Treasury stock method uses the average price and includes unrecognised SBC in proceeds. Net loss → no dilution.
  • IFRS net interest = r × net liability (beginning). US GAAP: interest on gross PBO and a separate expected return.
  • IFRS: past service cost → P&L now; remeasurements → OCI forever. US GAAP: both → OCI, then amortised.
  • Expected return on plan assets doesn't affect the PBO, only US GAAP pension cost.
  • Funded status change: compare (service + interest cost) with (actual return + contributions). Benefits paid cancel out.
  • Valuation: deduct the net pension liability as debt; ignore a surplus; deduct SBC and service cost from FCF; exclude net interest.
  • Indirect CFO: add back SBC; pension contributions (not expense) are the CFO outflow.
