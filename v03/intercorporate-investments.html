Intercorporate Investments

In 30 seconds

  • Degree of influence (not just % held) sets the method: none → financial asset; significant → equity method; control → consolidation; joint control → equity method.
  • Net income to parent is the same under equity method, proportionate consolidation and full consolidation. Only line items and ratios differ.
  • Equity method = "one-line consolidation": cost + share of NI − dividends − amortisation of excess price.
  • Acquisition method: 100% of target's assets & liabilities at fair value; goodwill is the residual, never amortised, tested for impairment.
  • Key IFRS vs US GAAP splits: partial goodwill (IFRS option), impairment test and reversal, contingent liabilities, VIE rules.

Significant influence evidence: board seat, policy-making role, material intercompany transactions, interchange of managers, technological dependency. A 15% holder with a board seat → equity method.

IFRS 9 classification

  • Amortised cost (debt only): passes the business model test (held to collect contractual cash flows) and the cash flow test (solely payments of principal and interest).
  • FVOCI (debt): hold to collect and sell. FV changes to OCI, recycled to P&L on sale.
  • FVPL: everything else, held-for-trading, derivatives, or elected to avoid an accounting mismatch.
  • Equity: never amortised cost. Trading → FVPL. Others: irrevocable election of FVOCI (only dividends hit P&L) or FVPL.
  • Reclassification: equity never. Debt only if the business model changes (rare); prospective, no restatement. To FVPL → gain/loss in P&L; to amortised cost → FV at that date becomes carrying amount.
  • Impairment: expected credit loss model (12-month ECL for performing assets, lifetime ECL for non-performing), replacing incurred loss.
  • US GAAP is similar but labels debt as held-to-maturity (amortised cost), available-for-sale (FV via OCI) and trading (FV via NI).

Deferred=s​×Profit on sale×% not yet resold​

Deducted from equity income this year; added back in the year the goods are sold to third parties. Same treatment for upstream (associate → investor, profit in associate's NI) and downstream (investor → associate, profit in investor's NI).

⚠️
Trap: eliminate only the investor's share s of the unrealised profit, not 100%. Profit = sale price − cost; % unsold = inventory still held ÷ total bought. (Under full consolidation, intercompany items are eliminated in full.)

04Business combinations

  • US GAAP labels: merger (A + B = A), acquisition (parent–sub, both survive), consolidation (A + B = new C). IFRS draws no distinction. Both use the acquisition method.
  • Price = FV of consideration (incl. FV of contingent consideration). Shares issued at market value, not par.
  • Transaction costs (legal, advisory) → expensed. Post-deal restructuring costs → expensed when incurred, not part of cost.
  • 100% of target's identifiable assets & liabilities at FV, including unrecorded intangibles (brands, patents, licences) and IPR&D (amortised if completed, else impaired).
  • Consolidated BS = acquirer's book values + target's fair values. Only the acquirer's retained earnings carry over.
  • Post-deal: higher COGS (inventory step-up) and higher D&A on stepped-up PP&E and new intangibles.
  • Bargain purchase (price < FV of net assets) → immediate gain in P&L.

05Goodwill impairment

F6

One-step test (both GAAPs)

Loss=​Carrying amount of unit−Recoverable amount​
IFRS
unit = cash-generating unit; recoverable = higher of value in use and FV less costs to sell
US GAAP

Tested at least annually. Loss goes to the IS as a separate line.

  • IFRS: loss first wipes out the CGU's goodwill; any excess is allocated pro rata to other non-cash assets.
  • US GAAP: loss is capped at the goodwill balance; other assets are not adjusted automatically.
  • Goodwill write-downs are never reversed (both GAAPs).

  • Equity method flatters margins: investee profit enters NI, but its sales don't enter revenue.
  • Consolidated operating margin is a blend of parent and sub margins (it lands between them).
  • Current ratio, asset turnover: no general rule. Compute from the vignette's numbers.
  • Analyst concerns: is the method appropriate? (19% holder hiding losses, or 25% holder with no real influence). Equity income may not be cash: check dividend restrictions. Investee debt is off the investor's balance sheet.

07SPEs & VIEs

  • IFRS 10: consolidate when you have control: power over the entity + exposure/rights to variable returns. Covers most SPEs.
  • US GAAP VIE: equity at risk insufficient to finance activities without extra support, or equity holders lack decision rights, the obligation to absorb losses, or the right to returns.
  • Primary beneficiary consolidates, regardless of voting interest: the party absorbing the majority of expected losses and/or receiving the majority of residual returns. If split → the loss absorber consolidates.
  • Securitisation via a consolidated SPE looks identical to borrowing against the receivables: cash ↑, debt ↑, receivables stay on. Any "gain on sale" and intercompany revenue are eliminated.
  • Unconsolidated SPE (old abuse): better asset turnover, lower leverage, booked gains.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • "Net income differs between equity method and consolidation" → false. Only presentation differs.
  • Equity method: dividends are not income. They reduce the investment account.
  • Excess price allocated to land and goodwill is not amortised. Only depreciable assets (÷ remaining life).
  • Goodwill under the equity method = price − s × fair value of net assets, not book value.
  • Unrecorded intangibles of the target (e.g. licences) get recognised at FV and amortised, which adds to consolidated D&A.
  • Consolidation adds 100% of the sub's assets and sales even at 51% ownership. The minority share shows only in NCI.
  • Partial goodwill is IFRS only. Partial → lower equity → higher D/E than full goodwill.
  • IFRS: impairment of equity method investments can reverse; US GAAP cannot. Goodwill impairment never reverses.
  • US GAAP goodwill loss is capped at goodwill; IFRS spills the excess onto other assets.
  • Securitisation through a consolidated SPE doesn't raise revenue or remove receivables.
