Financial Statement Analysis

Integration of Financial Statement Analysis Techniques

In 30 seconds

  • Analysis serves an economic decision. Pick the tools that fit the purpose; don't run every ratio.
  • Six phases: purpose → collect → process → analyse → conclude & communicate → follow up.
  • Disaggregate: DuPont with associates stripped out shows how the core business really performs.
  • Judge capital allocation by segment: does capex go to the segments with the best returns on assets (not just margins)?
  • Earnings quality checks: accruals ratios (low, stable = good) and cash flow ÷ operating profit (> 1 = earnings backed by cash).

01The analysis framework

PhaseOutput
1 Purpose & contextQuestions to answer, depth, timeline
2 Collect dataStatements, notes, segment data, industry info
3 ProcessAdjusted statements, common-size, ratios
4 Analyse / interpretAnswers, drivers, risks
5 Conclude & communicateReport and recommendation
6 Follow upUpdate each period; re-test the thesis
  • Phases 3 and 4 are done together in practice (each finding prompts the next test).
  • Consistency first: when a new standard is adopted with restated comparatives only back to one year, the analyst applies the opening adjustments to earlier years so the time series is comparable (e.g. a change from proportionate consolidation to the equity method; revised pension accounting).
  • Adjust for differences in standards, methods and assumptions vs peers before comparing.

EBIT margin. Tax × interest × EBIT margin = net margin

Three-way: net margin × asset turnover × leverage. Find which driver moved. Rising margin and turnover with falling leverage → ROE could rise further just by re-levering, with no operating change.

F2

Core (ex-associates) components

Core margin=SalesNI−Assoc. income​Core TAT=Avg TA−Assoc. inv.Sales​​

Income from associates is after tax with no revenue → it inflates net margin; the investment inflates assets → depresses turnover. EBT and the tax burden also exclude associates. Leverage is left unadjusted (you can't tell which financing funds the associates). Core ROE = core margin × core turnover × leverage.

  • Associates are not under management's control → strip them to judge the core. A large gap between reported and core margins means reliance on associates (and one-off gains on selling them).
  • Unusual charges (provisions, impairments): re-running ROE without them shows the trend. Leave them in the main analysis if they recur, reflect management decisions and affect shareholder returns.
💡
Reported ROE rising while core ROE falls = growth coming from associates or one-offs, not operations. That is a red flag even if headline numbers look good.

03Asset base, capital structure, liquidity

  • Common-size assets: a rising share of goodwill & intangibles = growth by acquisition. Confirm in CFI (share of investing cash spent on acquisitions).
  • Leverage (TA/E) says nothing about the type of financing. Common-size the long-term capital structure: bond debt is more onerous than, say, pension obligations. Stable TA/E can hide a shift from equity to financial debt.

04Segments & capital allocation

  • Segments are reported the way management decides (may mix geography and product). Accounting changes not carried back can break comparability.
F4

Capex allocation ratio

Segment assets/Total assetsSegment capex/Total capex​

> 1 = growth allocation (segment expanding); < 1 = shrinking in importance; = 1 maintenance. Compare with segment profitability: capital should flow to the highest-return segments.

F5

Segment return proxies

EBIT ROA=Avg segment assetsSegment EBIT​Cash ROA≈Avg segment assetsEBIT+D&A​​

Segment cash flows aren't disclosed, so EBIT + D&A is the cash proxy. Averaging assets smooths investment spikes.

⚠️
Margin ≠ return. The highest-EBIT-margin segment can have the lowest return on assets (big acquired asset base). A low capex allocation to it may then be right. Heavy acquisitions in a low-ROA segment plus goodwill impairments → possible overpaying, poor capital allocation.
  • Divestiture candidate = lowest margin/return segment using a large or growing share of capital (capex share > capital share).

05Accruals & cash flow tests

F6

Accruals ratios

BS-based=(NOAt​+NOAt−1​)/2NOAt​−NOAt−1​​CF-based=(NOAt​+NOAt−1​)/2NIt​−(CFOt​+CFIt​)​​
NOA
net operating assets = (TA − cash & ST investments) − (TL − total debt)

Low absolute level and low volatility = good. High or rising = worse earnings quality. Mind the signs: CFI is usually negative, so CFO + CFI is often small.

F7

Cash backing of operating profit

Operating profit (EBIT)CFO before interest & taxes​

Consistently > 1 and steady = earnings backed by cash. Consistently < 1 = suspect profit. To put CFO on an EBIT basis, add back taxes paid and add back interest paid only if it was classified in CFO (IFRS lets you put it in CFF).

F8

Cash flow capacity ratios

Cash ROA=Avg total assetsCFO​Reinvestment=Capex+IntangiblesCFO​Debt cover=Total debtCFO​Int. cover=Interest paidCFO​​

CFO/TA = cash return on all past investments (a falling trend questions allocation). Reinvestment coverage > 1 = self-funding. High CFO/debt and coverage → debt capacity for future deals. Years to repay debt ≈ debt ÷ (CFO − reinvestment).

06Decomposing the valuation

F9

Stand-alone P/E

Core P/E=Parent NI−Stake NIMkt cap−Stake MV​

A listed associate is valued separately by the market. Strip its market value and its earnings contribution (after removing NCI from group earnings) to see what the market pays for the core business. A core P/E below the market or the stake = possible undervaluation, or market scepticism about core profitability.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Compute ROE as margin × turnover × leverage each year before calling a trend. Don't eyeball one component.
  • "Raise ROE without operational changes" → leverage (margins and turnover are operational).
  • Strip associates from margin and turnover, not leverage. Associate income has no revenue → it inflates margin.
  • Recurring impairments/provisions stay in the core analysis; only genuine one-offs come out.
  • Divest the segment with the lowest margin/return that absorbs a large or rising capex share, not the biggest segment.
  • CF accruals = [NI − (CFO + CFI)] ÷ avg NOA. With CFI negative: 4,038 − (9,822 − 10,068) = 4,284 → ÷ 43,192 = 9.9%.
  • Judge accruals over the whole period: 11.0% → 5.9% → 9.9% = a modest net decline = slight improvement.
  • CFO before interest & taxes: if interest is already in CFF, add back taxes only (9,822 + 1,930 = 11,752; ÷ EBIT 6,270 = 1.9).
  • Cash/EBIT ratios above 1 and rising steadily → earnings are backed by cash.
  • Highest EBIT margin ≠ best investment: check return on assets (EBIT or cash basis).
