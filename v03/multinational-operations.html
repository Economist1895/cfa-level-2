Multinational Operations

In 30 seconds

  • Transactions in a foreign currency: gains/losses on receivables & payables go to net income, realised or not.
  • Functional currency of the sub picks the method: local currency → current rate method (adjustment → equity, CTA). Parent's currency → temporal method (gain/loss → NI).
  • Exposure: current rate = net assets; temporal = net monetary assets/liabilities (usually a net liability).
  • Hyperinflation (≥ 100% over 3 years): IFRS restate for inflation, then current rate; US GAAP use temporal.
  • Current rate preserves pure BS and pure IS ratios; temporal distorts most ratios.

01Foreign currency transactions

  • Local currency = where the entity is located. Functional = currency of its primary economic environment. Presentation = currency the statements are shown in. Any non-functional currency is "foreign".
  • Record at the spot rate on the transaction date (sale/inventory and the FC receivable/payable).
  • Revalue the open receivable/payable at each balance sheet date, then at settlement. Each change → gain/loss in NI (even if unrealised).

  • Gains/losses may be immaterial: few FC deals, stable rates, natural offsets (FC receivable + payable), or hedging (forwards, options).

02Choosing the method

Sub's functional currencyMethod → where translation G/L goes
Local currency (autonomous sub)Current rate → OCI, accumulated in CTA (equity); recycled to NI on disposal
Parent's currency (extension of parent)Temporal ("remeasurement" in US GAAP) → net income
Hyperinflationary economyIFRS: restate, then current rate (purchasing-power G/L → NI). US GAAP: temporal (G/L → NI)

Functional currency indicators (IFRS)

  • Priority: currency that mainly drives sales prices, and the country whose competition & regulation set those prices.
  • Then: currency of labour/material costs, of financing, of retained operating receipts.
  • Parent's currency points: sub is an extension of the parent, large share of transactions with parent, its cash flows directly affect/are remittable to the parent, needs parent funds to service debt.
  • Mixed sub: first translate other-FC balances into its functional currency, then translate to presentation currency at the current rate.
  • The parent's inflation rate is irrelevant; avoiding income volatility is not a valid reason to pick a functional currency.

Current rate: RE from the roll-forward; the CTA is the balancing figure. Temporal: no CTA. In a vignette, RE is the figure that balances the translated BS, and NI (incl. remeasurement G/L) can be backed out from it.

  • Temporal + inventory: FIFO → ending inventory at recent rates, COGS at older rates. LIFO → the opposite. Weighted average → average rate.
  • Temporal needs a record of historical rates for each non-monetary asset; current rate doesn't.

FC strengthens: revenues, assets and liabilities all ↑ under both methods. NI and equity ↑ under current rate and temporal with net monetary assets, but ↓ under temporal with net monetary liabilities (translation loss in NI). Reverse everything if the FC weakens.

CTA is cumulative: a positive adjustment this period can still leave a negative CTA balance.

05Effects on ratios

  • Current rate preserves ratios built purely from the BS (current ratio, D/A, D/E) or purely from the IS (margins, interest coverage).
  • Mixed ratios (IS item ÷ BS item: turnovers, ROA, ROE) are distorted under both methods (average vs current rate).
  • Temporal distorts almost everything, in no predictable direction. Exception: items using the same rate type (e.g. inventory turnover can be preserved).
  • Receivables turnover (sales at average, AR at current) and the cash ratio are the same under both methods.
  • Sales ÷ fixed assets: same sales, but fixed assets at historical (temporal) vs current rates. If the FC has strengthened since purchase, temporal → lower fixed assets → higher turnover.
  • NI under temporal includes the remeasurement G/L; under current rate it doesn't, so NI can differ a lot even when operations are identical.
⚠️
Gross margin: temporal translates COGS at historical rates. If the FC has been strengthening, old rates are lower → lower COGS → temporal gives the higher margin (highest with FIFO). If the FC is weakening, the current rate method (COGS at average) gives the higher margin. Under the current rate method, the margin equals the local-currency margin.

06Highly inflationary economies

  • Definition: US GAAP: cumulative 3-year inflation > 100% (≈ 26% a year). IFRS (IAS 29): cumulative rate approaching or exceeding 100% over 3 years is an indicator. Functional currency is then irrelevant.
  • US GAAP: temporal method, no inflation restatement. Avoids the "disappearing plant" problem (current rate × collapsing currency shrinks historical-cost assets).
  • IFRS: restate for local inflation (IAS 29), then translate everything at the current rate.

  • If the FX rate moves exactly with the inflation differential, the IFRS and US GAAP approaches give the same result.

07Disclosures, tax & sales growth

  • Required: exchange differences in NI (transaction G/L + temporal remeasurement G/L, which need not be split), plus the CTA balance with a beginning-to-end reconciliation. US GAAP also: CTA recycled to NI on disposal.
  • Mixed methods: a multinational can use both at once (some subs current rate, some temporal). Peers may choose differently, so compare by adding the CTA change to NI (clean-surplus view).
  • Effective tax rate = tax expense ÷ pretax profit. Read the statutory → effective reconciliation: "effect of foreign tax rates" changes come from profit mix shifts between jurisdictions or foreign rate changes. Transfer pricing shifts profit to low-tax countries. Home-country rules (exemption, credit, deferral until repatriation) also matter.
  • Sales growth = organic (volume + price) ± acquisitions/divestitures ± FX effect. Organic growth is more sustainable and more within management's control than FX-driven growth.
  • Sensitivity disclosures (e.g. profit impact of a 10% FX move, cash-flow-at-risk) feed forecasts and downside cases.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • A loan taken by a sub is translated with the sub's method (set by its functional currency), whatever currency the loan is in.
  • Temporal: debt is monetary → current rate, never the issue-date rate. Inventory & fixed assets → historical.
  • Current rate: all assets & liabilities at current; only equity at historical.
  • Temporal translation G/L → NI; current rate adjustment → OCI/CTA. Switching to the current rate method takes these swings off the income statement.
  • Current rate exposure = net assets (e.g. 400 − 77 − 175 = 148). Temporal exposure = monetary items only.
  • FC weakens + current rate → negative translation adjustment. FC weakens + net monetary liability (temporal) → gain.
  • Hyperinflation: IFRS restate then current rate; US GAAP temporal. Restated revenue uses GPI year-end ÷ GPI average.
  • Receivables turnover and the cash ratio don't change with the method.
  • Transaction G/L placement shifts operating margin only.
  • FX-driven sales growth is the least sustainable component.
