The Arbitrage-Free Valuation Framework

In 30 seconds

  • A bond is a package of zeros. Arbitrage-free value = each cash flow discounted at its own spot rate.
  • For bonds whose cash flows depend on rates (embedded options), use a binomial interest rate tree calibrated to the benchmark curve.
  • Tree needs 3 things: an interest rate model (lognormal), a volatility assumption, and the current benchmark curve.
  • Value by backward induction, pathwise averaging, or Monte Carlo (for path-dependent cash flows like MBS).
  • Term structure models: equilibrium (CIR, Vasicek) vs arbitrage-free (Ho–Lee, KWF).

01Arbitrage-free valuation

  • Arbitrage = no cash outlay, riskless profit. Law of one price: identical cash flows → identical price.
  • Value additivity: whole = sum of parts. If a bond ≠ sum of its stripped zeros, buy the cheap side, sell the rich side (strip or reconstitute).
  • Dominance: a risk-free future payoff must have a positive price, and two risk-free assets must share the same discount rate. Sell the one with the lower implied rate (rich), buy the higher-rate one (cheap).
  • Same bond, several prices → buy at the lowest price (highest yield), sell at the highest price (lowest yield).
  • Two bonds with the same maturity but different coupons are different packages of zeros, so they don't share one yield.

02The binomial interest rate tree

F2

Lognormal node spacing

i1,H​=i1,L​e2σi2,HL​=i2,LL​e2σ,i2,HH​=i2,LL​e4σ​
σ
volatility of the 1-period rate (proportional, so absolute moves shrink as rates near zero → no negative rates)

Adjacent nodes in a year differ by e2σ. Node k steps above the lowest = lowest × e2kσ. Going down: multiply by e−2σ. Up and down each have probability 0.5.

  • Rates are 1-period forward rates, (nearly) centred on the implied forwards. The middle node ≈ the implied forward rate for that year.
  • Recombining tree: HL = LH, so fewer nodes and faster computation.
  • Estimate σ from historical rate volatility or implied volatility from derivatives (caps, floors, swaptions).

VH​,VL​

Start at maturity (par + final coupon at every node), work right to left. The coupon is received at the end of each period; there is no coupon at time 0.

03Calibrating the tree

  • Goal: rates that reprice each benchmark (par) bond at par. This makes the tree arbitrage free.
  • Year by year: pick a trial rate (below the implied forward for the lower node), apply e2σ, value the benchmark bond, then adjust (Solver) until price = 100. Earlier rates stay fixed.
  • The average of the calibrated nodes sits slightly above the implied forward (convexity of the lognormal).
  • Higher σ → rates spread out around the forward curve. σ → 0 → rates collapse to the implied forwards.
💡
A calibrated tree values an option-free bond exactly as the spot curve does, whatever σ is. Volatility matters only once cash flows depend on rates (options). That's why we calibrate: verify the tree on benchmarks, then use it for bonds with embedded options.
⚠️
Trap: calibration does not find mispriced benchmarks. Benchmarks are assumed correctly priced, and the tree is fitted to them.

04Pathwise valuation & Monte Carlo

F4

Pathwise valuation

V0​=n1​p=1∑n​PVp​

Discount the cash flows along each rate path by that path's 1-period rates, then average. An N-year tree has 2N−1 paths (count via Pascal's triangle). Gives the same answer as backward induction.

Monte Carlo

  • Randomly simulate many paths (e.g. 500 monthly paths). Used when cash flows are path dependent (MBS prepayments depend on the rate path, not just the current rate).
  • Steps: simulate short rates → spot rates → cash flows per path → PV per path → average.
  • Drift adjustment: add a constant to all rates so benchmark bonds reprice to market (arbitrage free).
  • Mean reversion: upper and lower bounds on simulated rates.
  • More paths → better statistical accuracy, but not closer to true fundamental value. Garbage in, garbage out.

05Term structure models

F5

General one-factor short-rate process

dr=θt​dt+σt​dZ

Drift θt​dt = expected path (constant, time-dependent or mean-reverting). Stochastic term σt​dZ: Z is a normal Wiener process, so negative rates are possible.

F6

CIR (equilibrium)

drt​=k(θ−rt​)dt+σrt​​dZ

Mean reverts to θ at speed k. Volatility rises with the rate level → rates can't go negative.

F7

Vasicek (equilibrium)

drt​=k(θ−rt​)dt+σdZ

Same mean-reverting drift; constant volatility → rates can go negative.

F8

Ho–Lee (arbitrage-free)

drt​=θt​dt+σdZ

Time-dependent drift (one θ per step, so it matches market prices). Constant vol, normal → negative rates possible. No mean reversion.

F9

Kalotay–Williams–Fabozzi (arbitrage-free)

dln(rt​)=θt​dt+σdZ

Like Ho–Lee (no mean reversion, constant vol) but models the log of the short rate → rate is lognormal, no negatives. Tails affect option values.

  • One-factor (short rate) models are plausible because parallel shifts explain >90% of yield changes. Multifactor models capture curvature better but are more complex.
  • Gauss+ (modern, multifactor): short, medium and long factors. Long rate mean-reverts (macro); medium reverts to long; short has no random part (central bank). Result: a hump-shaped volatility curve, with medium rates most volatile.

06Exam traps

⚠️
  • Arbitrage-free price uses spot rates. Wrong answers use the YTM or the final-year spot rate for every flow.
  • Bootstrapping: each year uses that year's par coupon, not the longest bond's coupon.
  • Building a tree needs three inputs (model, volatility, benchmark curve), not two.
  • Option-free bond: tree value = spot-curve value regardless of σ.
  • Lower node from upper: multiply by e−2σ (e.g. 5.8365% × e−0.50 = 3.54% at σ = 25%). Nodes two steps apart differ by e4σ.
  • Middle node of a year ≈ the implied forward for that year. You can't derive a year-3 node from a year-2 node.
  • Node value: discount coupon + average of next values at this node's rate. Don't subtract the coupon or add one at time 0.
  • More Monte Carlo paths ≠ closer to fundamental value.
  • Ho–Lee and KWF are arbitrage-free (time-dependent drift). CIR and Vasicek are equilibrium (mean reversion). Only CIR's volatility depends on the rate level.
  • Equilibrium = fewer parameters, uses economic fundamentals. Arbitrage-free = fitted to market prices.
