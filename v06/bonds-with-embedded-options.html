Valuation and Analysis of Bonds with Embedded Options

In 30 seconds

  • Bond with options = straight bond ± options. Investor is short an issuer option (call, cap) and long an investor option (put, floor, conversion).
  • Value on a binomial tree: at each node, check exercise and reset the value (call → min(value, call price); put → max(value, put price)), then backward-induct.
  • Higher volatility → every option is worth more: callable bond ↓, putable bond ↑.
  • OAS = constant spread over the tree's forward rates that reprices the bond. Higher assumed σ → lower OAS for a callable.
  • Use effective duration and convexity. Callable bonds can have negative convexity; putable bonds always positive.

01Types of embedded options

  • Call (issuer): redeem early when rates fall or credit improves. Styles: European (one date), American (any time after the first call date), Bermudan (set dates after call protection). Make-whole call: price at a narrow spread to a benchmark, so refunding is uneconomic.
  • Put (investor): sell back, usually at par, when rates rise. European or Bermudan, never American.
  • Extendible: holder may keep the bond past maturity. Equivalent to a putable bond (different underlying straight bond).
  • Estate put: heirs can put at par on the holder's death; value depends on life expectancy.
  • Sinking fund bond: issuer retires principal over time (less credit risk). Often has a call, an acceleration provision (e.g. "triple up" at par) and a delivery option (deliver bonds bought below par). Call + delivery ≈ issuer's long straddle: it gains if rates move either way.

04Risky bonds & option-adjusted spread

F4

Option-adjusted spread (OAS)

Pmkt​=V(tree with every i→i+OAS)

The constant spread added to all 1-period forward rates on the tree so the model value = market price. Found by trial and error. A higher spread gives a lower value. Z-spread = OAS at zero volatility; for an option-free bond, OAS = Z-spread.

  • Relative value: compare with bonds of similar characteristics and credit quality. Higher OAS → cheap (buy); lower OAS → rich.
  • Different ratings (e.g. B vs CCC)? The OAS gap reflects credit, so you can't conclude on relative value.
  • Callable: higher assumed σ → lower OAS (more option value, less spread left over). A bond that looks cheap at low σ may not at high σ.
  • Default risk can also be modelled explicitly (default probabilities + recovery), but the spread approach is the industry standard.

05Effective duration & convexity

F5

EffDur=2(ΔCurve)PV0​PV−​−PV+​​

Parallel shift of the benchmark curve, credit spread held constant. Procedure: (1) solve for OAS from PV0​; (2) shift the curve down, rebuild the tree, revalue at the same OAS → PV−​; (3) shift up → PV+​. Modified duration is invalid when cash flows change with rates.

  • Callable: duration shortens as rates fall (call into the money) and lengthens as rates rise. Putable: shortens as rates rise. Deep in the money → behaves like a straight bond maturing on the exercise date.
  • One-sided durations beat two-sided ones near the money. Callable: up-duration > down-duration. Putable: down-duration > up-duration. Option-free: about equal.
  • Key rate durations: shift one par rate at a time. Option-free bond: largest at the maturity point. A par bond is sensitive only to its own maturity par rate. A zero or low-coupon bond can have negative KRDs at shorter maturities.
  • With options, KRDs depend on time to exercise too. High-coupon callable → acts like a bond to the call date. Low-coupon putable → acts like a bond to the put date.

06Capped & floored floaters

F7

Capped and floored floaters

Vcapped​Vfloored​​=Vstraight​−Vcap​=Vstraight​+Vfloor​​

Cap = issuer option (protects against rising rates). Floor = investor option (protects against falling rates). Both are exercised automatically.

  • Floater at the reference rate with no spread: straight value = 100, because coupon = discount rate at every node.
  • Set in arrears: the coupon paid at t + 1 uses the rate at the node at t. Cap: coupon = min(rate, cap). Floor: coupon = max(rate, floor). Discount at the node's rate.
  • The floor can bind at time 0 if today's rate is below the floor.

07Convertible bonds

  • Lower coupon than straight debt in exchange for equity upside. Issuer: cheaper funding, no repayment if converted. Shareholders: dilution.
  • Conversion price = par ÷ conversion ratio. Adjusted for splits, bonus shares, rights and warrants. Dividends above a threshold dividend → conversion price adjusted down.
  • Change of control: holder gets a put at par, or a lower conversion price. Hard put = cash only; soft put = issuer chooses cash, stock or notes.
  • Forced conversion: issuer calls when the share price is above the conversion price, so holders convert.
  • CoCos: higher coupon, deeply subordinated; convert or are written down if regulatory capital is breached. CoCoCos are also convertible at the investor's option.

08Exam traps

⚠️
  • Call option value = straight − callable. Put option value = putable − straight. Don't flip the signs.
  • In a tree, reset before rolling back: a callable node above the call price becomes the call price.
  • σ ↑ raises both option values: callable price falls, putable price rises. An out-of-the-money convertible is roughly unaffected.
  • Inverted curve: call value ↑, put value ↓.
  • Same coupon and maturity: putable > straight > callable. A convertible is above the straight bond even when out of the money (time value).
  • The bond whose duration may lengthen as rates rise is the callable; the putable's shortens.
  • Putable convexity is always positive; callable convexity turns negative only near or in the money.
  • Effective duration needs the same OAS in the up and down revaluations. Use the full price.
  • Higher OAS means cheap only between bonds of the same credit quality. Higher σ → lower callable OAS.
  • Minimum convertible value = max (conversion value, straight value), not the sum. MCP uses the bond's price, not par.
