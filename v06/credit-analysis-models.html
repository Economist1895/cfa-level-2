Credit Analysis Models

In 30 seconds

  • Credit risk = expected exposure × loss given default × probability of default, period by period.
  • CVA = PV of expected losses (risk-neutral PODs, risk-free discounting). Fair value = VND − CVA. The same compensation can be stated as a credit spread.
  • Rating migration lowers expected return: downgrades are likelier and widen spreads more than upgrades tighten them.
  • Structural models: equity = call on assets, explain why default happens. Reduced-form models: observable inputs, model when.
  • Credit curves are usually upward sloping for IG, can be inverted for high yield, and look "optically" inverted for distressed bonds priced at recovery.

01Credit risk parameters & CVA

  • Credit spread (G-spread) = corporate YTM − same-maturity government YTM. It covers expected loss plus liquidity and tax differences.
  • Default risk = probability of default only. Credit risk = probability and size of loss. A well-collateralised loan can have high default risk but low credit risk.
  • Expected exposure: amount at risk before recovery. Zero coupon, no volatility: 100/(1+r)T−t. Coupon bond: coupon due + PV of remaining flows. With a tree: probability-weighted node values + coupon.

F4

Risk-neutral POD (one period)

P0​=1+rf​X(1−P∗)+X⋅RR⋅P∗​

Solve for P∗ given the market price. Risk-neutral POD > actual (historical) POD, because it includes the premium for uncertain timing of default, and the spread also carries liquidity and tax effects.

  • Return on a defaulted bond (IRR) is worst if default comes early. YTM = return only if there is no default.
  • For a given price/spread, the assumed POD and RR are positively related (lower RR → lower implied POD).
  • Holding everything else fixed, doubling POD moves the spread more than halving RR. A % change in POD beats the same % change in RR.
  • POD and RR changes do not change VND, exposures or discount factors, only the CVA.

02Credit scores, ratings & migration

  • Credit scores (retail, e.g. FICO 300–850): payment history 35%, debt burden 30%, length of history 15%, credit types 10%, recent hard inquiries 10%. Excluded: age, salary, occupation, race, address, soft inquiries (self-checks).
  • Credit ratings (wholesale): ordinal. Notching lowers subordinated issues by 1–2 notches to reflect LGD, which is why they are "credit" not "default" ratings. Outlook: positive, stable, negative; watch.

03Structural vs reduced-form models

F6

Option view of equity and debt

E(T)=max[A(T)−K, 0]D(T)=A(T)−max[A(T)−K, 0]​

Equity = long call on assets, strike K = face value of debt. Debtholders own the assets and have written the call. Equivalent put view: D(T)=K−max[K−A(T),0], i.e. risk-free debt minus the put that gives shareholders limited liability.

  • Structural POD (asset value below the default barrier at T) rises with asset volatility, time to T and leverage.

04Arbitrage-free valuation of risky bonds

  • Build the benchmark tree (calibrated to the government par curve and σ). VND = tree value = benchmark DFs × cash flows.
  • Expected exposure at date t = Σ (node probability × node value) + coupon at t. Node probabilities on date t are binomial: e.g. date 2 = ¼, ½, ¼.
  • Volatility: no effect on VND, but a small effect on CVA through exposures (lognormal rates are skewed up, so higher σ → slightly lower exposure → slightly lower CVA).
  • Floater paying the benchmark flat → every node = 100. With a quoted margin, value > 100. Coupon is set at the start of the period (node rate) and paid at the end.
  • Discount margin (DM) = the FRN's equivalent of a credit spread. Fair value below par → DM > quoted margin. Found by trial and error on the tree.
⚠️
Trap: "credit spread over a theoretical same-coupon government bond" means: value the corporate's cash flows on the benchmark curve (= VND), get that YTM, and subtract it from the corporate YTM. It is not the benchmark par yield.

05Interpreting spreads & the credit curve

  • Benchmark yield = macro: expected inflation + expected real rate (+ risk premium). Spread = micro: expected default loss, liquidity, tax.
  • XVA: start from OIS-discounted value, then adjust: CVA (credit), FVA (funding), LVA (liquidity), TVA (tax).

Drivers of the credit spread curve

  • Credit quality: top IG curves are flat or slightly upward (spreads can only widen). HY curves are steeper and more sensitive to the cycle.
  • Financial conditions: strong economy → benchmark yields ↑, spreads ↓ (countercyclical spreads). Weak economy → spreads wider and steeper.
  • Supply/demand: the most frequently traded (recent) issues dominate the shape.
  • Company-value factors: higher equity volatility → higher implied POD → steeper curve.
  • Use only comparable bonds (senior unsecured, no options, no liens). Swap curve is often used as the benchmark for off-the-run maturities.

  • Servicer risk: operational and counterparty; judge by its track record on seasoned deals.
  • Structure: SPE bankruptcy remoteness requires a true sale. Credit enhancements: overcollateralisation, tranching, excess spread, early-amortisation triggers.
  • Covered bonds: senior bank debt with dual recourse (issuer + cover pool). Pool is dynamic: prepaid or bad assets must be replaced. Rated above the issuer. Redemption: hard bullet (missed payment = default), soft bullet (default deferred up to ~1 yr), conditional pass-through.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Exposure for a one-period coupon bond = par + coupon (e.g. 105), before recovery.
  • Expected future value uses actual PODs. Pricing uses risk-neutral PODs discounted at the risk-free rate, not the bond's YTM.
  • PODt​ = h × survival to t − 1, not h each year. POS = (1−h)t.
  • Fair value = VND − CVA. Compare with the market price: price > fair value → overvalued.
  • Migration: % price change = −ModDur × Δspread. Use the current rating's row and add the result to the YTM.
  • Reduced-form = regression on observable ratios and macro data. Structural = option pricing, needs the default barrier.
  • Equity = long call on assets. Debt = assets − call = risk-free debt − put.
  • Higher σ → no change in VND for an option-free bond; CVA changes only slightly.
  • Credit curves are not always upward: high yield can invert.
  • Rating agencies move ratings in small steps. An analyst more optimistic than the agencies expects a positive watch, not a jump to AAA.
