Credit Default Swaps

In 30 seconds

  • A CDS is insurance on a reference entity's debt. The protection buyer pays a premium and receives par − recovery after a credit event.
  • Protection buyer = short credit (gains when spreads widen). Protection seller = long credit. For index CDS, "buying the index" = long credit.
  • Standard coupons are 1% (IG) or 5% (HY). The gap to the fair spread is settled by an upfront premium ≈ (spread − coupon) × duration.
  • Mark-to-market P&L ≈ Δspread × duration × notional. Monetise it by entering an offsetting CDS.
  • Uses: hedge or take credit exposure, long/short and curve trades, and basis trades (bond spread vs CDS spread).

01Definitions & types

  • Reference entity = the borrower. Reference obligation = usually a senior unsecured bond. Coverage extends to all debt of equal or higher seniority.
  • Payoff is based on the cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) obligation: the lowest-priced eligible bond with the same seniority, not the bond you hold. Subordinated debt doesn't qualify.
  • Like a put option on credit. It does not remove all risk: the buyer takes on counterparty risk, and the definition of default may not match the actual loss.
  • Single-name, index (CDX in North America; iTraxx in Europe, Asia, Australia) and tranche CDS (covers losses up to set levels, so only a partial hedge).
  • Other uses: leverage, access to maturities not available in cash bonds, credit exposure without interest rate risk, and more liquidity than corporate bonds.
  • Total CDS notional can exceed the debt outstanding (buyers needn't own the debt). Naked CDS on sovereign debt is banned in Europe.

Contract terms

  • ISDA Master Agreement must be signed first. Standard contracts: Standard North American, Standard Europe.
  • Maturities 1–10 yrs, 5 yrs most liquid. Terminate on the 20th of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec; Mar and Sep are index roll dates.
  • Premium paid quarterly at a standard coupon: 1% (IG) or 5% (HY).

02Credit events & settlement

  • Credit events: bankruptcy; failure to pay (after a grace period, no bankruptcy filing needed, on any obligation); restructuring (must be involuntary or coercive; not a credit event in the US). Sovereigns: moratorium or repudiation.
  • An ISDA Determinations Committee decides: 15 members (10 dealers + 5 buy-side), needs a 12-vote supermajority. It also rules on succession events (merger, spinoff), which can split the CDS across entities.
  • Settlement is a right, not an obligation; typically 30 days after the declaration.
F1

Payout on a credit event

LGD=1−RRPayout=LGD×Notional​

RR is set by an auction of the CTD bond, and both parties accept it even if actual recovery differs. Physical settlement (less common): deliver the bond, receive par.

💡
Settlement choice: if you hold the CTD bond, you're indifferent (cash payout + sell bond = par). If you hold a pricier bond than the CTD, prefer cash settlement: payout (par − CTD price) + sell your bond at its higher price > par.

03Index CDS

  • CDX IG and iTraxx Main: 125 names. CDX HY: 100. iTraxx Crossover: up to 75 HY names.
  • Equally weighted: one default settles 1/N of the notional; the name drops out and the index continues with a smaller notional.
  • IG indexes quoted as spreads; HY indexes quoted as prices. New series every 6 months (on-the-run); moving to it = a roll.
  • More liquid than single names. Value depends on credit correlation: higher default correlation → protection costs more.
  • Index price ≠ sum of components → arbitrage, but transaction costs are high.
⚠️
Trap: sell $500m of index protection on 125 names = $4m long each name. Buy $3m single-name protection on one name → net long $1m (75% hedged). After it defaults, the index notional is $496m.

04Pricing & valuation

Hazard rate = POD given no prior default. A constant 2% hazard over 10 yrs: 1−0.9810 = 18.3% cumulative POD. Small annual rates compound into large lifetime PODs.

F3

CDS spread (one period)

CDS spread≈(1−RR)×POD

e.g. POD 2%, RR 60% → 80 bps. Same logic as CVA: expected exposure = notional.

F4

Upfront payment (two legs)

Upfront=​PV(protection leg)−PV(premium leg)​

> 0 → buyer pays seller (spread > coupon). < 0 → seller pays buyer (spread < coupon). Also: PV(credit spread) = upfront + PV(fixed coupon).

F5

Upfront premium approximation

Upfront %≈(spread−coupon)×DurCDS price=100−upfront %​

Dur = effective duration of the CDS. Reverse it: spread = coupon + upfront ÷ Dur. E.g. seller pays 2% upfront on IG, Dur 4 → spread = 100 − 50 = 50 bps, price = 102.

F6

Value change during the CDS's life

%ΔCDS price≈Δspread×DurBuyer P&L≈Δspread×Dur×Notional​

Spread widens → buyer gains, seller loses. Spread narrows → seller gains. Duration shortens over time, and the spread → 0 at maturity if no default.

  • Monetise: enter an offsetting CDS (buyer sells protection on the same terms) and collect or pay the new upfront. It needn't be with the same counterparty; central clearing helps.
  • Three ways to realise P&L: settle after a credit event, unwind with an offsetting CDS, or hold to maturity (seller keeps all premiums).

Credit curve

  • Credit spread ≈ POD × LGD. The credit curve is essentially set by CDS rates.
  • Constant hazard → roughly flat curve (not perfectly flat, because of discounting). Upward → more default risk in later years. Downward (rare) → near-term stress.
  • Long spread widens, short unchanged → long-term creditworthiness worse. Short spread jumps → near-term (liquidity) risk; if the firm survives, POD then falls.

05Applications

  • Hedge: lenders buy protection instead of selling an illiquid loan or bond (temporary, low cost). Sell protection = credit exposure with no interest rate risk and less capital. Dealers hedge by shorting the debt or equity (+ repo).
  • Long/short credit: sell protection on the name or index you expect to improve, buy protection on the one you expect to weaken (e.g. strong vs weak ESG governance). P&L = change in the spread difference.
  • Weaker economy expected → buy HY index protection, sell IG index protection.

06Basis trading & valuation disparities

F7

Bond credit spread & CDS basis

Bond spread=ybond​−MRRBasis=CDS spread−bond spread​
MRR
risk-free rate + funding spread

Negative basis (bond spread > CDS): credit is cheap in CDS → buy the bond + buy protection (hedge rates), capture the gap on convergence. Positive basis: the reverse.

  • The basis comes from differences in opinions, models, liquidity and repo financing conditions. Embedded options or bonds far from par complicate the maths.
  • Within one issuer: buy its cheap instrument, sell the rich one (complicated by priority of claims).
  • LBO / debt-funded takeover: more debt → spread widens. Trade: buy the stock (target) + buy protection on the issuer of the new debt.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • Recovery rate = CTD price (lowest same-seniority bond), not the bond you hold.
  • Own a bond priced above the CTD → cash settle (e.g. $6m payout + $5m bond = $11m vs $10m physical).
  • Failure to pay on any obligation, even subordinated, is a credit event without a bankruptcy filing.
  • Upfront ≈ (spread − coupon) × duration: (700 − 500) × 7 = 14%, not 2% and not (700/500) × 7.
  • Sold protection at 150, closed at 100 bps, Dur 3.9, £6m → gain ≈ 0.005 × 3.9 × 6m = £117k. Close a sold position by buying protection.
  • Basis: bond spread = yield − MRR (7% − 2.5% = 4.5%). Gain = 4.5% − 4.25% = 0.25%, not CDS − MRR.
  • Index CDS = full hedge of many names; tranche CDS = partial. Buying an index = long credit.
  • Downward credit curve = higher POD in early years; flat = constant hazard rate.
  • Cumulative survival multiplies each year's (1 − h): 0.9978 × 0.9965 × 0.9950 → POD ≈ 1.07%.
  • Need an ISDA Master Agreement before trading CDS.
