The Term Structure and Interest Rate Dynamics

In 30 seconds

  • Spot, forward, par and swap curves carry the same time-value information; given one, you can derive the others.
  • A spot rate is the geometric average of the 1-period rate and the forwards. Upward-sloping spot curve → forwards lie above spots.
  • If spot rates evolve as today's forwards predict, every bond earns the 1-period rate. Active managers bet on deviations from the forward curve.
  • Riding the curve pays when the curve is upward sloping and stays put.
  • Curve moves = level (most), steepness, curvature. Manage shaping risk with key rate durations.

01Spot rates, forward rates & the forward rate model

F1

Discount factor ↔ spot rate

DFN​=(1+zN​)N1​zN​=(DFN​1​)1/N−1​

Spot rate = YTM of an option-free, default-free zero. No reinvestment assumption, so it's the purest measure of the N-period rate.

F2

Forward pricing model

DFB​=DFA​×FA,B−A​FA,B−A​=DFA​DFB​​​
FA,B−A​
price agreed today, paid at time A, for a zero paying 1 at time B

No-arbitrage: buying the B-year zero = buying the A-year zero + a forward on a (B − A)-year zero.

F3

Forward rate model

(1+zB​)B=(1+zA​)A(1+fA,B−A​)B−A
fA,B−A​
rate for a loan starting at A with tenor B − A (first subscript = start, second = length)

Two readings of f7,1​ = 3%: the breakeven reinvestment rate that makes you indifferent between an 8-yr zero and a 7-yr zero rolled for 1 yr; and the rate you can lock in by extending maturity by one year.

F4

Spot rate = geometric mean of forwards

(1+zT​)T=​(1+z1​)(1+f1,1​)×(1+f2,1​)⋯(1+fT−1,1​)​

Spot = average, forward = marginal. Rising average → marginal above it. So: upward spot curve → forward curve above the spot curve, and further above for later start dates. Downward → below. Flat → forwards = spot.

⚠️
Trap: you can only compute a forward whose end date is on today's curve. Need f2,3​? You need z5​ and z2​. With a 30-yr curve, a 3-yr-forward curve extends only 27 yrs.

Par curve & bootstrapping

  • Par curve = YTMs of coupon government bonds priced at par (usually on-the-run issues).
  • Bootstrapping: treat each par bond as a portfolio of zeros and solve for spot rates by forward substitution, shortest maturity first. With annual coupons, z1​ = 1-yr par rate.
  • Each step: 1=∑t=1N−1​(1+zt​)tcN​​+(1+zN​)N1+cN​​, solve for zN​.

Curve shapes

  • Upward (most common, flattens at long end): expected rising/stable inflation and strong growth, plus term premiums.
  • Inverted: expected falling inflation; often precedes recessions. Flat: brief transition. Humped: rare, intermediate rates highest.

02YTM, realised return & rolling down the curve

  • YTM = weighted average of the spot rates used to price the bond, weighted toward the largest cash flow (maturity). Upward curve: z1​<yT​<zT​, and yT​ sits close to zT​.
  • YTM = expected return only if held to maturity, no default, and coupons reinvested at the YTM. Poor estimate when rates are volatile, the curve is sloped, default risk is high, or there are embedded options. Realistic only for a flat curve.
  • Realised return uses actual reinvestment rates and the actual exit curve. Even if forwards are realised, expected return ≠ YTM on a sloped curve.
💡
If future spot rates = today's forwards: the forward contract price is unchanged, and every bond, whatever its maturity, earns the 1-period spot rate over the period. Any change in forward price comes only from spot rates deviating from today's forwards.

F5

Bond total return (rolldown)

Return=​coupons (+ principal)+reinvestment income±capital gain/loss on sale​

Riding the yield curve: upward-sloping curve expected to stay unchanged → buy a bond longer than the horizon. It is revalued at successively lower yields (higher prices) as it ages, beating the maturity-matched bond. Bigger spread and longer maturity → bigger effect.

  • Maturity spread carry trade: borrow short, lend long (same currency) in a steep curve. Main risk: an unexpected rise in rates.
  • Riding a corporate curve: exit yield = shorter-maturity benchmark yield + its (usually lower) spread.

03Swap curve & spreads

F6

Swap rate from spot rates

t=1∑T​(1+zt​)tsT​​+(1+zT​)T1​=1

Fixed leg (LHS) = floating leg (= 1 at origination). The swap curve is a par curve: same maths as bootstrapping in reverse. Swap rates, spot rates and DFs are interchangeable.

Why use the swap curve?

  • Very liquid: no multiple issuers, customisable, efficient hedge. More maturity points than many government curves (out to 50 yrs).
  • Preferred where the government bond market is illiquid/short or the private sector dwarfs the public sector. Not controlled by governments → more comparable across countries.
  • Wholesale banks (hedge with swaps) use the swap curve; retail banks use the government curve.
  • Swap vs Treasury rates differ because of credit risk, liquidity differences by maturity, and imperfect arbitrage.
F7

Spread measures

Swap spread=sT​−ygov,Ton-the-run​I-spread=ybond​−sT,interp​TED=MRR−T-billMRR–OIS=MRR−OIS rate​

Swap spread = credit (and liquidity) premium of short-term bank lending over default-free rates; widens in recessions (countercyclical). Price a bond off the swap curve: yield = gov't yield + swap spread (+ I-spread). Z-spread = constant spread added to every spot rate to reprice a risky bond.

  • TED and MRR–OIS = barometers of bank credit and liquidity risk. TED rises if T-bill yields fall with MRR unchanged.
  • OIS: floating leg = geometric average of an overnight rate (e.g. fed funds). SOFR = volume-weighted rate on overnight Treasury repo (secured, transaction-based), replacing survey-based Libor.
  • Swap spreads can be negative (post-2008: dealer capital rules, demand for duration).

04Term structure theories

TheoryKey claim → forwards
Unbiased (pure) expectationsForward = unbiased predictor of future spot. All maturities perfect substitutes; risk neutrality.
Local expectationsEvery bond's return over a short period = risk-free rate. No-arbitrage based; allows risk premiums for longer horizons, so works for risky bonds too.
Liquidity preferencePremium for long-term rate risk, rising with maturity → forwards are upward-biased. Curve usually upward; can still invert if big rate falls expected.
Segmented marketsEach maturity's yield set by its own supply/demand, independent of others (ALM constraints: insurers long, money funds short).
Preferred habitatPlayers prefer a maturity but will move if the extra return is big enough. Mix of expectations + institutional factors (e.g. QE lowering MBS yields).
⚠️
Trap: the liquidity premium here is a term premium on all long bonds (even deep markets), not a premium for thin trading. Forced selling of fallen angels pushing up their yields → segmented markets.

05Yield curve factors, volatility & key rate duration

  • Level (parallel shift): explains most of the variance (>75%). Steepness: short rates usually move more than long. Curvature (twist): short and long ends move opposite to the middle; smallest effect.
  • Shaping risk = sensitivity to changes in the curve's shape. Effective duration covers parallel shifts only.
F8

Yield volatility (annualised)

σ(t,T)=Δt​σ[Δr(t,T)/r(t,T)]​

SD of the proportional yield change (lognormal). Monthly data: Δt=1/12, so divide by 1/12​. Short rates are more volatile than long rates (but long bond prices move more, due to duration).

  • Short/intermediate yields: driven mainly by monetary policy (~⅔). Long yields: mainly inflation and the real economy.
F9

Key rate durations

PΔP​≈−i∑​KeyDuri​Δzi​

Sum of key rate durations = effective duration. A position's KRD = its weight × its duration at that key rate.

F10

Level / steepness / curvature

PΔP​≈−DL​ΔxL​−DS​ΔxS​−DC​ΔxC​

Each sensitivity = −(ΔP from a 1-unit factor move) ÷ (P × 0.01). With a steepness move defined as short −1, long +1: DS​=KeyDurlong​−KeyDurshort​. If factors are standardised, a k-SD move = k × the factor's loading on that yield.

06Macro drivers & positioning

  • Bond (term) risk premium: expected excess return of a default-free long bond over a short bond / 1-period rate. Forward-looking; must be estimated.
  • Fiscal: bigger deficits → more supply → higher yields; shrinking deficits → lower. Longer government debt maturity structure → higher excess returns (segmented supply).
  • Demand: pension/insurer and foreign inflows raise prices and lower the risk premium. Central bank QE lowers it; tapering raises long yields (steepens).
  • Stronger expected growth → long rates up → wider term spread.
MoveWhat happens / typical cause
Bear flatteningShort yields rise more than long. Central bank hikes in an expansion.
Bull steepeningShort yields fall more than long. Central bank cuts in a recession.
Bull flatteningLong yields fall more than short. Flight to quality.
  • Rates expected to fall → extend duration; rise → shorten.
  • Expect steepening → buy short, sell long. Expect flattening → buy long, sell short. Make it duration-neutral to isolate shape.
  • Long-only: bullet (one maturity) vs barbell (short + long, same duration). Expected bull flattening → move bullet to barbell.
  • Judge any view against the forward curve, not today's spot curve.

07Exam traps

⚠️
  • f1,2​ uses z3​ and z1​ and is raised to the power 2: f1,2​=(1+z3​)3/(1+z1​)​−1.
  • Bootstrapping uses forward substitution, shortest maturity first, not backward.
  • YTM of an upward-curve coupon bond is below the same-maturity spot rate but closest to it.
  • "Forward curve above spot curve" ⇔ spot curve upward sloping. Expecting the slope to reverse → future forward curve below spot.
  • Projected spot = today's forwards → bond fairly valued. Projected spot above forwards → overvalued.
  • Riding the curve needs an upward slope that stays unchanged; the steeper curve gives the bigger pickup.
  • Swap spread = swap rate − government yield. With negative government yields: 0.16% − (−0.10%) = 0.26%.
  • Short-rate volatility comes from monetary policy uncertainty; long-rate volatility from the real economy and inflation.
  • KRD % change: −∑KeyDuri​Δzi​. A 50 bp fall gives a positive change.
  • Hiking in an expansion → bear flattening. Flight to quality → bull flattening. Falling deficits → lower yields.
